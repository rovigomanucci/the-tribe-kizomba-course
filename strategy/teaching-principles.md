# Teaching Principles

Status: Approved

## 1. Moves attract attention

Students often ask for moves because moves provide visible progress. Use this motivation rather than fighting it.

## 2. Technique sits underneath the move

Every headline move must teach one foundation, such as weight transfer, axis, rotation, pathway, connection, or timing.

## 3. One movement family, three layers

### Core

The smallest safe and repeatable version.

### Progressing

Adds one entry, exit, direction, or timing change.

### Challenge

Adds a decision, musical constraint, or Fusion variation.

## 4. Teach systems, not collections

For each move, teach:

- Preparation
- Entry
- Core action
- At least two exits
- One recovery
- One social application

## 5. Keep the room together

Faster students work on quality, timing, size, direction, or musical interpretation. They do not receive unrelated choreography while others struggle with the foundation.

## 6. Use outcomes in corrections

Prefer outcome language such as:

- Create space for the follower's next step.
- Keep the follower's axis available.
- Finish the weight transfer before redirecting.

Avoid long lists of isolated body-part corrections.

## 7. Social dancing begins in class one

Every session contains partner rotation, navigation, recovery, and a guided social task.

## 8. Safety is part of technique

Consent, range, balance, preparation, and recovery are taught inside the movement rather than added as a warning at the end.
