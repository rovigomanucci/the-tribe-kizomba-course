# Level 2, Week 7: Dynamic Counterbalances

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 2 / Fusion improvers
- Previous class: Level 2 Week 6

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to connect one small counterbalance to a direction change, cross, or walk-out while preserving the shared recovery point and avoiding uncontrolled momentum.

## Class purpose

Week 7 adds movement after the static and linear foundations from Week 6. The objective is not to increase range or spectacle. Students learn to recover first, redirect second, and add leg action only when timing, axis, frame, and partner consent remain clear.

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

- Reproduce a safe small counterbalance and independent recovery.
- Redirect only after both partners begin returning toward axis.
- Exit a counterbalance into walking.
- Create a counterbalance-to-cross pathway with a clearly free leg.
- Reduce the movement when momentum or structure becomes unclear.
- Keep rotational and lift variations optional.

## Starting conditions

Students should demonstrate explicit preparation, small range, stable open frame, equal recovery timing, and the ability to return to individual axis without a correction step.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Counterbalance recovery recap
- Small direction change after recovery begins
- Counterbalance into cross
- Counterbalance exit into walking
- Entry from open line or completed pivot
- Return to neutral

### Previewed only

- Counterbalance into small leg lift
- Rotational counterbalance
- Counterbalance into Saída exit

### Excluded

- Surprise entry
- Deep or fast range
- Carrying partner weight
- Rotation before stable recovery
- Combining multiple advanced elements continuously

## Technical foundations

- Preparation and fresh partner agreement remain mandatory.
- Recovery begins before redirection.
- Momentum is generated and absorbed by each dancer's own structure.
- A cross or lift requires a clearly free leg.
- Range decreases when adding direction or leg action.
- Return to neutral is always the valid recovery.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Week 6 recovery check

### Purpose

Confirm the foundation before adding direction change.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Lead static and linear counterbalance with full recovery.
- Observe range, frame, and whether students walk too early.
- State the rule: recover first, redirect second.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate personal structure and active return to axis.
- Identify students who should remain with Week 6 core work.

### Student task

Prepare, enter one small counterbalance, recover to individual axes, and walk out.

### Success signal

Most couples recover without hanging, rebound, or correction steps.

### Transition

Separate and practise absorbing a direction change individually.

## 5–10 minutes: Individual recovery and redirection

### Purpose

Teach control of momentum before partner work.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a small inclined line, return toward vertical, then step into a new direction.
- Show why redirecting before recovery causes instability.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate soft knees, organised trunk, and controlled directional step.
- Observe collapsing, hopping, or overtravelling.

### Student task

Create a minimal inclination, recover nearly to vertical, then step into a new line and stop.

### Success signal

Students change direction without falling into the new step.

### Common errors and corrections

- Direction changes too early: “Recover before you redirect.”
- Step absorbs uncontrolled momentum: “Reduce the range and own the landing.”
- Upper body leads without feet: “Let the whole body reorganise.”

### Transition

Form partners and add a small direction change after static counterbalance.

## 10–16 minutes: Partner redirection preparation

### Purpose

Establish shared timing without completing a full dynamic action.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate preparation, static counterbalance, beginning recovery, and a tiny directional invitation.
- Stop before travel.
- Contrast redirection with pulling the partner sideways.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain recognising the recovery before receiving the new line.
- Demonstrate staying active rather than being carried.

### Student task

Enter, recover partway, acknowledge one new direction, return to neutral, and reset.

### Success signal

Couples agree on the new direction without arm force or sudden load.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once and recalibrate range.

### Transition

Complete the direction-change exit.

## 16–23 minutes: Counterbalance with direction change

### Purpose

Teach the core dynamic redirection with a clear recovery point.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate small counterbalance, shared recovery, new direction, and complete stop.
- Keep the change below a quarter turn for the core room.
- Observe whether leaders use stored tension to throw the partner.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate active recovery and controlled response to the new line.
- Observe late recovery and overtravel.

### Student task

Prepare, enter, hold briefly, recover, change direction once, stop, and return to neutral.

### Success signal

Direction changes remain compact and both partners control the endpoint.

### Common errors and corrections

- Release becomes projection: “Reduce the shared load before redirecting.”
- Follower is carried: “Recover through your own structure.”
- New direction is too large: “Choose a smaller angle.”

### Transition

Use the recovered free leg to create a cross.

## 23–30 minutes: Counterbalance into cross

### Purpose

Connect redirection to Week 1 crossing without combining before control.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate counterbalance, recovery, clear free-leg recognition, small cross, and pause.
- Show the unsafe cross created while the partner is still loaded.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate keeping both legs unavailable until recovery restores a stable standing side.
- Reinforce that the cross remains unweighted until clearly led.

### Student task

Recover from counterbalance, identify the free leg, guide one cross, replace or transfer clearly, and walk out.

### Success signal

The cross begins after recovery and does not absorb unresolved momentum.

### Common errors and corrections

- Cross begins under load: “Return toward axis before leg action.”
- Weighted leg is contacted: “Wait until the standing side is certain.”
- Follower transfers automatically: “Cross first; weight only when invited.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

Simplify the recovery into a walk-out.

## 30–37 minutes: Counterbalance exit into walk

### Purpose

Create the most socially useful dynamic recovery.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate prepare, enter, recover, establish a clear walking line, and use two or three compact steps.
- Emphasise scanning floor space before exit.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting for the direction after the shared load has reduced.
- Observe rebound and oversized first steps.

### Student task

Exit one small counterbalance into a short walk, stop safely, and return to Basic 3.

### Success signal

Couples walk out with normal step size and no sudden acceleration.

### Common errors and corrections

- First step is too large: “Use a normal social step after recovery.”
- Couple accelerates: “Do not convert tension into speed.”
- Floor space is ignored: “Choose the exit only when the line is clear.”

### Transition

Connect the entry to a completed pivot.

## 37–43 minutes: Entry from completed pivot

### Purpose

Integrate Week 4 rotation while maintaining sequential clarity.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate quarter pivot, complete recovery, explicit counterbalance preparation, small entry, and walk-out.
- Keep open-line entry available as core.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate finishing rotation before accepting shared load.
- Observe blended actions and missing preparation.

### Student task

Choose open-line or completed-pivot entry, complete one dynamic counterbalance, and recover to walking.

### Success signal

Each element has a visible completion point.

### Transition

Offer a small leg-lift exit only to stable couples.

## 43–49 minutes: Optional counterbalance into small leg lift

### Purpose

Test integration while preserving all Week 3 safety rules.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate full counterbalance recovery, free-leg preparation, tiny lift, replacement, and walk-out.
- Keep cross or walk exit as the core task.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate grounded axis and no jumping.
- Reinforce that the lift disappears if the free leg or recovery is unclear.

### Student task

Core students use cross or walk exit. Progressing students, after stable recovery, add one tiny lift and controlled replacement.

### Success signal

The lift remains small and begins only after the counterbalance has resolved.

### Safety cue

No lift under load, no hooking behind the knee, and no carrying body weight.

### Transition

Introduce rotational counterbalance only as a demonstration and optional challenge.

## 49–54 minutes: Optional rotational counterbalance

### Purpose

Clarify the conditions required for rotation without making it a class requirement.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a minimal rotational change with very small counterbalance range.
- Show the safe alternative: ordinary direction change.
- Require stable Week 6 recovery before participation.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining body line and completing rotation through the feet.
- Reinforce opting out or reducing range.

### Student task

Most students repeat the core direction change. Stable consenting couples may explore one minimal rotational variation and return to neutral.

### Success signal

No rotational action uses deep range, surprise load, or planted-knee twisting.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before final practice; return to static calibration first.

### Transition

State the independent task.

## 54–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Social-dance task

Use one dynamic counterbalance per song section. Prepare, enter, recover, and choose either a direction change, cross, or walk-out. Reduce the movement immediately when timing, axis, frame, consent, or floor space becomes unclear.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Prompt only: “Prepare,” “Recover first,” “Choose one exit,” “Small range,” and “Walk.”
- Stop any projected release, carried weight, or rotation without recovery.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe active structure, free-leg clarity, and partner consent.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Success signal

Most couples use one controlled dynamic action and return immediately to ordinary social flow.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 8 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap prepare, enter, recover, redirect, complete, walk out.
- Explain that Week 8 integrates the entire Level 2 vocabulary through musical and partner adaptation.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce that recovery is the centre of every dynamic variation.
- Ask which exit preserved the most control.

### Student task

Identify the rule for combining: add only one new element after the previous one is fully controlled.

### Success signal

Students understand that dynamic does not mean large, fast, or uncontrolled.

# Demonstration plan

1. Review small static counterbalance and full recovery.
2. Demonstrate individual recovery before redirection.
3. Add one compact direction change.
4. Show unsafe projection and carried weight.
5. Demonstrate cross only after free-leg clarity.
6. Show walk-out as the default recovery.
7. Present lift and rotation as optional variations.
8. Return to ordinary social flow with music.

# Partner-rotation plan

- Rotations: after redirection preparation, after cross practice, and before independent practice.
- Every new partnership returns to static preparation and small range.
- Lift and rotational variations require fresh consent and demonstrated recovery.
- Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Small counterbalance
- Full recovery
- One direction change
- Walk-out to Basic 3

## Progressing version

- Counterbalance into cross
- Entry from completed pivot
- Adapt exit and range to a new partner

## Challenge version

- Counterbalance into small leg lift
- Minimal rotational counterbalance
- Counterbalance into compact Saída exit

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Adding momentum without recovery | “Recover first; redirect second.” |
| Carrying partner weight | “Each dancer returns through their own structure.” |
| Release projects the partner | “Reduce shared load before choosing the line.” |
| Cross begins under load | “Restore a clear standing side first.” |
| Combining too many techniques | “Choose one exit and return to dancing.” |
| Rotation exceeds control | “Reduce both angle and counterbalance range.” |

# Safety and consent

- No surprise counterbalances or automatic advanced variations.
- Keep ranges small and self-recoverable.
- Rotational counterbalance requires stable recovery and fresh agreement.
- No lift under load or hook behind the knee.
- Avoid dynamic counterbalance with unresolved back, knee, balance, or trust concerns.
- Teachers explain corrections before physical contact.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Recovery begins before direction change.
- Dynamic range stays smaller than static range.
- Crosses and lifts use a clearly free leg.
- Couples walk out without rebound or acceleration.
- Students use one advanced exit rather than stacking techniques.

## After the class

Record:

- Percentage recovering before redirection
- Most common momentum-management error
- Any carried-weight or consent concern
- Cross versus walk-out success rate
- Whether rotational variation should remain demonstration-only
- Content requiring review in Week 8

- Is counterbalance into cross achievable for the weakest admitted student?
