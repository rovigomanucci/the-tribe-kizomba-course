# Level 2, Week 6: Counterbalance Foundations

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 2 / Fusion improvers
- Previous class: Level 2 Week 5

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to prepare, enter, hold, and recover from a small static or linear counterbalance while each partner maintains structure and remains able to return to individual axis.

## Class purpose

Week 6 builds on suspension and release by introducing a small shared counterbalance. The goal is not dramatic range. Students learn that counterbalance depends on explicit preparation, equal timing, stable structure, compatible distance, and a shared recovery—not on dropping body weight or hanging from the arms.

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

- Establish consent and a compatible starting distance.
- Maintain an organised body line without bending from the lower back.
- Enter a small static counterbalance together.
- Create a small linear counterbalance and recover.
- Return to individual axis before walking out.
- Reduce or cancel the action when structure, timing, or trust is unclear.

## Starting conditions

Students should demonstrate controlled suspension and release, stable open frame, independent recovery, and the ability to maintain posture without hanging or locking the arms.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Counterbalance readiness and consent
- Static two-person counterbalance
- Small linear counterbalance
- Shared hold and recovery
- Entry from open line or suspension
- Exit to individual axis and Basic 3

### Previewed only

- Side counterbalance
- Distance change while maintaining structure
- Counterbalance into walking

### Excluded

- Deep ranges
- Surprise counterbalances
- Dropping body weight
- Lower-back hinging
- Rotational counterbalance
- Use with unresolved back, knee, balance, or trust concerns

## Technical foundations

- Prepare and agree before entering.
- Both partners carry their own muscular structure.
- Keep the body line organised rather than bending at the waist.
- Use small compatible distance.
- Enter and recover on shared timing.
- Hands and arms communicate; they do not suspend body weight.
- Return fully to individual axis before continuing.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Week 5 recap and readiness check

### Purpose

Confirm suspension, release, and independent recovery before counterbalance.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Lead delayed transfer, compression, release, and walk-out.
- Observe hanging, locked arms, and late recovery.
- State that counterbalance is optional and requires preparation.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate vertical structure and return to personal axis.
- Identify students who should practise timing and connection without inclination.

### Student task

Create one small compression and release, then separate into individual balance.

### Success signal

Most couples can release without adjustment steps or dependency.

### Transition

Separate and train body structure individually.

## 5–11 minutes: Individual structure and recovery line

### Purpose

Teach the body organisation needed before sharing counterbalance.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a tall organised line with soft knees and active trunk.
- Show a tiny whole-body inclination from the ankles versus bending from the lower back.
- Demonstrate immediate return to vertical.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the same action from the side.
- Observe lower-back collapse, locked knees, and head displacement.

### Student task

Maintain an organised line, create a minimal ankle-led inclination, return to vertical, and confirm balance without using the arms.

### Success signal

Students can return to vertical independently and without stepping.

### Common errors and corrections

- Bending at the waist: “Keep the body line organised and reduce the range.”
- Knees lock: “Keep the legs active, not rigid.”
- Return requires a step: “Make the inclination smaller.”

### Transition

Form partners and calibrate distance and consent without leaning.

## 11–17 minutes: Partner preparation, consent, and distance

### Purpose

Create a safe shared setup before any counterbalance.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate explicit preparation and a verbal or clearly agreed opt-in.
- Show open-frame distance that allows both partners to remain vertical.
- Introduce the sequence: prepare, enter, hold, recover.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate accepting, reducing, or declining the action.
- Explain that a new partner means a new calibration.
- Observe arm locking and unequal distance.

### Student task

Agree on participation, establish distance, create gentle connection, and practise the entry signal without changing body angle.

### Success signal

Partners can communicate readiness and cancel the action without awkwardness.

### Common errors and corrections

- Entry begins without agreement: “Prepare and receive a clear response first.”
- Distance is too large: “Move closer before creating load.”
- Arms lock: “Keep the elbows responsive.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once and recalibrate from zero.

### Transition

Add a minimal static counterbalance.

## 17–24 minutes: Static counterbalance

### Purpose

Teach the core shape with almost no travel.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate shared preparation, tiny opposing inclination, one-pulse hold, and recovery.
- Keep the range small enough that either partner could return alone.
- Show the unsafe drop-and-catch version.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate active body structure and equal timing.
- Reinforce that the follower does not hang or soften into the arms.
- Observe asymmetrical range.

### Student task

Prepare, enter a minimal static counterbalance, hold for one pulse, recover together, and release connection.

### Success signal

Both partners maintain organised structure and recover without pulling or stepping.

### Common errors and corrections

- One partner drops: “Enter gradually and carry your own structure.”
- Unequal range: “Match the smaller partner's distance.”
- Arms carry weight: “Reduce range until the body—not the grip—creates stability.”

### Safety cue

Stop immediately if either partner cannot recover independently.

### Transition

Add a small linear pathway.

## 24–31 minutes: Linear counterbalance

### Purpose

Introduce controlled movement into and out of counterbalance.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a small linear step away, shared structure, and return.
- Keep the movement symmetrical and close to neutral.
- Mark the recovery point clearly.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate matching timing without increasing range.
- Observe delayed entry or one partner travelling farther.

### Student task

Prepare, take one small compatible step into linear counterbalance, hold briefly, return together, and regain individual axis.

### Success signal

Partners move and recover on shared timing without sudden load.

### Common errors and corrections

- One partner moves first: “Enter together after preparation.”
- Distance grows unexpectedly: “Use one small step and stop.”
- Recovery is late: “Return before the frame weakens.”

### Transition

Isolate the recovery process.

## 31–38 minutes: Recovery and release

### Purpose

Make the exit the most reliable part of the action.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate reducing counterbalance, returning to vertical, confirming individual axis, then releasing the frame.
- Show why walking out before recovery is unsafe.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining structure through the entire return.
- Observe partners who collapse once the hold ends.

### Student task

Enter a small static or linear counterbalance, recover fully to individual axes, pause, then walk out together.

### Success signal

Every couple visibly returns to neutral before taking the next step.

### Common errors and corrections

- Walking begins too soon: “Own your axis before moving.”
- Frame releases during recovery: “Maintain communication until both are vertical.”
- Partner is pulled inward: “Reduce the shared angle before stepping.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section and restart with readiness calibration.

### Transition

Connect the action to Week 5 suspension.

## 38–44 minutes: Entry from suspension

### Purpose

Use a familiar prepared hold as a controlled entry without creating surprise load.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate delayed transfer, return to a stable open line, explicit counterbalance preparation, small entry, and recovery.
- Keep direct open-line entry available as the core version.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate distinguishing suspension from counterbalance preparation.
- Observe actions that blend before the first recovery is complete.

### Student task

Choose direct open-line entry or completed suspension entry, create one small counterbalance, recover, and walk out.

### Success signal

Counterbalance begins only after the prior movement is fully resolved.

### Transition

Practise the Basic 3 exit and social recovery.

## 44–49 minutes: Exit to Basic 3

### Purpose

Return advanced content to recognisable social flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate full recovery, release into a clear direction, and Basic 3.
- Reinforce that the exit does not use stored tension to throw the partner.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting for the new direction after axis returns.
- Observe anticipatory steps and rebound.

### Student task

Recover from counterbalance, settle into neutral, and enter Basic 3 without rebound or pulling.

### Success signal

Couples transition quietly into Basic 3 with normal step size.

### Transition

Offer a side variation only to stable couples.

## 49–54 minutes: Optional side counterbalance

### Purpose

Preview a new direction while preserving the same structure and recovery rules.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a very small side-oriented counterbalance.
- Keep feet under control and range minimal.
- Retain static or linear versions as the core task.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate side-body organisation without collapsing the waist.
- Reinforce the right to decline or remain with the core version.

### Student task

Core students repeat static and linear versions. Progressing students, after agreement, explore one small side counterbalance and recover.

### Success signal

Side range remains self-recoverable and does not twist knees or spine.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before final practice; obtain fresh agreement.

### Transition

Set the final social task.

## 54–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Social-dance task

Prepare, enter, hold briefly, and recover together. Use no more than one small counterbalance per phrase. No surprise entries. Both partners return to their own axes before Basic 3 or walking.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Prompt only: “Prepare,” “Small range,” “Hold,” “Recover,” and “Own your axis.”
- Stop any dropping, lower-back bending, or locked-arm loading immediately.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe consent, structure, equal timing, and recovery.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Success signal

Most couples use one controlled counterbalance and recover without dependent weight or abrupt rebound.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 7 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap agree, prepare, enter, hold, recover, release.
- Explain that Week 7 adds direction change and leg action only after this recovery is reliable.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce structural responsibility and the right to decline.
- Ask which entry produced the clearest shared timing.

### Student task

Identify the safety test: both partners can return to vertical independently.

### Success signal

Students understand that range must decrease before structure or trust is compromised.

# Demonstration plan

1. Show static counterbalance preparation without entering.
2. Demonstrate individual body line and ankle-led inclination.
3. Add shared entry and one-pulse hold.
4. Show unsafe dropping, lower-back bending, and locked-arm hanging.
5. Demonstrate full return to individual axes.
6. Add a small linear step.
7. Show side variation only as optional.
8. Exit to Basic 3 with music.

# Partner-rotation plan

- Rotations: after preparation drill, after recovery practice, and before independent practice.
- Fresh consent and distance calibration are required with every partner.
- Students with back, knee, balance, or trust concerns use vertical timing drills only.
- Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Readiness and consent
- Static counterbalance
- Shared recovery
- Exit to Basic 3

## Progressing version

- Linear counterbalance
- Entry from completed suspension
- Adapt distance to a new partner

## Challenge version

- Small side counterbalance
- Counterbalance into walking
- Minor distance change while maintaining structure

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Dropping body weight | “Enter gradually and carry your own structure.” |
| Bending from lower back | “Keep the body line organised and reduce range.” |
| Locked arms | “Use responsive elbows, not a rigid grip.” |
| Unequal distance | “Match the smaller shared range.” |
| Late recovery | “Return before the frame weakens.” |
| Walking before axis returns | “Own your balance first.” |

# Safety and consent

- No surprise counterbalances.
- Each new partner requires fresh agreement and distance calibration.
- Both partners carry their own structure and must be able to recover independently.
- Avoid deep ranges and lower-back bending.
- Do not use counterbalance with unresolved back, knee, balance, or trust concerns.
- Teachers explain corrections before physical contact.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Students prepare and agree before entering.
- Counterbalance range remains small.
- Neither partner hangs or drops weight.
- Recovery returns both partners to individual axes.
- Basic 3 exits remain quiet and controlled.

## After the class

Record:

- Percentage recovering without a step
- Most common structural error
- Any consent, back, knee, or balance concern
- Static versus linear success rate
- Whether side counterbalance should remain challenge-only
- Content requiring review in Week 7

- Can the weakest admitted student return independently every time?
