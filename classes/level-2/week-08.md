# Level 2, Week 8: Fusion Flow Challenge

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 2 / Fusion improvers
- Previous class: Level 2 Week 7

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to build one short adaptable Fusion pathway, change its timing and texture, simplify it for a new partner, and recover safely whenever an element loses clarity.

## Class purpose

Week 8 consolidates the Level 2 vocabulary through musical interpretation, partner adaptation, and decision-making. Students are not assessed on completing a fixed spectacle sequence. The block, slide, lift, pivot, cross, counterbalance, and musical exit form a vocabulary menu. Students select only the elements they can prepare, control, and recover with the current partner.

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

- Select a safe subset of Level 2 elements.
- Build a short pathway with clear preparation and recovery points.
- Perform the same movement family with smooth, sharp, and suspended textures.
- Adapt range, timing, and complexity to a new partner.
- Remove an element immediately when control or consent becomes unclear.
- Complete a song section using choices rather than memorised execution.

## Starting conditions

Students should have experience with all previous Level 2 weeks. No student is required to perform every advanced element. The starting version is determined by the safest reliable vocabulary each partnership shares.

## Movement scope

### Vocabulary menu

- Prepared Saída entry
- Foot block
- Leg slide
- Small leg lift
- Pivot
- Cross
- Static or dynamic counterbalance
- Walking or Basic 3 recovery
- Musical hold or exit

### Fully taught

- Flow selection and reduction
- Clear preparation and recovery points
- Smooth interpretation
- Sharp stop-driven interpretation
- Slow suspended interpretation
- Partner adaptation after rotation
- Full song-section decision task

### Excluded

- Mandatory use of every element
- Fixed choreography as assessment
- Surprise lifts or counterbalances
- Deep ranges
- Completing the sequence after control is lost
- Prioritising spectacle over partner response

## Technical foundations

- Preparation precedes every advanced element.
- Recovery separates elements and prevents uncontrolled stacking.
- The current partner determines range and complexity.
- Musical interpretation changes quality and timing, not safety rules.
- Removing an element is a successful decision, not a failure.
- Ordinary walking and Basic 3 remain valid exits at all times.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Welcome, readiness, and safety framing

### Purpose

Set the class as an integration challenge rather than a performance test.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Explain the student-facing promise.
- State that no partnership must use every advanced element.
- Review the hierarchy: partner comfort, preparation, control, recovery, music, complexity.
- Lead a short Basic 3 and Saída warm-up.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce active follower responsibility, consent, and adaptation.
- Identify students who should use a reduced core pathway.

### Student task

Dance Basic 3, one Saída, one stop, and one recovery while checking balance and connection.

### Success signal

Students understand that simplification and recovery are central assessment behaviours.

### Transition

Separate and explore three movement textures without partner complexity.

## 5–11 minutes: Three textures individually

### Purpose

Differentiate musical interpretation from movement vocabulary.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the same simple walk with smooth continuous quality, sharp stop-driven quality, and slow suspended quality.
- Keep the step pattern unchanged.
- Explain that texture changes timing, energy, and continuity.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from a second angle.
- Observe students who increase range or add styling instead of changing texture.

### Student task

Use one walking pattern in all three textures while preserving balance and pulse.

### Success signal

Students visibly change movement quality without changing the basic pathway or losing control.

### Common errors and corrections

- Sharp becomes aggressive: “Create clarity, not force.”
- Suspended becomes frozen: “Keep breath and pulse alive.”
- Smooth loses structure: “Continuous does not mean collapsed.”

### Transition

Form partners and choose a reduced vocabulary set.

## 11–17 minutes: Partnership vocabulary selection

### Purpose

Build an honest shared starting point before creating a flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask each couple to select three reliable elements: one entry, one featured action, and one recovery.
- Demonstrate a simple example: Saída, block, walk-out.
- Require an explicit agreement before lifts or counterbalances.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Encourage followers to identify which elements feel clear and which should be removed.
- Observe partnerships selecting too many actions.

### Student task

Choose three shared elements and practise each separately with its preparation and recovery.

### Success signal

Each couple can perform the selected elements individually before connecting them.

### Partner rotation

No rotation yet; establish the first shared version.

### Transition

Connect the three elements with visible recovery points.

## 17–24 minutes: Build the core Fusion pathway

### Purpose

Create a short adaptable flow without erasing the boundaries between techniques.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate entry, featured action, recovery, second action, and final walk-out.
- Mark each completion point.
- Encourage fewer elements and more clarity.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without memorising the order.
- Reinforce completing each transfer before the next preparation.

### Student task

Build a pathway of three to five elements using at least one ordinary recovery between advanced actions.

### Success signal

The follower can follow the pathway without being told the order, and each action has a clear endpoint.

### Common errors and corrections

- Elements blend together: “Complete and recover before the next preparation.”
- Flow depends on memorisation: “Lead one decision at a time.”
- Pathway is too long: “Remove one element and improve the transitions.”

### Transition

Test the block-slide-cross family as one optional cluster.

## 24–30 minutes: Blocks, slides, and crosses integration

### Purpose

Review free-leg logic while preventing repeated leg manipulation.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate stop, free-leg check, one block or slide, optional cross, release, and walk-out.
- Require only one leg-contact action per phrase.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate stable standing-side control and active free-leg response.
- Observe joint contact, automatic transfer, and loss of axis.

### Student task

Choose one block, slide, or cross pathway and connect it to the core flow. Remove any element that becomes unclear.

### Success signal

Leg contact remains low, light, isolated, and followed by recovery.

### Transition

Test lift-pivot integration as a separate optional cluster.

## 30–36 minutes: Lifts and pivots integration

### Purpose

Review axis control and sequence order without requiring both elements.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a small lift-and-replace followed later by a quarter pivot.
- Show that lift and pivot do not need to be combined directly.
- Offer pivot-only as the core version.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce grounded lift response, controlled replacement, and foot release in rotation.
- Observe jumping, hooking, and planted-knee twisting.

### Student task

Choose a small lift, a pivot, or both with a full recovery between them. Keep the safer single-element version when needed.

### Success signal

Axis remains stable and no lift or pivot is forced to complete the planned flow.

### Transition

Add one suspension or counterbalance only after explicit preparation.

## 36–42 minutes: Suspension and counterbalance integration

### Purpose

Integrate shared-load vocabulary without allowing it to dominate the flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a musical hold, return to axis, optional small counterbalance, full recovery, and walk-out.
- Limit the flow to one shared-load action.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce explicit agreement, personal structure, and independent recovery.
- Observe hanging, surprise entry, and excessive range.

### Student task

Choose either a suspension or a small counterbalance, use it once, return fully to individual axes, and continue with Basic 3.

### Success signal

Shared-load content is brief, agreed, self-recoverable, and followed by ordinary dancing.

### Safety cue

No surprise counterbalance, deep inclination, or dynamic exit without demonstrated Week 6–7 recovery.

### Transition

Reduce and refine the full pathway.

## 42–47 minutes: Flow reduction and control audit

### Purpose

Teach students to remove complexity before testing musical textures.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask couples to identify their weakest transition.
- Require them to remove or simplify one element.
- Demonstrate how a shorter flow can produce clearer musicality.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Ask followers which preparation is least clear.
- Reinforce that partner feedback changes the final flow.

### Student task

Remove one weak element, rehearse the reduced pathway, and confirm that every recovery works without teacher counting.

### Success signal

The revised flow is shorter, clearer, and easier to follow.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once. The new partnership starts from three reliable elements rather than reproducing the previous flow unchanged.

### Transition

Apply the smooth texture with the new partner.

## 47–51 minutes: Smooth interpretation

### Purpose

Perform the selected movement family with continuous legato quality.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Use a smooth track section.
- Prompt “continuous,” “small,” “breathe,” and “recover.”
- Prevent smoothness from becoming weak structure.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe standing axis and continuous response.
- Reinforce reducing range with the new partner.

### Student task

Dance the reduced flow with smooth transitions and no abrupt accents.

### Success signal

The flow remains controlled while the energy becomes continuous.

### Transition

Keep the vocabulary and change only the texture.

## 51–54 minutes: Sharp stop-driven interpretation

### Purpose

Create contrast using clear stops without increasing force.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate precise stops, compact accents, and quiet recovery.
- Reinforce that sharp does not mean sudden partner manipulation.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate active stops and complete weight clarity.
- Observe bracing and impact.

### Student task

Use the same reduced flow with clearer stops and accents while maintaining low contact and personal balance.

### Success signal

Timing becomes more defined without increased force or range.

### Transition

Apply the suspended texture.

## 54–57 minutes: Slow suspended interpretation

### Purpose

Use delay, breath, and space without turning the flow into continuous suspension.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate one or two delayed moments, full release, and ordinary walking between them.
- Prevent students from holding every transition.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce breathing, active structure, and independent recovery.

### Student task

Use the same flow with selected delays and one optional suspension, then return to normal pulse.

### Success signal

Students place suspension selectively and preserve the underlying rhythm.

### Transition

Set the final independent song-section challenge.

## 57–59 minutes: Independent Fusion challenge

### Social-dance task

Dance one song section using at least three shared movement families, one clear musical texture, and one visible recovery. Remove any element that loses control with the current partner. No fixed sequence is required.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Observe preparation, musical choice, partner adaptation, and recovery.
- Avoid prompting specific moves.
- Intervene only for safety or repeated loss of control.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe followability, consent, axis, and whether feedback changes the flow.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Success signal

Couples make independent choices, simplify when necessary, and complete the section without spectacle-driven force.

## 59–60 minutes: Cycle recap and next steps

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap the Level 2 hierarchy: prepare, recognise, guide, control, recover, interpret, adapt.
- Reinforce safe, adaptable social use and identify useful next steps for continued practice.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce partner response and personal responsibility.
- Invite one brief reflection on the most reliable Level 2 skill.

### Student task

Identify one technique they can use reliably and one that still requires reduced range or further practice.

### Success signal

Students can describe their own safe working vocabulary and recovery strategy.

# Demonstration plan

1. Demonstrate one short three-element flow with music.
2. Mark every preparation and recovery without music.
3. Show the same flow in smooth, sharp, and suspended textures.
4. Demonstrate the unsafe version that forces all elements regardless of partner or music.
5. Remove one element and show the clearer reduced version.
6. Change partner and rebuild from a smaller vocabulary.
7. Return to ordinary social flow after every advanced section.

# Partner-rotation plan

- Main rotation occurs after flow reduction, before texture work.
- The new partnership does not inherit the previous sequence automatically.
- Begin with three mutually reliable elements and obtain fresh agreement for lifts, suspension, or counterbalance.
- Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Prepared Saída or open-line entry
- One block, slide, cross, or pivot
- Basic 3 or walking recovery
- One musical texture

## Progressing version

- Three to five connected elements
- One lift or pivot
- One suspension or static counterbalance
- Adapted version with a new partner

## Challenge version

- Dynamic counterbalance with controlled exit
- Three distinct musical interpretations of the same movement family
- Deliberate removal and replacement of elements according to music and partner response

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Performing sequence regardless of music | “Let the music choose quality and timing.” |
| Prioritising spectacle over response | “Reduce range until the partner remains clear.” |
| Skipping preparation | “No advanced element without a visible setup.” |
| Stacking techniques without recovery | “Complete, recover, then decide.” |
| Repeating the same flow with a new partner | “Rebuild from the vocabulary you both share.” |
| Continuing after control is lost | “Remove the element and return to walking.” |

# Safety and consent

- Every advanced element remains optional.
- Fresh partner agreement is required for lifts, inclinations, and counterbalances.
- No joint pressure, surprise load, deep range, or carried body weight.
- Removing an unsafe or unclear element is the expected response.
- Partner comfort and recovery override completing any planned flow.
- Teachers explain corrections before physical contact.

# Music plan

| Section | Music quality | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Texture exploration | Three contrasting excerpts with clear pulse | Low to medium |
| Flow building | Neutral, predictable Fusion track | Medium |
| Smooth interpretation | Sustained legato texture | Medium |
| Sharp interpretation | Clear stops and accents | Medium |
| Suspended interpretation | Spacious phrasing and held notes | Low to medium |
| Final challenge | Varied track with readable sections | Medium |

# Success measures

## During the class

- Students select rather than force vocabulary.
- Every advanced element has preparation and recovery.
- The same movement family changes texture without losing safety.
- Complexity decreases appropriately after partner rotation.
- Couples remove elements when control or consent becomes unclear.

## After the class

Record:

- Percentage completing an independent song section
- Which Level 2 element remained least reliable
- Most common preparation or recovery failure
- Whether students adapted after rotation
- Which musical texture produced the strongest control
- Any safety or consent concern requiring curriculum revision
- Students ready for continued Fusion development versus targeted review

# Open questions and test points

- Is the original vocabulary menu too broad for one 60-minute integration class?
- Do students genuinely reduce complexity after changing partner?
- Which three-element core pathway best reveals course readiness?
- Can students change texture without changing safety or range?
- Does the final task assess social adaptability better than a fixed demonstration?
- Which Level 2 elements should become optional specialist modules rather than core expectations?
