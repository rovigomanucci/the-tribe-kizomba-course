# Level 2, Week 5: Suspension and Release

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 2 / Fusion improvers
- Previous class: Level 2 Week 4

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to create a brief suspended feeling through delayed transfer, compression, and release while both partners remain able to return to their own axis independently.

## Class purpose

Week 5 introduces suspension as timing and controlled connection, not as dropping, hanging, or dipping. The lesson builds from stable stops and completed pivots into delayed forward and backward transfers, then uses a small compression-and-release action to recover into walking.

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

- Delay a transfer without losing the pulse or personal balance.
- Prepare forward and backward suspension clearly.
- Use responsive compression without collapsing the frame.
- Release back to individual axis before continuing.
- Match a brief hold to a musical pause.
- Decline or reduce inclination when balance, trust, or structure is unclear.

## Starting conditions

Students should demonstrate controlled stopping, stable open connection, completed pivot recovery, and the ability to maintain posture without hanging on the partner.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Individual delayed transfer
- Delayed forward transfer
- Delayed backward transfer
- Compression and release
- Return to individual axis
- Walk-out recovery
- Musical hold and release

### Previewed only

- Small supported inclination
- Suspension into pivot
- Extended musical hold

### Excluded

- Deep dips
- Sudden leaning
- Hanging body weight
- Backbends
- Surprise suspensions
- Breath-holding or rigid bracing

## Technical foundations

- Prepare before delaying or inclining.
- Both partners retain muscular structure and personal responsibility.
- Compression is responsive, not forceful.
- A hold pauses transfer; it does not remove the pulse.
- Release returns both partners to their own axis.
- Inclination stays small enough for either partner to recover independently.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Week 4 recovery recap

### Purpose

Confirm that students can stop after rotation and recover to neutral before adding suspension.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Lead quarter pivot, complete stop, and walk-out recovery.
- Observe whether students carry momentum or use the partner for balance.
- Introduce the class promise and no-dip boundary.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completed rotational recovery and independent axis.
- Identify students who should practise delayed transfer without inclination.

### Student task

Pivot, stop, settle on individual axes, and walk out together.

### Success signal

Most couples recover without leaning, correction steps, or arm tension.

### Transition

Separate and explore delayed transfer individually.

## 5–11 minutes: Individual delayed transfer

### Purpose

Teach suspension timing without partner support.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate stepping toward a transfer, delaying briefly, then completing it.
- Keep the standing leg active and posture organised.
- Show the difference between delay and freezing rigidly.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate breath, soft knees, and controlled release.
- Observe locked joints, collapsed torso, and loss of pulse.

### Student task

Approach a transfer, hold for one pulse while balanced, then complete the step and continue walking.

### Success signal

Students delay and release without hopping or falling onto the receiving foot.

### Common errors and corrections

- Body freezes: “Keep the pulse alive even when transfer waits.”
- Standing leg collapses: “Support yourself before releasing.”
- Breath is held: “Breathe through the delay.”

### Transition

Form partners and establish responsive compression without travel.

## 11–17 minutes: Connection and compression drill

### Purpose

Create elastic connection without pushing or hanging.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate open connection with small compression through the frame.
- Show immediate release to neutral.
- Contrast responsive tone with rigid pushing.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain receiving and matching pressure without surrendering axis.
- Demonstrate the opt-out response: reduce tone and return to neutral.
- Observe leaning and locked elbows.

### Student task

Create a small shared compression, hold briefly, release, and repeat without stepping.

### Success signal

Both partners remain upright and can release without an adjustment step.

### Common errors and corrections

- Pushing through arms: “Use whole-body organisation, not hand force.”
- Partner hangs: “Keep your own axis while matching connection.”
- Frame locks: “Responsive, not rigid.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once.

### Transition

Add a delayed forward transfer.

## 17–24 minutes: Delayed forward transfer

### Purpose

Apply suspension timing to a clear directional movement.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate preparation, small forward intention, delayed transfer, and completion.
- Keep the action within both partners' balance range.
- Show release into walking.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate receiving direction while delaying weight transfer.
- Reinforce keeping the standing foot active until release.
- Observe followers who step early or lean backward.

### Student task

Prepare, approach the forward transfer, delay for one pulse, complete together, and walk out.

### Success signal

Couples share the delay without pulling, bracing, or arriving on different timings.

### Common errors and corrections

- Follower transfers early: “Keep the standing side until the release arrives.”
- Leader increases force during delay: “Hold the timing, not the partner.”
- Couple loses pulse: “Hear the beat underneath the suspension.”

### Transition

Repeat with the opposite directional relationship.

## 24–31 minutes: Delayed backward transfer

### Purpose

Develop control when the receiving direction feels less secure.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a small backward invitation with clear preparation.
- Keep the pathway short and avoid pulling.
- Show recovery to neutral before walking.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining posture and compact step range.
- Observe reaching, leaning, or collapsing through the back.

### Student task

Prepare a backward transfer, delay briefly, complete it within a small range, return to axis, and walk out.

### Success signal

Both partners complete the action without reaching beyond their base or hanging on the connection.

### Common errors and corrections

- Step reaches too far: “Keep the receiving foot close enough to own your balance.”
- Upper body falls away: “Delay within your axis, not outside it.”
- Leader pulls: “Prepare through direction and wait for response.”

### Transition

Combine delayed transfer with compression and release.

## 31–38 minutes: Compression and release in motion

### Purpose

Create a complete suspension unit with an explicit recovery point.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate approach, compression, one-pulse hold, release, and completion.
- Mark the moment both partners return to individual axis.
- Observe whether release becomes a shove.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate matching compression while maintaining personal structure.
- Reinforce controlled return rather than being projected away.

### Student task

Enter from open line, create one small compression, hold, release to axis, and continue walking.

### Success signal

The release is quiet and both partners recover without extra steps.

### Common errors and corrections

- Release becomes sudden: “Reduce pressure before continuing.”
- Partners remain dependent: “Return to your own axis before the next step.”
- Frame collapses: “Keep structure without locking.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

Apply the sequence after a completed pivot.

## 38–44 minutes: Entry from completed pivot and walk-out

### Purpose

Connect Week 4 recovery to the new timing without creating a long combination.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate quarter pivot, complete recovery, delayed transfer, release, and walk-out.
- Keep open-line entry available as the core version.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate finishing the pivot before the suspension preparation begins.
- Observe students combining actions before balance is restored.

### Student task

Choose open-line or completed-pivot entry, create one suspension, recover to axis, and walk out.

### Success signal

Suspension begins only after the preceding movement is complete.

### Transition

Connect the hold to musical phrasing.

## 44–49 minutes: Musical hold and release

### Purpose

Use suspension as interpretation rather than a repeated trick.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Play a track with clear pauses or sustained moments.
- Demonstrate dancing through ordinary beats and suspending only on a clear musical opportunity.
- Limit the hold to one or two pulses.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate listening through the hold rather than predicting release.
- Observe breath-holding and rigidity.

### Student task

Dance continuously and use one prepared suspension on a clear musical hold, then recover immediately.

### Success signal

Students choose fewer suspensions and place them intentionally.

### Transition

Offer a small inclination only to stable, consenting couples.

## 49–54 minutes: Optional small inclination

### Purpose

Clarify the safe limit of supported shape without teaching a dip.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate explicit preparation and a very small shared inclination.
- Keep both feet grounded and both bodies capable of independent recovery.
- Show the unsafe deep or surprise version.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain consent, range control, and the right to stay vertical.
- Demonstrate returning to axis before any exit.

### Student task

Core students repeat compression and release vertically. Progressing students, after agreement, explore a minimal inclination and return to axis.

### Success signal

No partner becomes dependent on the other or moves beyond a self-recoverable range.

### Safety cue

No unresolved back, knee, neck, balance, or trust concern should enter inclination work.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before final practice; inclination does not carry automatically to the new partner.

### Transition

State the final task and require fresh consent after rotation.

## 54–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Social-dance task

Use one prepared suspension on a clear musical hold. Both partners must return to their own axis before continuing. Inclination remains optional and requires explicit agreement with the current partner.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Prompt only: “Prepare,” “Delay,” “Breathe,” “Release,” and “Recover.”
- Stop any surprise lean, hanging, or deep range immediately.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe personal structure, consent, and recovery.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Success signal

Most couples suspend briefly, release quietly, and return to social flow.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 6 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap prepare, delay, compress, release, own axis, walk out.
- Explain that Week 6 extends shared timing into small counterbalance foundations.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce the right to remain vertical and the need to breathe.
- Ask which recovery cue felt most reliable.

### Student task

Identify the non-negotiable recovery point: both partners back on their own axes.

### Success signal

Students understand that suspension is controlled timing, not dropping or dipping.

# Demonstration plan

1. Show delayed transfer with music.
2. Demonstrate individual balance without connection.
3. Add responsive compression.
4. Show unsafe hanging, pushing, and surprise inclination.
5. Demonstrate clear release to separate axes.
6. Add musical timing.
7. Present inclination only as a small optional variation.
8. Walk out with music.

# Partner-rotation plan

- Rotations: after connection drill, after compression in motion, and before independent practice.
- Every new partner begins with vertical delayed transfer.
- Inclination requires fresh agreement and never transfers by assumption.
- Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Individual delayed transfer
- Delayed forward transfer
- Compression and release
- Return to axis and walk out

## Progressing version

- Delayed backward transfer
- Entry from completed pivot
- Musical hold and release

## Challenge version

- Small supported inclination with explicit consent
- Suspension into a completed pivot
- Two-pulse hold with controlled release

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Leaning before preparation | “Prepare the timing before changing the shape.” |
| Hanging body weight | “Keep enough structure to recover alone.” |
| Frame collapses | “Responsive tone, not softness without support.” |
| Release becomes a shove | “Reduce pressure before continuing.” |
| Breath is held | “Breathe through the suspension.” |
| Couple continues before axis returns | “Own your balance before the next step.” |

# Safety and consent

- No deep dips, backbends, or surprise inclinations.
- Both partners remain able to recover independently.
- Inclination is optional and requires explicit agreement.
- Avoid inclination with unresolved back, knee, neck, balance, or trust concerns.
- No hanging from arms or collapsing the frame.
- Teachers explain corrections before physical contact.

# Music plan

| Section | Music quality | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Delayed transfer | Slow pulse with clear space | Low |
| Compression and release | Sustained texture | Low to medium |
| Musical holds | Clear pauses or held notes | Medium |
| Independent practice | Fusion track with visible dynamic contrast | Medium |

# Success measures

## During the class

- Students delay transfer while retaining balance.
- Compression remains responsive and small.
- Release returns both partners to their own axis.
- Musical holds are selective rather than continuous.
- No surprise inclination or dependent body weight appears.

## After the class

Record:

- Forward versus backward suspension success
- Most common frame or axis error
- Any consent or inclination concern
- Percentage recovering independently
- Whether inclination should remain challenge-only
- Content requiring review in Week 6

# Open questions and test points

- Is backward suspension appropriate as core material?
- Do students understand pulse continuing beneath the hold?
- Is explicit verbal consent needed before every inclination practice?
- Can the weakest admitted student recover independently?
- Which musical texture encourages suspension without rigidity?
