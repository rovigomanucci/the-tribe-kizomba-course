# Level 2, Week 2: Leg Slides and Tracing

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 2 / Fusion improvers
- Previous class: Level 2 Week 1

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to guide a small inside or outside leg slide near the floor while both partners preserve alignment, personal balance, and the option to stop.

## Class purpose

Week 2 extends free-leg recognition into continuous low contact. The lesson distinguishes a slide from a kick, drag, or forced stretch. Range is determined by the follower's natural pathway, not by the size of the leader's idea.

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

- Prepare a clearly free leg before contact.
- Maintain parallel alignment and a stable standing side.
- Guide inside and outside slides close to the floor.
- Slide, replace, and recover before transferring weight.
- Adapt direction and range to a new partner.
- Stop immediately when resistance or misalignment appears.

## Starting conditions

Students should demonstrate the Week 1 sequence: stop, identify the free leg, create low contact, release, and recover. Students unable to maintain weight clarity practise the no-contact pathway.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Alignment and range check
- Inside leg slide
- Outside leg slide
- Slide and replace
- Entry from prepared cross or open parallel position
- Exit to neutral or Basic 3

### Previewed only

- Slide into cross
- Circular tracing
- Slide with direction change

### Excluded

- Joint pressure
- High tracing
- Dragging a weighted foot
- Kicks or flicks
- Forced turnout or extended range

## Technical foundations

- Prepare the free leg before contact.
- Keep contact light and near the floor.
- Follow the natural leg line.
- The follower maintains the standing side and active free-leg pathway.
- Replace the foot before weight transfer unless a clear cross is prepared.
- Smaller range is the default with a new partner.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Week 1 recap and readiness check

### Purpose

Re-establish free-leg recognition and low-contact standards.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Lead Basic 3, prepared stop, and one low block.
- Restate that resistance means stop, not increased pressure.
- Observe whether students contact a clearly free leg.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate stable standing-side control.
- Identify students who should use no-contact tracing.

### Student task

Prepare, identify the free leg, create one quiet block, release, and recover.

### Success signal

Most couples complete the Week 1 action without impact, leaning, or joint contact.

### Transition

Separate and explore sliding pathways individually.

## 5–11 minutes: Individual slide pathway and alignment

### Purpose

Teach the follower-side pathway before partner guidance.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a free foot tracing close to the floor.
- Show inside and outside lines within a comfortable range.
- Contrast active tracing with dragging a weighted foot.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate standing-leg alignment, soft knee, and stable pelvis.
- Show how range decreases when alignment changes.
- Observe collapsed standing sides and locked knees.

### Student task

Stand on one leg, trace the free foot inside and outside, replace it, and change sides without losing balance.

### Success signal

Students move the free foot smoothly while the standing knee remains aligned with the toes.

### Common errors and corrections

- Foot drags heavily: “Make the foot light before tracing.”
- Standing hip collapses: “Keep the standing side organised.”
- Range exceeds control: “Stop before alignment changes.”

### Transition

Form partners and practise the pathway without leg-to-leg contact.

## 11–16 minutes: No-contact partner tracing

### Purpose

Create shared directional understanding before adding contact.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate preparing the free leg from an open parallel position.
- Guide the intended direction through the body and frame only.
- Keep the range small.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate responding with the free leg while maintaining personal axis.
- Explain that the follower completes the pathway rather than being pushed through it.

### Student task

Leaders prepare and indicate an inside or outside pathway without touching the leg. Followers trace and replace.

### Success signal

Couples agree on direction without verbal planning or leg contact.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once.

### Transition

Add light inside contact.

## 16–23 minutes: Inside leg slide

### Purpose

Teach the first controlled contact pathway near the floor.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate stop, free-leg check, light inside contact, slide, and release.
- Show contact below the knee and away from the joint.
- Demonstrate how to stop when the follower's range ends.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate active free-leg participation.
- Reinforce keeping the standing side stable.
- Show the difference between following contact and surrendering body weight.

### Student task

Prepare one inside slide, keep it small, release before weight transfer, replace the foot, and recover to Basic 3.

### Success signal

The slide remains quiet and low, and the follower can stop it at any point without losing balance.

### Common errors and corrections

- Pressure increases with distance: “Range ends when contact stops being light.”
- Contact approaches the knee: “Move lower and reduce the pathway.”
- Follower becomes passive: “Maintain your own leg and standing side.”

### Safety cue

Any resistance, pain, or joint pressure ends the action immediately.

### Transition

Repeat the same preparation for the outside pathway.

## 23–29 minutes: Outside leg slide

### Purpose

Develop the second slide direction while preserving alignment.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the outside pathway with reduced range.
- Show how to reposition the partnership rather than reaching.
- Observe leaders who turn the action into a sweep or kick.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining toe and knee alignment.
- Observe forced turnout or standing-side collapse.

### Student task

Prepare one outside slide, release, replace, and return to neutral.

### Success signal

The outside slide stays within the follower's natural line and does not rotate the knee.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader reaches with the leg: “Move the partnership closer; do not stretch for contact.”
- Follower forces turnout: “Keep the foot in your natural pathway.”
- Slide becomes a kick: “Maintain contact and reduce speed.”

### Transition

Practise replacing the foot cleanly after either slide.

## 29–35 minutes: Slide and replace

### Purpose

Make recovery the default outcome before adding crosses.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Alternate inside and outside slides.
- Clearly release and guide replacement.
- Return to Basic 3 after every action.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate replacing the free foot without transferring early.
- Observe followers who leave the leg extended or fall onto it.

### Student task

Choose one slide, replace the foot under the body, confirm balance, and walk out.

### Success signal

Couples recover without hopping, dragging, or an unplanned weight transfer.

### Common errors and corrections

- Foot remains extended: “Return the free foot before continuing.”
- Weight arrives too early: “Replace first; transfer only after the lead is clear.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

Connect the slide to a prepared cross for progressing students.

## 35–42 minutes: Slide into cross

### Purpose

Use the slide as a controlled pathway into the Week 1 cross.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a short inside slide into a small cross.
- Pause before any weight transfer.
- Offer slide-and-replace as the core alternative.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate preserving the standing axis during the cross.
- Reinforce that the crossed position does not require immediate weight.

### Student task

Core students slide and replace. Progressing students slide into a cross, pause, and recover.

### Success signal

The cross follows the existing free-leg path without increased force.

### Common errors and corrections

- Force increases to complete the cross: “Stop where the natural pathway ends.”
- Follower transfers automatically: “Crossing is a position before it becomes a step.”

### Transition

Practise entries from a prepared cross and open parallel position.

## 42–48 minutes: Entry and exit choices

### Purpose

Make slides available from more than one social position.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate entry from open parallel position and a prepared cross.
- Show exits to neutral and Basic 3.
- Require a complete reset between actions.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate remaining available without presenting the leg.
- Observe anticipation and hidden weight changes.

### Student task

Choose one entry, one slide direction, and one recovery. Repeat with reduced range after changing partner.

### Success signal

Couples retain control when the setup changes.

### Transition

Introduce range adaptation and partner feedback.

## 48–53 minutes: Range and alignment adaptation

### Purpose

Teach students to calibrate rather than impose a standard slide size.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask leaders to begin with the smallest possible action.
- Increase range only while contact remains light and alignment remains clear.
- Reinforce cancelling the action when the partner's structure changes.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain nonverbal resistance and verbal opt-out.
- Encourage followers to maintain their own comfortable endpoint.

### Student task

Repeat the same slide with two different partners, starting smaller each time and stopping at the first change in alignment.

### Success signal

Movement size changes appropriately between partners.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before final practice.

### Transition

Set the social-dance constraint.

## 53–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Social-dance task

Use no more than one slide per phrase. Prepare a free leg, choose inside or outside according to alignment, replace or cross, and recover immediately into social flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Prompt only: “Prepare,” “Stay low,” “Keep it light,” “Replace,” and “Recover.”
- Stop any joint pressure or force immediately.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe standing-side stability and partner adaptation.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Success signal

Most slides are small, optional, and followed by a clean recovery.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 3 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap prepare, align, slide, release, replace, recover.
- Explain that Week 3 uses the same free-leg preparation for a small lift.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce that active follower balance remains essential.
- Ask which direction felt more natural and why.

### Student task

Identify their comfortable range and the signal that tells them to stop.

### Success signal

Students understand that alignment and partner range determine the movement size.

# Demonstration plan

1. Show the complete slide with music.
2. Identify the standing and free legs.
3. Demonstrate the no-contact pathway.
4. Add light low contact.
5. Show unsafe joint pressure, dragging, and kicking.
6. Show immediate release and recovery.
7. Repeat with reduced range and music.

# Partner-rotation plan

- Rotations: after no-contact tracing, after slide and replace, and before independent practice.
- Start smaller with every new partner.
- Students may practise no-contact pathways instead of leg contact.
- Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Free-leg preparation
- Inside slide
- Outside slide
- Slide and replace
- Return to Basic 3

## Progressing version

- Entry from prepared cross
- Slide into cross
- Adapt slide size and direction to a new partner

## Challenge version

- Circular tracing
- Slide with small direction change
- Alternative exit after a completed cross

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Pressing into a joint | “Move contact lower and remove pressure.” |
| Dragging the foot | “Make the leg free before moving it.” |
| Turning the slide into a kick | “Stay connected and reduce speed.” |
| Follower collapses standing side | “Keep your axis over the standing leg.” |
| Range exceeds alignment | “Stop at the first structural change.” |
| Weight transfers before replacement | “Replace first; transfer second.” |

# Safety and consent

- Contact remains optional, low, and light.
- Avoid all pressure on knees and ankles.
- Stop immediately when resistance or misalignment appears.
- No forced turnout, stretch, or extended range.
- The follower maintains control of the leg pathway and may end it at any time.
- Teachers explain corrections before physical contact.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Students prepare a genuinely free leg.
- Contact stays away from joints.
- Followers preserve standing-side alignment.
- Slide range adapts between partners.
- Couples replace or cross without collapsing balance.

## After the class

Record:

- Which slide direction was safer and clearer
- Most common alignment error
- Any consent or joint-pressure concern
- Percentage adapting range with a new partner
- Whether slide into cross should remain progressing content
- Content requiring review in Week 3

# Open questions and test points

- Does the no-contact phase sufficiently reduce force?
- Which contact point gives clarity without approaching a joint?
- Is outside slide too demanding for the weakest admitted student?

- Which musical texture encourages smooth rather than sharp contact?
