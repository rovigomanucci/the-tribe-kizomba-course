# Level 2, Week 1: Cross and Redirect

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 2 / Fusion improvers
- Previous experience: Level 1 experience or equivalent readiness assessment

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to create a low, controlled foot block and a simple leg cross only after identifying the partner's free leg, then recover into walking without kicking, forcing, or carrying the partner's weight.

## Class purpose

Week 1 establishes the safety and communication standard for all later Level 2 leg actions. The class prioritises weight clarity, free-leg recognition, low contact, individual balance, and immediate recovery over visual complexity.

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

- Identify their own weighted and free leg.
- Recognise the follower's free leg through movement and connection.
- Prepare a stop before creating leg contact.
- Create follower and leader foot blocks with low, light contact.
- Guide a simple cross and return.
- Exit into walking or Basic 3.
- Abandon the action safely when weight or balance is unclear.

## Starting conditions

Students should already demonstrate reliable Basic 3, controlled stopping, compact Saídas, complete weight transfer, personal balance, and responsive partner connection.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Free-leg recognition
- Prepared stop
- Follower foot block
- Leader foot block
- Simple cross and return
- Return to walking or Basic 3

### Previewed only

- Cross with direction change
- Contratempo exit
- Pivot preparation

### Excluded

- Force against the knee or ankle
- High leg contact
- Kicking actions
- Continuous block combinations
- Hooks and lifts

## Technical foundations

- Contact only the clearly free leg.
- Establish balance before contact.
- Keep contact low, soft, and brief.
- Guide the pathway rather than striking or trapping the foot.
- Both partners retain their own body weight.
- Replace the free foot before transferring weight.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Welcome and Level 1 assessment recap

### Purpose

Confirm that the room is ready for controlled leg-contact work.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- State the class promise and safety boundary.
- Lead Basic 3, stop, restart, and Follower Saída recap.
- Observe weight clarity and stopping control.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate complete weight transfer and stable stops.
- Identify students who need the core version only.
- Reinforce that advanced elements remain optional.

### Student task

Dance Basic 3, stop on request, identify the weighted foot, and restart without an adjustment step.

### Success signal

Most students stop with one clearly weighted leg and one relaxed free leg.

### Transition

Continue individually and isolate free-leg recognition.

## 5–11 minutes: Own free-leg recognition

### Purpose

Make weight status observable before partner contact is introduced.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Guide step, stop, and free-foot checks.
- Ask students to lift or trace the free foot without shifting the standing side.
- Demonstrate unclear split weight versus a stable axis.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Show the drill from another angle.
- Observe collapsed standing hips, locked knees, and hidden weight changes.

### Student task

Step, stop, identify the free leg, move it slightly, replace it, and restart.

### Success signal

Students can move the free foot without hopping or transferring weight accidentally.

### Common errors and corrections

- Split weight: “Choose one standing leg before moving the other.”
- Free foot carries weight: “Make the moving foot light enough to leave the floor.”
- Standing side collapses: “Grow through the standing leg without locking it.”

### Transition

Form partners and recognise weight without leg contact.

## 11–17 minutes: Partner weight-reading drill

### Purpose

Teach recognition through connection rather than visual guessing.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a prepared stop from Basic 3.
- Show how the leader waits until the follower completes the transfer.
- Test the pathway through a very small directional invitation, without touching the leg.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain the follower experience of completing and settling weight.
- Demonstrate why anticipation hides the free leg.
- Observe followers who brace or lean.

### Student task

Leaders stop, wait, and identify the likely free leg. Followers complete the transfer and remain balanced without presenting or lifting the leg deliberately.

### Success signal

Couples identify the free side consistently without verbal coordination.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader guesses early: “Wait until the transfer is complete.”
- Follower presents the leg: “Remain neutral and available.”
- Connection becomes rigid: “Use the frame to listen, not to hold.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once.

### Transition

Add low contact for the follower foot block.

## 17–24 minutes: Follower foot block

### Purpose

Create the first controlled block against a clearly free foot.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3, prepared stop, weight check, and low block.
- Show contact near the foot rather than the knee.
- Demonstrate immediate release when alignment changes.
- Contrast guiding with kicking.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining personal axis.
- Explain that the blocked foot remains free and relaxed.
- Show the safe response when contact is unexpected: stop and reset.

### Student task

Enter from Basic 3, stop, establish the free leg, create one light block, release, replace the foot, and return to walking.

### Success signal

Blocks remain low and quiet, with no visible impact or loss of balance.

### Common errors and corrections

- Contact on the weighted leg: “No action until the free leg is certain.”
- Kicking: “Place contact; do not strike.”
- Follower leans: “Keep your weight over your standing foot.”

### Safety cue

Stop immediately when the partner resists, stiffens, or loses alignment.

### Transition

Reverse the responsibility and teach the leader foot block.

## 24–30 minutes: Leader foot block

### Purpose

Develop shared responsibility and prevent the lesson becoming follower-only manipulation.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate presenting a genuinely free leader foot.
- Keep the body balanced and the contact pathway small.
- Show how to replace the foot before continuing.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the corresponding low block or guided contact.
- Reinforce waiting for the leader's weight to settle.
- Observe excessive force or reaching.

### Student task

Create one leader free-foot opportunity, one low block, release, replace, and return to Basic 3.

### Success signal

The leader remains independently balanced and the contact does not interrupt the pulse.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader gives body weight: “Offer the free foot, not your balance.”
- Partner reaches with the leg: “Reduce the distance before creating contact.”
- Foot remains trapped: “Release before the next transfer.”

### Transition

Use the same preparation to create a simple cross.

## 30–37 minutes: Simple cross and return

### Purpose

Convert free-leg guidance into a clear cross without force.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the prepared stop and free-leg pathway.
- Guide a small cross and pause before weight transfer.
- Show the return to neutral and walking.
- Emphasise pathway first, transfer second.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate allowing the free leg to travel while keeping the standing side stable.
- Explain that the follower does not transfer until invited.
- Observe accidental crossing of the weighted leg.

### Student task

Prepare, guide one free leg across, pause, replace or transfer only when clear, and return to Basic 3.

### Success signal

The cross is created without pulling the torso, forcing the leg, or losing the standing axis.

### Common errors and corrections

- Cross created before the stop: “Balance first, pathway second.”
- Follower transfers automatically: “The crossed position does not require immediate weight.”
- Leader pulls the upper body: “Guide the leg pathway through the whole-body direction.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

Connect entries and exits with the new partner.

## 37–43 minutes: Entry and recovery choices

### Purpose

Make the action socially usable rather than a fixed exercise.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate entry from Basic 3 and Follower Saída.
- Show two recoveries: replace and walk, or return to Basic 3.
- Reinforce abandoning the action when weight is unclear.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without knowing whether a block or cross will occur.
- Observe anticipation and delayed recovery.

### Student task

Use one prepared entry, choose one block or cross, then recover through walking or Basic 3.

### Success signal

Couples can enter and recover without verbal planning or repeated leg contact.

### Transition

Add a controlled direction-change option for progressing students.

## 43–49 minutes: Cross with direction change

### Purpose

Introduce redirection without making contratempo compulsory.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a small cross, completed balance, then a new walking direction.
- Keep the direction change below a quarter turn for the core room.
- Offer contratempo exit only to stable couples.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the current pathway before accepting redirection.
- Observe planted-foot twisting.

### Student task

Core students cross and return. Progressing students cross, settle, and redirect into a new line.

### Success signal

Direction changes occur only after both partners are balanced.

### Common errors and corrections

- Redirection begins too early: “Complete the cross before choosing the exit.”
- Planted knee twists: “Release the foot and turn through the whole body.”

### Transition

Move into selective social-dance decisions.

## 49–54 minutes: Selection and restraint drill

### Purpose

Teach that blocks and crosses are occasional choices, not continuous decoration.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask leaders to dance mainly with Level 1 vocabulary.
- Permit one block or cross per phrase.
- Require a clear stop and free-leg check first.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe whether followers remain neutral and balanced.
- Reinforce opting out by returning to walking when the preparation is unclear.

### Student task

Dance continuously and use at most one prepared leg action per phrase.

### Success signal

Couples spend more time dancing than setting up leg actions.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before final practice.

### Transition

State the final social task and reduce teacher prompts.

## 54–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Social-dance task

Use one low block or simple cross per musical phrase. Identify the free leg before contact, keep both partners independently balanced, and recover immediately into walking or Basic 3.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Use only short prompts: “Stop,” “Read the weight,” “Low contact,” “Release,” and “Recover.”
- Intervene immediately for contact near a joint or any forced action.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe balance, anticipation, and consent signals.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Success signal

Most couples use isolated, controlled actions and return to social flow without repeated setup.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 2 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap preparation, free-leg recognition, low contact, release, and recovery.
- Explain that Week 2 extends the same safety standard into leg slides and tracing.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce personal axis and the right to stop any leg action.
- Ask which preparation cue felt clearest.

### Student task

Name the sequence: stop, identify, guide, release, recover.

### Success signal

Students understand that uncertainty means abandoning the leg action rather than forcing it.

# Demonstration plan

For each block and cross:

1. Show the complete action with music.
2. Show the prepared stop without music.
3. Identify weighted and free legs.
4. Show the leader-role pathway.
5. Show the follower-role responsibility.
6. Demonstrate the unsafe kick or joint contact.
7. Demonstrate the corrected low, quiet action.
8. Return to walking with music.

# Partner-rotation plan

- Rotations: after weight reading, after cross and return, and before independent practice.
- Use one consistent direction.
- Students may opt out of leg contact and practise preparation only.
- The teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Prepared stop
- Free-leg recognition
- One follower foot block
- One simple cross and return
- Recovery to Basic 3

## Progressing version

- Leader foot block
- Entry from Follower Saída
- Cross with a small direction change
- Adapt contact and range to a new partner

## Challenge version

- Contratempo exit
- Alternative entry from Saída
- Pivot preparation after a completed cross

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Contacting the weighted leg | “No contact until the free leg is certain.” |
| Kicking instead of guiding | “Place and guide; never strike.” |
| Contact near knee or ankle joint | “Move contact lower and remove pressure.” |
| Follower gives body weight | “Keep your axis over the standing leg.” |
| Leader forces the cross | “Guide the pathway and allow the partner to respond.” |
| Action repeats continuously | “One action, then return to dancing.” |

# Safety and consent

- Leg contact is optional and may be declined at any time.
- Contact stays low, soft, and away from joints.
- No pressure against the knee or ankle.
- Stop immediately if the partner resists, stiffens, or loses alignment.
- Both partners carry their own body weight.
- Teachers explain corrections before physical contact.

# Music plan

| Section | Music quality | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment and weight drills | Clear, spacious pulse | Low |
| Blocks and crosses | Slow, uncluttered Kizomba or Ghetto Zouk | Low to medium |
| Direction change | Clear phrasing and stable tempo | Medium |
| Independent practice | Social Fusion track with visible phrase structure | Medium |

# Success measures

## During the class

- Most students identify a free leg before contact.
- Blocks remain low and quiet.
- Crosses preserve the follower's standing axis.
- Couples recover into walking without correction steps.
- Students abandon unclear actions rather than forcing them.

## After the class

Record:

- Percentage ready for Level 2 core work
- Most common weight-reading error
- Any joint-contact or consent concern
- Which block was easier to control
- Percentage recovering without teacher counting
- Content requiring review in Week 2
