# Level 2, Week 3: Leg Lifts

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 2 / Fusion improvers
- Previous class: Level 2 Week 2

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to guide one small prepared free-leg lift, replace the foot safely, and recover into social flow while both partners remain independently balanced.

## Class purpose

Week 3 converts the low slide pathway into a small change of height. The class must make a clear distinction between guiding a free leg and lifting a partner. Stable axis, preparation, range control, and replacement are more important than the height of the action.

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

- Prepare and verify a genuinely free leg.
- Maintain a stable standing side during a small lift.
- Guide an inside lift without hooking or carrying body weight.
- Replace the foot before transferring weight.
- Continue into a cross or walk only after balance is restored.
- Reduce or abandon the lift when the partner's axis changes.

## Starting conditions

Students should demonstrate light slide contact, free-leg recognition, stable open connection, and the ability to replace the foot without an accidental transfer.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Lift preparation
- Small inside lift
- Lift and replace
- Lift into cross
- Entry from open parallel position
- Exit through replacement and walk

### Previewed only

- Entry from Follower Saída
- Syncopated lift
- Lift into pivot preparation

### Excluded

- Lifting the follower's body weight
- Hooking behind the knee
- High or fast lifts
- Surprise lifts
- Repeated lift combinations

## Technical foundations

- Stop and identify the free leg first.
- Establish the standing axis before changing height.
- Guide the pathway from close to the floor.
- Keep the lift below the partner's comfortable range.
- The follower remains active and balanced; no jumping.
- Replace the free foot before continuing unless a clear cross is prepared.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Week 2 recap and readiness check

### Purpose

Confirm that students can prepare, slide, replace, and recover before adding height.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Lead one inside slide and one slide-and-replace.
- Observe range, contact, and weight clarity.
- Restate that resistance or loss of axis cancels the next action.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate active standing-side control.
- Identify students who should remain with no-contact or slide-only work.

### Student task

Prepare a free leg, complete one small slide, replace, and return to Basic 3.

### Success signal

Most couples recover without dragging, leaning, or early weight transfer.

### Transition

Separate and train standing-axis control individually.

## 5–11 minutes: Individual axis and free-leg height

### Purpose

Find a safe personal lift range before partner guidance.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Guide students to stand on one leg and move the free foot slightly from the floor.
- Demonstrate that height decreases when balance changes.
- Emphasise soft knee and vertical axis.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a tiny lift, pause, and controlled replacement.
- Observe locked knees, hip collapse, and hopping.

### Student task

Prepare the free leg, lift it only a few centimetres, pause, replace it quietly, and repeat on the other side.

### Success signal

Students lift and replace without jumping, gripping the floor, or changing the standing foot.

### Common errors and corrections

- Jumping: “The standing leg stays; only the free leg moves.”
- Lift becomes high: “Stop before your axis changes.”
- Foot drops: “Control the replacement as carefully as the lift.”

### Transition

Form partners and communicate the lift pathway without leg contact.

## 11–17 minutes: No-contact lift preparation

### Purpose

Establish shared timing and consent before physical guidance.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a prepared stop from open parallel position.
- Indicate the lift through body direction and frame without touching the leg.
- Pause and return to neutral.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain how the follower recognises preparation while maintaining personal axis.
- Demonstrate remaining grounded instead of jumping to create the effect.

### Student task

Leaders prepare and indicate a small lift. Followers move the free leg within their own range, replace, and recover.

### Success signal

Couples agree on timing without verbal planning or leg-to-leg contact.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once.

### Transition

Add low contact for the inside lift.

## 17–24 minutes: Small inside lift

### Purpose

Teach the core lift with minimal height and maximum control.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate stop, free-leg check, low contact, small upward pathway, and immediate release.
- Show that the leader's structure remains upright and does not scoop the partner.
- Contrast guiding the free leg with trying to lift the follower.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate stable standing-side support.
- Explain that the follower follows the pathway actively and never jumps.
- Show the right to reduce or stop the range.

### Student task

Prepare one small inside lift, pause briefly, release, replace the foot, and return to Basic 3.

### Success signal

The follower's body height and standing axis remain stable while only the free leg changes level.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader tries to lift body weight: “Guide the free leg; never lift the person.”
- Contact hooks behind the knee: “Keep contact low and away from the joint.”
- Follower jumps: “Stay grounded and let only the free leg respond.”

### Safety cue

No lift continues after resistance, loss of axis, or joint contact.

### Transition

Focus on controlled replacement.

## 24–30 minutes: Lift and replace

### Purpose

Make recovery as intentional as the lift.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate lowering the free leg to the floor before inviting any transfer.
- Show replacement into neutral and replacement into a walking line.
- Observe leaders who release too early.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate controlling the descent rather than dropping the foot.
- Reinforce waiting for the transfer invitation.

### Student task

Lift, pause, replace quietly, confirm balance, and walk out.

### Success signal

The foot returns under control and the next step begins only after both partners are stable.

### Common errors and corrections

- Foot drops: “Lower with control; do not release the leg suddenly.”
- Weight transfers on landing: “Replace first, then receive the next direction.”
- Leader holds the leg: “Guide and release; never trap.”

### Transition

Use the controlled replacement pathway to create a cross.

## 30–37 minutes: Lift into cross

### Purpose

Connect the lift to Week 1 crossing without increasing force.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a small lift followed by a guided cross.
- Pause before weight transfer.
- Offer lift-and-replace as the core alternative.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining the standing axis while the free leg crosses.
- Reinforce that the cross position does not require immediate weight.

### Student task

Core students lift and replace. Progressing students lift, guide into a small cross, pause, and recover.

### Success signal

The cross follows naturally from the free-leg pathway without pulling the torso or increasing height.

### Common errors and corrections

- Lift and cross merge too quickly: “Complete the lift before changing direction.”
- Follower transfers automatically: “Cross first; weight only when invited.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

Practise entries and exits with a new partner.

## 37–43 minutes: Entry from open parallel position

### Purpose

Create a repeatable social entry with clear preparation.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate open parallel alignment, prepared stop, small lift, replace, and walk.
- Reinforce reducing range after every partner rotation.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting without presenting the leg.
- Observe anticipation and bracing.

### Student task

Use the complete core sequence once, then return to social vocabulary before repeating.

### Success signal

Couples complete the action without verbal planning and return immediately to walking.

### Transition

Add the Follower Saída entry for stable couples.

## 43–48 minutes: Follower Saída entry and controlled exit

### Purpose

Apply the lift inside an existing pathway without turning the class into choreography.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a compact Follower Saída, settle the free leg, create one small lift, and exit through replacement.
- Keep open-parallel entry available as the core version.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Show how to complete the Saída before the lift preparation.
- Observe lifts attempted before weight becomes clear.

### Student task

Choose open-parallel or Follower Saída entry, complete one lift, and exit through walking or a cross.

### Success signal

The lift happens after a complete preparation, not during an unfinished Saída step.

### Transition

Train restraint and partner adaptation.

## 48–53 minutes: Range calibration and selection

### Purpose

Teach that lifts are occasional, partner-specific choices.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask leaders to begin with the smallest possible lift.
- Permit one lift per phrase only when axis and free-leg status are clear.
- Require a slide or no action when preparation is uncertain.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce nonverbal resistance, verbal opt-out, and active balance.
- Observe whether followers feel pressured to produce height.

### Student task

Dance mainly with social vocabulary and use at most one prepared lift per phrase.

### Success signal

Students choose fewer, smaller lifts and adapt range after changing partner.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before final practice.

### Transition

Set the independent social task.

## 53–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Social-dance task

Use one small lift only after the follower is stable and the free leg is clear. Replace the foot before transferring weight, then return to walking, Basic 3, or a controlled cross.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Prompt only: “Prepare,” “Free leg,” “Small range,” “Replace,” and “Recover.”
- Stop any hooking, jumping, or attempt to carry weight.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe axis, consent, and controlled replacement.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Success signal

Most couples use isolated, low-range lifts and recover without loss of balance.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 4 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap prepare, guide, pause, replace, recover.
- Explain that Week 4 uses completed crosses and free-leg control to introduce pivots and hook preparation.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce that followers never jump or surrender body weight.
- Ask which preparation gave the clearest sense of safety.

### Student task

State the difference between guiding a free leg and lifting a partner.

### Success signal

Students understand that height is optional and control is mandatory.

# Demonstration plan

1. Show the complete small lift with music.
2. Identify the standing and free legs.
3. Demonstrate no-contact preparation.
4. Add low guiding contact.
5. Show unsafe body lifting, knee hooking, and follower jumping.
6. Demonstrate controlled replacement.
7. Return to walking with music.

# Partner-rotation plan

- Rotations: after no-contact preparation, after lift into cross, and before independent practice.
- Restart with minimal range for every new partner.
- Students may practise slide-only or no-contact versions.
- Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Prepared stop
- Small inside lift
- Lift and replace
- Walk out

## Progressing version

- Lift into cross
- Entry from Follower Saída
- Adapt range to a new partner

## Challenge version

- Syncopated lift after stable preparation
- Lift into pivot preparation
- Alternative controlled exit

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Trying to lift body weight | “Guide the free leg, not the person.” |
| Hooking behind the knee | “Keep contact low and away from joints.” |
| Follower jumping | “Stay grounded; only the free leg moves.” |
| Lift exceeds axis control | “Reduce height before balance changes.” |
| Foot drops on replacement | “Control the way down.” |
| Weight transfers too early | “Replace first; transfer second.” |

# Safety and consent

- Every lift is optional.
- The leader never lifts or supports the follower's body weight.
- No hooking behind the knee or pressure on joints.
- Range stays below the partner's comfortable limit.
- Stop immediately when resistance, pain, fear, or axis loss appears.
- Followers may choose slide-only or no-contact participation.
- Teachers explain corrections before physical contact.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Students prepare a clear free leg.
- Followers remain grounded and independently balanced.
- Lift range stays small.
- Replacement is controlled.
- Couples abandon unclear lifts rather than forcing them.

## After the class

Record:

- Percentage controlling the standing axis
- Any hooking, jumping, or body-weight concern
- Which entry produced the clearest preparation
- Whether lift into cross should remain progressing content
- Percentage adapting range after rotation
- Content requiring review in Week 4

# Open questions and test points

- Is the no-contact stage long enough?
- Can the weakest admitted student replace the foot without an accidental transfer?
- Does Follower Saída improve preparation or add overload?
- Are students prioritising height over control?
- Which musical accent supports a small lift without encouraging a kick?
