# Level 2, Week 4: Pivots, Hooks, and Rotations

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 2 / Fusion improvers
- Previous class: Level 2 Week 3

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to complete controlled quarter and half pivots, connect one pivot to a leg cross, and return to walking while protecting each partner's axis and knees.

## Class purpose

Week 4 develops rotational control from the small turns taught in Level 1 and the free-leg preparation developed in Weeks 1–3. Rotation is created through the feet and body, never by twisting a planted leg or turning the partner through the arms. Hook preparation remains optional and is introduced only after stable pivots.

## Learning outcomes

Students should be able to:

- Prepare a pivot from a stable open line or completed cross.
- Release the standing foot before changing orientation.
- Complete quarter and half pivots within personal balance.
- Maintain knee-to-toe alignment.
- Connect a pivot to a small leg cross.
- Return to a circle or walking line only after both partners finish rotating.
- Abandon hook preparation when the free leg is unclear.

## Starting conditions

Students should demonstrate controlled quarter turns, stable lift-and-replace, clear free-leg recognition, and an open-position frame that does not depend on arm force.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Individual pivot mechanics
- Partner quarter pivot
- Partner half pivot
- Pivot into leg cross
- Entry from a completed cross or open line
- Exit to walking or compact circle

### Previewed only

- Lift into hook preparation
- Travelling pivot
- Reverse rotational exit
- Suspension preparation

### Excluded

- Forced hooks
- Multiple spins
- Fast travelling pivots
- Rotation on a planted foot
- Arm-led turning

## Technical foundations

- Complete the current transfer before rotating.
- Release and reorganise the feet as the body turns.
- Keep knees aligned with toes.
- Maintain individual axis and responsive connection.
- The follower completes the current rotation before receiving a new direction.
- A hook requires a genuinely free leg and remains optional.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Week 3 recap and rotational readiness

### Purpose

Confirm free-leg control and balanced recovery before adding pivots.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Lead a small lift-and-replace and lift-into-cross recap.
- Observe standing axis and whether students twist during the cross.
- Introduce the class promise and knee-safety rule.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate controlled replacement and stable open frame.
- Identify students who should remain with quarter pivots only.

### Student task

Prepare a free leg, create one small cross, settle balance, and walk out.

### Success signal

Most students complete the cross without planted-foot twisting or loss of axis.

### Transition

Separate and isolate pivot mechanics.

## 5–11 minutes: Individual pivot mechanics

### Purpose

Teach safe rotation through the feet before partner connection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a quarter pivot with released feet and organised posture.
- Show the unsafe planted-foot twist.
- Progress from quarter to half pivot only when balance remains clear.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate soft knees, toe-knee alignment, and controlled spotting of the new direction.
- Observe hopping, locked knees, and upper-body-only turning.

### Student task

Complete quarter pivots, pause in balance, then attempt a half pivot using small reorganising steps or a controlled swivel within comfort.

### Success signal

Students finish each rotation with stable weight and knees aligned over the feet.

### Common errors and corrections

- Planted-foot twist: “Release the foot before changing orientation.”
- Upper body turns alone: “Let the feet and body arrive together.”
- Rotation exceeds balance: “Reduce the angle before adding speed.”

### Transition

Form partners and communicate one quarter pivot without leg action.

## 11–17 minutes: Partner rotation preparation

### Purpose

Establish shared timing without arm-led turning.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate preparation from an open line.
- Create a small rotational invitation through the centre and pathway.
- Stop after one quarter turn.
- Show the incorrect hand-driven version.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain receiving rotation while maintaining personal axis.
- Demonstrate completing the turn before accepting a new direction.
- Observe anticipation and leaning.

### Student task

Leaders prepare one quarter turn. Followers complete it independently. Both pause and return to neutral.

### Success signal

Couples rotate without pulling the arms or using the partner for balance.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once.

### Transition

Build the complete quarter pivot from the new partnership.

## 17–24 minutes: Quarter pivot

### Purpose

Teach the core rotational unit with clear entry and recovery.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate entry from open line and completed cross.
- Show quarter pivot, balance check, and walk out.
- Observe movement size and arm tension.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate foot release and stable axis.
- Reinforce that the follower does not add extra rotation.
- Observe knees turning away from toes.

### Student task

Enter, complete one quarter pivot, settle, and return to walking or Basic 3.

### Success signal

Most couples complete the quarter pivot and recover without correction steps.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader turns through the arms: “Lead the pathway; do not rotate the shoulders.”
- Follower adds rotation: “Complete only the direction that is available.”
- Couple exits early: “Finish the turn before choosing the line.”

### Transition

Extend the same mechanics into a half pivot.

## 24–31 minutes: Half pivot

### Purpose

Increase rotation while preserving the same safety standard.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate two connected quarter turns as the accessible half-pivot pathway.
- Offer a smoother half pivot only to stable couples.
- Emphasise that speed does not create clarity.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining axis through the midpoint.
- Observe students who collapse, hop, or twist the standing knee.

### Student task

Core students connect two quarter pivots. Progressing students complete one controlled half pivot, pause, and recover.

### Success signal

Students maintain balance and connection throughout the full change of orientation.

### Common errors and corrections

- Momentum carries the student past the endpoint: “Reduce speed and finish the intended angle.”
- Feet remain fixed: “Allow the base to reorganise.”
- Frame becomes rigid: “Keep the connection responsive, not locked.”

### Transition

Use a completed pivot to create a free-leg cross.

## 31–38 minutes: Pivot into leg cross

### Purpose

Connect rotational control to the crossing vocabulary from Week 1.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate quarter pivot, completed balance, free-leg recognition, and small cross.
- Pause before weight transfer.
- Show the unsafe version where the cross begins during rotation.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the pivot before allowing the free leg to cross.
- Observe planted-foot twisting and automatic transfer.

### Student task

Pivot, settle, identify the free leg, guide one cross, and recover to walking.

### Success signal

The cross begins only after rotation is complete and the free leg is clear.

### Common errors and corrections

- Cross starts too early: “Finish rotation before leg action.”
- Follower transfers automatically: “Cross is a position before it becomes a step.”
- Leader pulls the torso: “Guide the free-leg pathway after the axis settles.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

Introduce optional hook preparation without completing a forced hook.

## 38–44 minutes: Hook preparation

### Purpose

Clarify what makes a hook safe and why it is not compulsory.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a small lift or cross that produces a clearly free leg.
- Show a minimal hook-shaped pathway without pressure behind the knee.
- Emphasise immediate release and replacement.
- Keep pivot-into-cross as the core alternative.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate active free-leg control and personal range.
- Explain that followers do not wrap or clamp the leg.
- Show the opt-out response: replace and walk.

### Student task

Core students repeat pivot into cross. Progressing students explore a minimal hook preparation, release, replace, and recover.

### Success signal

No hook is forced, trapped, or used to support weight.

### Safety cue

Any unclear free leg, joint contact, or loss of axis cancels the hook preparation.

### Transition

Connect entries and exits without adding further figures.

## 44–49 minutes: Entry and exit choices

### Purpose

Make pivots adaptable to social flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate entries from open line and completed cross.
- Show exits to walking and a compact circle.
- Require both partners to complete rotation before exit.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without predicting the exit.
- Observe late recovery and overrotation.

### Student task

Choose one entry, quarter or half pivot, optional cross, and one controlled exit.

### Success signal

Couples change direction without verbal planning or arm force.

### Transition

Apply a one-sequence-per-phrase restraint rule.

## 49–54 minutes: Rotational selection drill

### Purpose

Prevent continuous pivot combinations and prioritise recovery.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask leaders to use one pivot sequence per phrase, then return to walking.
- Require a smaller version with each new partner.
- Reinforce cancelling rotation when floor space is unclear.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe axis, anticipation, and partner-specific range.
- Reinforce returning to neutral after any missed lead.

### Student task

Dance mainly with walking vocabulary and insert one controlled pivot sequence per phrase.

### Success signal

Students spend more time in social flow than in rotational setup.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before final practice.

### Transition

Set the independent task.

## 54–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Social-dance task

Use one pivot sequence, then return to walking. Change direction only after both partners complete the rotation. Add a cross only when the free leg and standing axis are clear.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Prompt only: “Prepare,” “Release the foot,” “Complete,” “Recover,” and “Walk.”
- Stop any forced hook or planted-knee twist immediately.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe individual axis and completion of rotation.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Success signal

Most couples complete a compact pivot and recover without carrying momentum into the next action.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 5 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap prepare, pivot, complete, optional cross, recover.
- Explain that Week 5 uses controlled stops and completed pivots to explore suspension and release.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce knee alignment and personal axis.
- Ask whether quarter or half pivot produced better control.

### Student task

Identify the condition that must exist before a hook or cross: completed rotation and a clear free leg.

### Success signal

Students understand that smaller controlled rotation is preferable to forced range.

# Demonstration plan

1. Show the complete quarter pivot with music.
2. Demonstrate foot release and body alignment without music.
3. Show leader-role preparation and follower-role completion.
4. Show unsafe arm turning and planted-foot twisting.
5. Demonstrate recovery before a new direction.
6. Add the cross only after the pivot is complete.
7. Show hook preparation as optional and minimal.
8. Return to walking with music.

# Partner-rotation plan

- Rotations: after partner preparation, after pivot into cross, and before independent practice.
- Begin with quarter pivots and reduced range after every rotation.
- Students may opt out of hook preparation.
- Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Quarter pivot
- Two connected quarter turns as a half-pivot option
- Return to walking

## Progressing version

- Smooth half pivot
- Pivot into leg cross
- Entry from completed cross
- Exit to compact circle

## Challenge version

- Minimal lift into hook preparation
- Travelling pivot with clear floor space
- Reverse rotational exit
- Suspension preparation after completed recovery

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Twisting a planted foot | “Release the foot before rotating.” |
| Turning through the arms | “Lead the pathway, not the shoulders.” |
| Hook without free-leg preparation | “No hook until rotation and weight are complete.” |
| Follower overrotates | “Complete only the direction that is available.” |
| Cross begins during pivot | “Finish rotation before leg action.” |
| Momentum prevents recovery | “Reduce speed and return to walking.” |

# Safety and consent

- No forced hooks or joint pressure.
- Keep knees aligned with toes.
- Reduce the rotation immediately when either partner loses axis.
- Hook preparation is optional and may be replaced with a simple cross.
- No one uses the partner for balance.
- Teachers explain corrections before physical contact.

| Section | Music quality | Energy |

| Pivot mechanics | Clear, moderate pulse | Low |
| Partner pivots | Stable rhythm and spacious phrasing | Low to medium |
| Pivot into cross | Defined accents without excessive speed | Medium |
| Independent practice | Fusion track with clear rotational opportunities | Medium |

# Success measures

## During the class

- Students release the feet before rotating.
- Knees remain aligned with toes.
- Couples complete rotation before crossing or exiting.
- Hook preparation remains small and optional.
- Most couples return to walking after one pivot sequence.

## After the class

Record:

- Quarter versus half-pivot success rate
- Most common knee or foot-placement error
- Any arm-led or forced-hook concern
- Percentage completing the cross after, not during, rotation
- Whether hook preparation should remain challenge-only
- Content requiring review in Week 5

# Open questions and test points

- Is the half pivot achievable for the weakest admitted student?
- Does teaching two quarter turns produce better mechanics than one half pivot?
- Can students separate rotation from leg action?
- Does hook preparation add value or overload the class?
- Which music supports control without encouraging spinning?
