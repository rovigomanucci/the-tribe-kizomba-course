# Level 1, Week 8: Social Dance Flow

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners completing the cycle
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 7

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to dance a complete song using Level 1 choices rather than a memorised routine, while adapting to the partner, music, and available floor space.

## Class purpose

Week 8 integrates the complete Level 1 vocabulary into social dancing.

The objective is not to prove that students can reproduce every movement in a fixed order. Students should demonstrate that they can:

- Begin simply
- Make clear movement choices
- Adapt to a new partner
- Protect surrounding floor space
- Use pauses and timing intentionally
- Recover after mistakes
- Reduce complexity when timing or communication is lost
- Complete a song without constant teacher counting

## Starting conditions

Students should have introductory experience with the previous Level 1 material:

- Marca, Basic 1, Basic 2, and Basic 3
- Forward and backward walking
- Stops and restarts
- Clockwise Estrela
- Leader's Saída
- Follower's Saída
- Simple Vírgula and Retrocesso
- Pauses, taps, and delayed exits
- Floorcraft and recovery

Students do not need equal mastery of every figure. The core assessment is safe, connected, adaptable social flow.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Dance continuously through a complete song.
- Use at least three Level 1 movement families.
- Include one compact rotation.
- Include one Saída.
- Include one pause or timing choice.
- Recover through Marca or Basic 3 after uncertainty.
- Adapt step size and vocabulary to floor space.
- Avoid sequence dependence.
- Dance successfully with more than one partner.
- Identify the skills that need further practice after Level 1.

## Movement scope

### Fully integrated

- Marca
- Basic 1
- Basic 2
- Basic 3
- Short forward and backward walks
- Stop and restart
- Clockwise Estrela
- Leader's Saída
- Follower's Saída
- Simple Vírgula
- Retrocesso
- Pause
- Tap and go
- Delayed exit
- Recovery through Marca or Basic 3

### Previewed only

- Personal choice of preferred movement families
- Reduced-vocabulary musical interpretation
- Open-position continuation toward Level 2

### Excluded

- New figures
- Fixed graduation choreography
- Assessment based on completing every movement
- Large pivots or travelling rotations
- Forced close connection
- Added complexity after timing is lost

## Technical foundations

- Quality and recovery matter more than quantity.
- Complete the current transfer before changing movement.
- Use the smallest movement that communicates clearly.
- Check floor space before travelling, rotating, or opening a corridor.
- Repeat simple movements long enough for the partner to understand them.
- Return to Marca or Basic 3 when timing or orientation becomes unclear.
- Follow the current information rather than predicting a sequence.
- Adapt to each new partner rather than reproducing identical movement size.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Welcome and course-completion framing

### Purpose

Set expectations for an integration class rather than a performance test.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Introduce the class promise.
- Explain that no new figures will be taught.
- Clarify that successful recovery counts as skill.
- Explain the complete-song objective and partner rotations.
- Reinforce that students are not assessed through fixed choreography.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce that followers are not expected to know the sequence.
- Explain that personal balance and clear response are core achievements.
- Demonstrate a simple flow with one intentional recovery.

### Student task

- Listen to the class objective.
- Select a starting role.
- Identify one movement family they feel confident using and one they want to review.

### Success signal

Students understand that clarity, adaptability, floorcraft, and recovery are the class priorities.

### Transition

Spread students out for an individual pulse and weight-transfer warm-up.

## 5–11 minutes: Foundations and recovery warm-up

### Purpose

Reconnect the foundations shared by every Level 1 movement.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Lead pulse, Marca, Basic 1, Basic 2, and Basic 3 individually.
- Add stops and restarts.
- Intentionally interrupt the sequence and recover through Marca.
- Reinforce complete weight transfer.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from a second viewing angle.
- Observe posture, balance, and tapping errors.
- Support students who need a simplified recap.

### Student task

- Dance the four basic movement choices individually.
- Stop with clear weight placement.
- Recover through Marca after an intentional mistake.
- Change movement only after completing the current transfer.

### Success signal

Most students move through the basic vocabulary and recover without losing balance.

### Common errors and corrections

- Movement changes mid-transfer: “Finish the current step first.”
- Students stop after a mistake: “Use Marca to keep dancing.”
- Steps become large: “Reduce the range and keep the pulse.”

### Transition

Form partners and rebuild simple connected flow before adding movement families.

## 11–17 minutes: Connected basics and recovery

### Purpose

Establish a stable base with the first partner.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a flow using only Marca, Basic 1, Basic 2, and Basic 3.
- Include one stop and one recovery.
- Ask leaders to repeat movements rather than change continuously.
- Observe arm use and timing.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without verbal cues.
- Reinforce complete transfer and personal balance.
- Observe anticipation and connection tension.

### Student task

- Dance using only the four basic choices.
- Repeat each movement before changing.
- Include one stop and restart.
- Recover through Marca when uncertain.

### Success signal

Couples maintain continuous flow without relying on advanced figures.

### Common errors and corrections

- Too many changes: “Use fewer choices and repeat them longer.”
- Follower predicts: “Respond to the movement that exists now.”
- Leader pulls during recovery: “Rebuild the pulse before adding direction.”

### Transition

Add one rotational movement and its safe exit.

## 17–23 minutes: Rotation integration

### Purpose

Review Estrela as a selective floorcraft choice rather than a required sequence element.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3 into compact clockwise Estrela.
- Show walking and Marca exits.
- Reinforce checking circular floor space before entry.
- Ask leaders to use no more than one Estrela during the drill.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate walking around the shared centre.
- Reinforce personal axis and avoiding prediction.
- Observe crossing and circle expansion.

### Student task

- Dance simple basics.
- Enter one compact Estrela when space allows.
- Choose walking or Marca as the exit.
- Continue social flow.

### Success signal

Most couples complete one selective Estrela and continue without losing orientation.

### Common errors and corrections

- Estrela repeated continuously: “One rotation is enough; return to simple flow.”
- Circle expands: “Protect the shared centre.”
- Exit becomes abrupt: “Finish the circular step before choosing the line.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once at the end of the section.

### Transition

With the new partner, integrate one Saída.

## 23–29 minutes: Saída integration

### Purpose

Review corridor decisions and partner adaptation.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate choosing either Leader's Saída or Follower's Saída according to available space.
- Reinforce lane protection and clear preparation.
- Ask leaders to use only one Saída during the drill.
- Observe arm force and corridor width.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining the prepared pathway.
- Reinforce waiting and independent balance.
- Observe anticipation, drift, and forced torso rotation.

### Student task

- Begin with basic flow.
- Choose one Saída.
- Keep the corridor compact.
- Return into Basic 3 or Marca.
- Adapt movement size to the new partner.

### Success signal

Couples complete one Saída and return without collision, pushing, or memorised anticipation.

### Common errors and corrections

- Corridor is unavailable: “Choose another movement rather than forcing the entry.”
- Leader blocks the pathway: “Create the lane before inviting travel.”
- Follower predicts: “Stay with the current direction until it changes.”

### Transition

Add one compact redirection and immediate recovery.

## 29–35 minutes: Vírgula, Retrocesso, and recovery

### Purpose

Review redirection as a choice rather than a mandatory continuation.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate one simple Vírgula and one Retrocesso.
- Compare the floor-space requirements.
- Show immediate recovery into Marca after an unclear lead.
- Ask leaders to choose only one redirection during the drill.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate foot release and waiting for the exit.
- Reinforce personal balance.
- Observe planted-knee twisting and anticipation.

### Student task

- Dance simple flow.
- Select one compact redirection.
- Exit into Basic 3.
- Recover immediately through Marca when communication is unclear.

### Success signal

Couples complete or safely abandon the redirection without forcing the original plan.

### Common errors and corrections

- Rotation becomes large: “Use only the direction the exit requires.”
- Missed lead is forced: “Recover and choose again.”
- Exit begins before balance: “Finish the redirection first.”

### Transition

Review one timing choice before moving into longer social practice.

## 35–41 minutes: Pause and timing integration

### Purpose

Include musical choice without destabilising the flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3 with one pause.
- Add an optional tap-and-go or delayed exit.
- Reinforce balanced stillness and clear restart preparation.
- Ask leaders to use one timing choice only.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting without counting the pause length.
- Reinforce the difference between a tap and a weighted step.
- Observe comfort and balance.

### Student task

- Dance one familiar movement family.
- Add one pause, tap-and-go, or delayed exit.
- Restart after clear preparation.
- Return to Marca when balance is lost.

### Success signal

Most couples include one timing choice and continue without losing the shared pulse.

### Common errors and corrections

- Tap receives weight: “Touch and remain on the standing foot.”
- Restart is guessed: “Wait for the new movement information.”
- Timing play becomes too frequent: “One clear choice is enough.”

### Transition

Introduce a constrained flow task using three movement families.

## 41–47 minutes: Three-family flow challenge

### Purpose

Build decision-making without requiring the complete vocabulary.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask each leader to choose three movement families only.
- Require one recovery through Marca.
- Encourage repetition and clear transitions.
- Observe floorcraft and sequence dependence.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following a limited vocabulary with no known order.
- Observe anticipation and partner adaptation.
- Reinforce relaxed connection.

### Student task

- Select three movement families.
- Use them in any order.
- Include one recovery.
- Avoid adding a fourth family until the flow is stable.

### Success signal

Couples create a coherent flow using limited vocabulary rather than chasing quantity.

### Common errors and corrections

- Students try to include everything: “Three families are enough.”
- Fixed order repeats: “Change the order or repeat one family longer.”
- Complexity increases after timing loss: “Reduce to Marca and Basic 3.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

Use the new partner for an adaptability round.

## 47–52 minutes: New-partner adaptability round

### Purpose

Test whether students communicate rather than reproduce identical movement size and timing.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask leaders to begin with Marca and Basic 3.
- Delay advanced vocabulary until the new connection is stable.
- Encourage smaller movement with an unfamiliar partner.
- Observe adaptation and consent.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce waiting for the new partner's information.
- Observe whether followers assume the previous partner's movement size.
- Support students who need a simpler task.

### Student task

- Begin with simple basics.
- Establish connection and pulse.
- Add one rotation, Saída, redirection, or timing choice only after the flow is stable.
- Adjust movement size and connection to the new partner.

### Success signal

Couples establish comfortable flow with a new partner before adding complexity.

### Transition

Explain the complete-song task and success criteria.

## 52–58 minutes: Complete-song social dance

### Purpose

Deliver the student-facing promise and observe independent social dancing.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start one complete beginner-friendly song or a continuous six-minute section.
- Give only the opening reminders: start simply, check the floor, recover early, and use fewer moves.
- Stop speaking after the opening phase.
- Observe the full room without interrupting individual mistakes.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe balance, anticipation, connection comfort, and recovery.
- Join temporarily only when role numbers require it.
- Record broad patterns for the after-class review.

### Student task

Dance continuously through the complete track using:

- At least three movement families
- One compact rotation
- One Saída
- One pause or timing choice
- One recovery through Marca or Basic 3

Students should omit any requirement that cannot be completed safely or clearly.

### Success signal

Most couples complete the song with continuous flow, safe floorcraft, and at least one visible recovery.

### Teacher intervention rule

Do not stop the whole song unless an unsafe pattern affects multiple couples. Quality, consent, and recovery take priority over completing the task list.

## 58–60 minutes: Course recap and next-step reflection

### Purpose

Close the cycle, reinforce achievement, and identify future development needs.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap the Level 1 principles: pulse, complete transfer, connection, floorcraft, choice, and recovery.
- Emphasise that the course outcome is social-dance independence, not figure quantity.
- Explain the expected readiness criteria for the next stage without teaching new content.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce personal balance, response, consent, and adaptability.
- Invite students to identify one strength and one practice priority.

### Student task

- Reflect on the complete-song experience.
- Identify one movement family they trust.
- Identify one technical skill needing more practice.
- Ask final questions.

### Success signal

Students can describe their progress in terms of transferable social-dance skills rather than only named figures.

# Demonstration plan

The teachers should demonstrate one short social flow that includes:

1. A simple beginning with Marca and Basic 3
2. One compact Estrela
3. One Saída
4. One redirection
5. One pause
6. One intentional recovery

Repeat the demonstration with a different order and fewer movements to prove that no fixed routine is required.

Show one overloaded version and contrast it with a simpler, clearer version.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After rotation integration
2. After the three-family challenge
3. Optional final rotation before the complete-song task when class numbers and time allow

Use one consistent direction. Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven. Students may switch roles or decline a rotation.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Marca
- Basic 1, Basic 2, and Basic 3
- Short walk
- One stop and restart
- One recovery
- Complete-song participation

## Progressing version

- Use at least three movement families.
- Include one compact Estrela.
- Include one Leader's or Follower's Saída.
- Include one pause or tap.
- Adapt movement size to a new partner.

## Challenge version

- Change the order of movement families.
- Use fewer moves for longer periods.
- Match movement size and direction to changing floor space.
- Use both Saídas at different points without repeating a fixed sequence.
- Recover deliberately after a planned interruption.

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Sequence dependence | “Choose from the current situation, not a memorised order.” |
| Trying to include every figure | “Use fewer movement families with more clarity.” |
| Ignoring floor space | “Let the available floor choose the movement.” |
| Adding complexity after timing loss | “Return to Marca or Basic 3.” |
| Repeating identical movement size with every partner | “Adapt to the person in front of you.” |
| Follower anticipates familiar sequences | “Respond to the current information.” |
| Recovery is treated as failure | “Recovery is part of social dancing.” |

# Safety and consent

- Quality and recovery matter more than quantity.
- No assessment through fixed choreography.
- Students may omit any requested movement that is unsafe, unclear, or uncomfortable.
- Open hold remains valid throughout.
- No forced close connection, pivots, body movement, or range.
- Both partners remain responsible for personal balance.
- Students may decline a partner, role, movement, or physical correction.
- Leaders must scan the floor before travelling, rotating, or opening a corridor.

# Teaching-role responsibilities

## Leader-role teacher

- Frame the class around decision-making, floorcraft, and recovery.
- Demonstrate the leader role across integrated movement families.
- Observe clarity, space selection, sequence dependence, and adaptation.
- Manage timing and rotations without over-coaching the final song.

## Follower-role teacher

- Demonstrate following without knowing the movement order.
- Explain balance, response, consent, and new-partner adaptation.
- Observe anticipation, comfort, weight transfer, and recovery.
- Record broad patterns during the complete-song task.

The roles may be exchanged without changing the plan.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Most couples maintain continuous basic flow.
- Students use at least three movement families without a fixed order.
- Rotations and Saídas remain compact and selective.
- Timing choices do not disrupt balance.
- Students recover through Marca or Basic 3.
- Couples adapt to a new partner.
- Most couples complete the final song without constant teacher counting.
- Floorcraft and consent remain visible throughout.

## After the class

Record:

- Percentage completing the full song
- Percentage recovering without teacher intervention
- Most common sequence-dependence pattern
- Most common floorcraft issue
- Movement family students avoided
- Movement family students overused
- Whether the final task list improved focus or created overload
- Readiness concerns before progression
- Curriculum changes required before the next cycle

# Connection to the programme

- Previous class: pauses, taps, delayed exits, and timing choices
- Next stage: Level 1 consolidation, supervised social practice, or progression assessment for the next course
- Skills carried forward: independent social flow, movement selection, partner adaptation, floorcraft, musical choice, consent, and recovery

# Open questions and test points

- Is six minutes sufficient for the complete-song task?
- Should the final task require both Saídas or allow one?
- Does specifying required elements encourage checklist dancing?
- Should the final song be followed by a second reduced-vocabulary song in future cycles?
- What percentage of students can recover without stopping?
- Which skills should determine readiness for the next level?
- Does the new-partner adaptability round need more time?
