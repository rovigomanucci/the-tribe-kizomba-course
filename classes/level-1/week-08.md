# Level 1, Week 8: Pause, Tap, and Social Dance Flow

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 7, Vírgula and Retrocesso

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to use a balanced pause or a simple tap as a musical choice, then dance a complete song using the Level 1 vocabulary with floor awareness, adaptation, and recovery.

## Class purpose

Week 8 completes the course without removing the musicality material. Students first learn the physical difference between a pause, a tap, and a weighted step. They then use one timing choice inside a guided complete-song social dance.

The aim is not to fit every figure into one song. Students learn to select fewer movement families, adapt to their partner and the floor, and recover early when communication becomes unclear.

## Starting conditions

Students should have introductory control of:

- Marca, Basic 1, Basic 2, and Basic 3
- Walking, stops, and restarts
- Estrela, Leader's Saída, and Follower's Saída
- Cha-cha, Vírgula, and Retrocesso
- Compact range, partner connection, and recovery through Marca or Basic 3

Students who are not yet stable with a tap remain with the pause-and-restart core version.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Pause on one clearly weighted foot.
- Tap without transferring body weight.
- Restart after a clear preparation.
- Use one pause, tap, or delayed exit without disrupting balance.
- Select suitable Level 1 movement families in any order.
- Adapt movement size and connection to a new partner and floor space.
- Recover through Marca or Basic 3 without treating recovery as failure.
- Complete a full song with reduced teacher support.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Balanced pause
- Tap without weight transfer
- Pause and restart in Basic 3
- One tap-and-go option
- One timing choice inside a familiar movement family
- Complete-song social-dance integration
- Recovery and floorcraft

### Previewed only

- Delayed exit from a Saída or redirection
- Optional Tarraxinha texture only where teachers judge it appropriate

### Excluded

- Complex syncopations
- Extended Tarraxinha patterns
- Styling-led taps
- Long fixed routines
- Checklists that force unsafe movement

## Technical foundations

- A pause finishes on one weighted foot.
- A tap touches the floor without taking body weight.
- The restart begins from clear body direction, not from the tap.
- Timing texture never removes personal balance or partner choice.
- Use fewer movements when space, connection, or rhythm is unclear.
- Recovery is part of good social dancing.

# Class sequence

## 0–6 minutes: Welcome and redirection recap

### Purpose

Reconnect recovery and complete transfer before students reduce the movement into a pause.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap one small Vírgula or Retrocesso into Basic 3.
- Demonstrate recovering through Marca when the exit is unclear.
- Explain that the class adds timing texture, then tests the full course vocabulary.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the current transfer before a change.
- Observe anticipation, balance, and connection range.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3.
- Choose one compact redirection or remain with Basic 3.
- Recover through Marca or Basic 3.

### Success signal

Most couples maintain flow and recover without force before adding a pause.

### Transition

Separate students and turn the stop into a balanced pause.

## 6–13 minutes: Balanced pause individually

### Purpose

Teach the physical quality of a pause before partner communication.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a balanced pause and contrast it with freezing between both feet.
- Hold the pause for one or two pulses.
- Show a clear restart from the standing foot.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from a second angle.
- Observe students who shift weight during the pause.
- Reinforce quiet upper body and independent balance.

### Student task

- Walk or dance Basic 3.
- Finish a transfer.
- Pause on one weighted foot.
- Restart after one or two pulses.

### Success signal

Most students pause and restart without losing balance or changing the weighted foot.

### Common errors and corrections

- Weight between both feet: “Choose one standing foot.”
- Restart happens immediately: “Finish the pause before moving.”
- Upper body collapses: “Stay settled above the standing leg.”

### Transition

Use the available free foot to introduce a tap.

## 13–20 minutes: Tap without weight transfer

### Purpose

Make the difference between a step and a tap unmistakable.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a weighted step and non-weighted tap side by side.
- Show that the same foot remains free after the tap.
- Keep the action small and close to the floor.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower action from a second angle.
- Observe students who transfer onto the tapping foot.

### Student task

- Pause on one foot.
- Tap the free foot lightly.
- Lift the same foot again to confirm it remained free.
- Restart from the standing foot.

### Success signal

Most students tap and lift the same foot again without an adjustment transfer.

### Common errors and corrections

- Tap receives weight: “Touch the floor, keep the body on the standing foot.”
- Tap becomes large: “Make the action smaller than a step.”
- Restart begins from the tap: “The tap stays free.”

### Transition

Form partners and communicate a simple pause before adding the tap.

## 20–28 minutes: Partner pause and restart

### Purpose

Teach shared stillness and a clear restart without gripping or verbal cues.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3 into a balanced pause.
- Show preparation through the torso before restart.
- Contrast clear preparation with pulling through the arms.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting without counting a fixed pause length.
- Reinforce personal balance and response to the new direction.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3.
- Complete a pause on a weighted foot.
- Restart after clear preparation.
- Recover through Marca if the restart is unclear.

### Success signal

Couples pause and restart together without gripping, leaning, or verbal cues.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once at the end of the section.

### Transition

Keep the new partner and add one small tap during the pause.

## 28–35 minutes: Tap and go

### Purpose

Add one light timing texture without turning it into styling or a new routine.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3, pause, tap, and restart.
- Keep the tap small and functional.
- Show the error of transferring weight onto the tap.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower tap action.
- Explain that the tap follows available balance and direction.
- Observe early restart and anticipation.

### Student task

- Enter a balanced pause.
- Add one small tap.
- Restart after a clear lead.
- Return to Marca if balance changes.

### Success signal

Couples complete a tap-and-go action without changing the standing foot or forcing the partner.

### Common errors and corrections

- Tap carries weight: “Touch and remain on the standing foot.”
- Leader moves the follower's leg: “Create the pause and direction, do not place the foot.”
- Tap becomes a styling competition: “Keep the action functional and small.”

### Transition

Use one timing choice inside a limited social flow.

## 35–42 minutes: Three-family flow with one timing choice

### Purpose

Build choice-making before the final song without overloading students.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask leaders to select only three movement families.
- Require one pause or tap choice and one recovery.
- Encourage repetition and clear transitions.
- Reinforce that cha-cha needs lateral space and redirection needs a clear exit.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following limited vocabulary with no known order.
- Observe anticipation and adaptation.
- Reinforce relaxed connection and available posture.

### Student task

- Select three movement families.
- Use them in any order.
- Add one pause, tap, or delayed exit.
- Include one recovery through Marca or Basic 3.
- Do not add a fourth family until the flow is stable.

### Success signal

Couples create a coherent flow using limited vocabulary and one clear timing choice.

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

Use the new partner for an adaptability round before the full song.

## 42–47 minutes: New-partner adaptability round

### Purpose

Test whether students communicate instead of reproducing movement size and timing from the prior partner.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask leaders to start simply with Marca and Basic 3.
- Delay cha-cha, Saídas, and redirection until the connection is stable.
- Encourage smaller movement with an unfamiliar partner.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce waiting for new partner information.
- Observe whether followers assume the prior partner's range or timing.

### Student task

- Begin with simple basics.
- Establish connection and pulse.
- Add one selected movement family and one timing choice after flow is stable.
- Adapt movement size and connection to the new partner.

### Success signal

Couples establish comfortable flow with a new partner before adding complexity.

### Transition

Explain the complete-song task and its reduced-vocabulary success criteria.

## 47–58 minutes: Complete-song social dance

### Purpose

Deliver the Level 1 outcome and observe independent social dancing.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start one complete beginner-friendly song.
- Give opening reminders only: start simply, check the floor, recover early, and use fewer movements.
- Stop speaking after the opening phase.
- Observe without interrupting individual mistakes.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe balance, anticipation, comfort, and recovery.
- Join temporarily only when role numbers require it.
- Record broad patterns for after-class review.

### Student task

Dance continuously through the complete track using:

- At least three movement families
- One selected rhythm or timing choice, such as cha-cha, pause, or tap
- One Saída or compact redirection when safe and clear
- One recovery through Marca or Basic 3

Students omit any item that cannot be completed safely or clearly.

### Success signal

Most couples complete the song with continuous flow, safe floorcraft, and at least one visible recovery.

### Teacher intervention rule

Do not stop the whole song unless an unsafe pattern affects multiple couples. Quality, consent, and recovery take priority over completing a task list.

## 58–60 minutes: Course recap and next-step reflection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap Level 1 principles: pulse, complete transfer, connection, choice, floorcraft, and recovery.
- Name cha-cha as core Level 1 vocabulary.
- Emphasise that social-dance independence matters more than figure quantity.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce personal balance, response, consent, and adaptation.
- Invite students to identify one strength and one practice priority.

### Student task

- Reflect on the complete-song experience.
- Identify one movement family they trust.
- Identify one technical skill needing more practice.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After partner pause and restart
2. After three-family flow
3. Optional final rotation before the complete-song task when class numbers and time allow

Students may switch roles or decline a rotation.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Marca and Basic 3
- One balanced pause
- One recovery
- Complete-song participation

## Progressing version

- Use three movement families.
- Include one cha-cha or tap choice.
- Include one Saída or compact redirection.
- Adapt movement size to a new partner.

## Challenge version

- Change the order of movement families.
- Use fewer movements for longer periods.
- Match movement size and direction to changing floor space.
- Use a delayed exit only when balance and connection remain clear.

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Pause between both feet | “Choose one standing foot.” |
| Tap takes weight | “Touch the floor, keep the body on the standing foot.” |
| Restart is guessed | “Wait for the new movement information.” |
| Trying to include every figure | “Use fewer movement families with more clarity.” |
| Ignoring floor space | “Let the available floor choose the movement.” |
| Adding complexity after timing loss | “Return to Marca or Basic 3.” |
| Recovery is treated as failure | “Recovery is part of social dancing.” |

# Safety and consent

- Quality and recovery matter more than quantity.
- Open hold remains valid.
- No forced close connection, pivots, body movement, or range.
- Both partners remain responsible for personal balance.
- Students may decline a partner, role, movement, or physical correction.
- Leaders scan the floor before lateral travel, rotation, or opening a corridor.
- Students omit any final-task item that is unsafe, unclear, or uncomfortable.

# Open questions and test points

- Is the pause-and-tap block sufficient before the final song?
- Do students reliably distinguish a tap from a step before partner work?
- Does the three-family constraint create focus rather than checklist dancing?
- What percentage of students recover without teacher intervention?
- Which movement families are avoided or overused?
- Which skills should determine readiness for Level 2?
