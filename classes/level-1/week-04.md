# Level 1, Week 4: Leader's Saída

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 3

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to create a clear leader pathway beside the follower, walk through the corridor without collision, and return to social flow without pushing or pulling.

## Class purpose

Week 3 taught students to redirect the partnership through Estrela and a quarter-turn exit. Week 4 uses that directional awareness to create two parallel walking lanes.

The Leader's Saída should not be taught as the leader forcing the follower out of the way. The follower maintains a stable pathway while the leader creates and uses an available corridor beside them.

## Starting conditions

Students should have introductory control of:

- Basic 3
- Short forward and backward walks
- Controlled stopping
- Clockwise Estrela
- Quarter-turn exits
- Small side steps
- Personal balance and light connection

Students who are not stable in the corridor position remain with the core entry and immediate return.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Recognise two parallel walking lanes.
- Prepare the corridor without pushing the follower sideways.
- Dance a compact Leader's Saída.
- Keep the leader out of the follower's line.
- Allow both partners to walk independently.
- Return to closed hold without collision.
- Exit into Basic 3 or Marca.
- Use the movement selectively according to floor space.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Lane-awareness drill
- Corridor position
- Leader's Saída from Basic 3
- Short corridor walk
- Return to closed hold
- Exit into Basic 3
- Recovery into Marca

### Previewed only

- Entry from Estrela
- Side exit
- Rotational exit
- Corridor setup for Follower's Saída

### Excluded

- Long corridor walks
- Fast lane changes
- Rotational Leader's Saída as core material
- Follower's Saída as a complete figure
- Arm-led redirection
- Large travelling combinations

## Technical foundations

- The follower's lane must remain available.
- The leader changes lane without stepping into the follower's path.
- Space is created through body direction and foot placement, not by pushing.
- Both partners maintain independent balance.
- Steps stay close to the floor and underneath the body.
- The return is prepared before the lanes close.
- The leader scans the floor before opening the corridor.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Welcome and Week 3 recap

### Purpose

Reconnect quarter-turn exits and controlled walking before introducing separate lanes.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Introduce the class promise.
- Lead a short recap of Basic 3, Estrela, and exit into walking.
- Reinforce small steps and clear orientation.
- Point out the open corridor created after the Estrela exit.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate personal axis through the Estrela exit.
- Observe followers who cross or drift sideways.
- Reinforce complete weight transfer and stable walking.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3.
- Enter one compact Estrela.
- Exit into a short walk.
- Stop in balance.

### Success signal

Most couples exit Estrela onto a clear line without pulling or collision.

### Transition

Separate students and introduce walking lanes without partner connection.

## 5–10 minutes: Lane-awareness drill individually

### Purpose

Make the geometry of the corridor visible before teaching the figure.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Mark two parallel lanes on the floor verbally or visually.
- Demonstrate walking forward in one lane.
- Step sideways into the second lane without crossing the first.
- Continue forward while keeping both lanes clear.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower remaining in the original lane.
- Show the collision created when the leader steps diagonally across the follower's line.
- Observe spacing and step size.

### Student task

- Walk forward in one lane.
- Move into the adjacent lane using a compact side step.
- Continue walking without crossing the original line.
- Return to the starting lane in balance.

### Success signal

Students can change lanes while preserving a clear parallel pathway.

### Common errors and corrections

- Diagonal crossing: “Move beside the lane, not through it.”
- Side step too large: “Create only the space you need.”
- Feet crossing: “Keep each step underneath your own pathway.”

### Transition

Assign students the leader-role pathway and repeat the lane change.

## 10–16 minutes: Leader pathway individually

### Purpose

Teach the leader footpath before connection adds complexity.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the essential Leader's Saída pathway without a partner.
- Show the entry, corridor walk, and return.
- Emphasise keeping the torso aligned with the intended lane.
- Avoid presenting a long count-based sequence.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Stand in the follower lane as a visual reference without connection.
- Show where a collision would occur if the leader enters the follower's line.
- Observe students who rotate unnecessarily.

### Student task

- Enter the adjacent lane.
- Take two or three compact walking steps.
- Prepare the return.
- Re-enter the original orientation without crossing the imagined follower pathway.

### Success signal

Most students complete the leader pathway without stepping through the follower lane.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader rotates too much: “Change lane first; keep the pathway simple.”
- Return begins late: “Prepare the return before the lanes close.”
- Steps travel too far: “Keep the corridor compact.”

### Transition

Form partners and create the corridor with minimal travel.

## 16–22 minutes: Corridor-position partner drill

### Purpose

Establish the position and connection before walking through it.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the entry from Basic 3 into the corridor position.
- Show how the leader moves beside the follower without pushing them away.
- Pause in the position so students can see both lanes.
- Demonstrate the incorrect arm-led version.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain the follower experience of maintaining the original pathway.
- Demonstrate stable personal balance.
- Show that the follower should not step sideways automatically.
- Observe followers who rotate or leave their lane.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Prepare the lane change through body direction.
- Move beside the follower.
- Keep the follower's path available.

Followers:

- Complete the current step.
- Maintain the original lane.
- Avoid moving sideways unless clearly redirected.

### Success signal

Couples reach the corridor position without pushing, pulling, or colliding.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader pushes follower sideways: “Create your lane with your feet, not the arms.”
- Follower abandons the lane: “Keep walking on the pathway already established.”
- Couple leans apart: “Maintain independent balance and light contact.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once at the end of the drill.

### Transition

Keep the new partner and add the full Leader's Saída.

## 22–30 minutes: Core Leader's Saída

### Purpose

Teach the complete essential figure from Basic 3 into the corridor.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the full movement with music.
- Repeat slowly without music.
- Show the leader pathway from the front and side.
- Enter from Basic 3.
- Pause in the corridor before continuing.
- Observe lane choice, arm use, and movement size.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower pathway.
- Reinforce forward walking and complete transfer.
- Explain that the follower should not memorise the leader footwork.
- Observe anticipation and unwanted side steps.

### Student task

- Begin in Basic 3.
- Enter the Leader's Saída.
- Arrive in the corridor position.
- Take one or two controlled steps.
- Stop before returning.

### Success signal

Most couples enter the corridor cleanly and maintain two visible lanes.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader steps into follower line: “Protect the follower's lane before moving beside it.”
- Follower turns toward the leader: “Continue on the prepared pathway.”
- Connection becomes rigid: “Let the feet create the geometry.”
- Students rush through the position: “Arrive before continuing.”

### Transition

Extend the corridor walk while keeping the distance compact.

## 30–36 minutes: Corridor walk

### Purpose

Develop independent walking beside the partner.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate two or three parallel walking steps.
- Keep the shoulders and connection relaxed.
- Show how oversized steps make the lanes collapse.
- Observe leaders who pull the follower to maintain alignment.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate walking independently in the original lane.
- Reinforce small steps and forward intention.
- Observe followers who drift toward the leader.

### Student task

- Enter the corridor.
- Walk two or three steps in parallel lanes.
- Maintain the pulse.
- Keep both partners independently balanced.
- Stop before the return.

### Success signal

Couples walk side by side without lane collision or arm tension.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader drifts inward: “Stay in your lane.”
- Follower follows the leader sideways: “Continue forward unless the pathway changes.”
- Timing separates: “Keep the same pulse even though the paths differ.”

### Transition

Teach the return to closed hold.

## 36–42 minutes: Return to closed hold

### Purpose

Close the corridor safely and recover into familiar social flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate preparing the return before stepping across.
- Show the leader returning without cutting through the follower's step.
- Exit into Basic 3.
- Demonstrate the collision caused by returning too late.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the current step before accepting the return.
- Reinforce that the follower maintains the lane until the new direction is clear.
- Observe followers who turn early.

### Student task

- Dance the Leader's Saída.
- Walk briefly in the corridor.
- Prepare the return.
- Re-establish closed orientation.
- Continue with Basic 3.

### Success signal

Most couples return without stepping on each other's line or losing timing.

### Common errors and corrections

- Return begins too late: “Prepare before the final corridor step.”
- Leader crosses the follower path: “Close your lane without cutting theirs.”
- Follower rotates early: “Stay with the current pathway until it changes.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

With the new partner, practise two recovery choices.

## 42–48 minutes: Exit choices and recovery

### Purpose

Prevent sequence dependence by giving students two simple outcomes.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate return into Basic 3.
- Demonstrate return into Marca when space is limited.
- Ask leaders to choose the exit before the final corridor step.
- Reduce verbal prompting.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following both exits without prediction.
- Observe weight clarity during the return.
- Reinforce personal balance.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Enter the Leader's Saída.
- Maintain the corridor.
- Choose Basic 3 or Marca as the exit.

Followers:

- Maintain the current lane.
- Wait for the return direction.
- Complete each transfer without guessing.

### Success signal

Couples complete either exit without verbal coordination.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader chooses after movement begins: “Decide before closing the corridor.”
- Follower predicts the exit: “Finish the current pathway first.”
- Couple loses timing: “Return to Marca and rebuild the pulse.”

### Transition

Add floorcraft and selective use.

## 48–53 minutes: Floorcraft and corridor decisions

### Purpose

Teach when the Leader's Saída is appropriate on a social floor.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Explain that the movement needs lateral space as well as forward space.
- Ask leaders to scan both lanes before entering.
- Demonstrate cancelling the entry when the corridor is unavailable.
- Encourage simpler basics when space closes.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate remaining available when the entry is cancelled.
- Observe followers who begin the side pathway automatically.
- Reinforce compact walking.

### Student task

- Dance using previous vocabulary.
- Enter the Leader's Saída only when two compact lanes are available.
- Use it once, then choose a safe exit.
- Return to Marca if the corridor closes.

### Success signal

Couples use the movement selectively and preserve surrounding floor space.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before independent social dance.

### Transition

Explain the allowed vocabulary and movement-frequency constraint.

## 53–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Purpose

Integrate the Leader's Saída into social flow without repetition.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start a clear moderate track.
- Limit prompts to check both lanes, protect the follower path, prepare the return, and recover through Marca.
- Gradually stop speaking.
- Intervene only for repeated collision or arm force.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe anticipation, lane drift, and balance.
- Reinforce independent forward walking.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Student task

Dance continuously using Level 1 vocabulary.

Central rule:

> Use the Leader's Saída no more than once per musical phrase. Preserve two lanes and choose either Basic 3 or Marca as the exit.

### Success signal

Most couples use the Leader's Saída once, maintain the corridor, and return without collision.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 5 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap lane awareness, corridor position, independent walking, and return preparation.
- Explain that Week 5 keeps the corridor but redirects the follower into a new pathway.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce maintaining the prepared lane and avoiding anticipation.
- Ask students which part felt hardest: entry, corridor, or return.

### Student task

- Identify the follower lane and leader lane.
- Name the two exit choices.
- Ask final questions.

### Success signal

Students understand that the movement is a lane change, not a push or pull.

# Demonstration plan

For the Leader's Saída:

1. Show the full movement with music.
2. Repeat without music.
3. Show the leader pathway alone.
4. Show the follower pathway alone.
5. Demonstrate from the side so both lanes are visible.
6. Show the collision caused by crossing the follower lane.
7. Show the corrected compact corridor.
8. Return to music.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After corridor-position drill
2. After return to closed hold
3. Before independent social dance

Use one consistent direction. Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven. Students may switch roles or decline a rotation.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Entry from Basic 3
- Corridor position
- One or two corridor steps
- Return into Basic 3

## Progressing version

- Longer but compact corridor walk
- Choice between Basic 3 and Marca exits
- Adapt lane width to the partner
- Enter after an Estrela exit

## Challenge version

- Forward exit
- Side exit
- Rotational exit
- Change corridor length without changing timing
- Prepare the follower pathway used in Week 5

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Leader steps into follower line | “Protect the follower's lane before changing yours.” |
| Leader pushes follower sideways | “Create space with your feet, not the arms.” |
| Follower abandons the lane | “Continue on the pathway already prepared.” |
| Lanes become too wide | “Create only the corridor you need.” |
| Timing separates | “Different paths, same pulse.” |
| Return begins too late | “Prepare before the final corridor step.” |
| Couple loses timing after return | “Recover through Marca.” |

# Safety and consent

- Keep feet close to the floor.
- Do not create space by pushing or pulling the follower.
- Preserve both partners' personal balance.
- Keep corridor steps compact.
- Open hold remains valid.
- Students may decline a partner, role, or physical correction.
- Use the movement only when lateral and forward floor space are available.

# Teaching-role responsibilities

## Leader-role teacher

- Explain lane geometry and return preparation.
- Demonstrate the leader pathway.
- Observe corridor width, arm use, timing, and floorcraft.
- Manage rotation and class timing.

## Follower-role teacher

- Demonstrate the follower pathway.
- Explain lane stability, independent walking, and avoiding anticipation.
- Observe drift, balance, and timing during the return.
- Provide a second viewing angle.

The roles may be exchanged without changing the plan.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Students recognise two parallel lanes.
- Leaders enter beside rather than through the follower pathway.
- Followers maintain the prepared lane.
- Couples walk independently without arm tension.
- Most couples return into Basic 3 without collision.
- Students use Marca as recovery.
- Leaders scan both lateral and forward space before entering.

## After the class

Record:

- Percentage reaching the corridor without pushing
- Most common leader pathway error
- Most common follower anticipation error
- Whether corridor width remained compact
- Whether the Basic 3 return was clearer than the Marca exit
- Whether students used the movement too frequently
- Sections that exceeded their allocated time
- Content requiring review in Week 5

# Open questions and test points

- Is six minutes enough for the individual leader pathway?
- Do students need a physical floor marker to understand the lanes?
- Is the entry from Basic 3 clearer than the entry from Estrela?
- Should the Marca exit remain core or become a recovery only?
- Are two corridor steps enough before the return?
- Does the movement stay compact with a new partner?
- Is five minutes sufficient for independent practice?
