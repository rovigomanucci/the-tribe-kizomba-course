# Level 1, Week 6: Cha-cha, Rhythm and Lateral Flow

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 5, Follower's Saída

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to recognise the cha-cha rhythm, dance a compact lateral cha-cha with clear weight transfer, and enter and exit it through familiar Saída and Basic 3 vocabulary.

## Class purpose

Cha-cha is a core part of Level 1. It gives students a distinct rhythmic and lateral option, rather than more steps in a fixed sequence.

It comes after both Saídas because students now understand a prepared pathway, lateral movement, changing direction, and returning to closed flow. The class teaches the rhythm first, then partner connection, then one clear entry and exit. The goal is to make cha-cha usable in social dancing, not to teach a long cha-cha routine.

## Starting conditions

Students should have introductory control of:

- Marca, Basic 1, Basic 2, and Basic 3
- Natural walking and stopping
- Leader's and Follower's Saída
- Complete weight transfer
- Comfortable open or closed connection

Students who are not yet stable with a Saída remain with the core cha-cha from Marca or Basic 3.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Hear and mark the cha-cha rhythm.
- Complete each compact lateral transfer without rushing.
- Maintain personal balance and a responsive connection.
- Lead or follow a basic cha-cha without arm force or anticipation.
- Enter cha-cha from a prepared Saída pathway.
- Exit to Basic 3 or closed flow.
- Return to Marca when the rhythm or lead becomes unclear.
- Choose cha-cha only when the floor space supports lateral movement.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Cha-cha rhythm and weight changes
- Compact lateral cha-cha individually and with a partner
- Cha-cha from Marca or Basic 3
- One prepared entry from a Saída pathway
- Exit to Basic 3
- Recovery through Marca

### Previewed only

- A travelling cha-cha with modest range
- A delayed cha-cha entry
- A second Saída entry

### Excluded

- Fast or large travelling cha-cha
- Styling-led variations
- Multiple cha-cha patterns in sequence
- Arm-led lateral movement
- Footwork that loses the shared pulse
- Using cha-cha in crowded floor space

## Technical foundations

- Hear the rhythm before adding the shape.
- Finish each weight transfer before changing direction.
- Keep the action compact and underneath the body.
- The leader changes the shared pathway through the torso, not the hands.
- The follower completes the current transfer before responding.
- Lateral movement needs floor awareness.
- Recovery through Marca is always valid.

# Class sequence

## 0–6 minutes: Welcome and Saída recap

### Purpose

Reconnect the lateral pathway from Week 5 and establish the reason for cha-cha in the course.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- State the class promise.
- Recap a compact Leader's or Follower's Saída into Basic 3.
- Explain that cha-cha expands the rhythm and lateral vocabulary, rather than replacing the Saídas.
- Keep the recap short and familiar.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting for the prepared pathway.
- Observe complete transfer and connection range.
- Reinforce that the follower does not predict the next rhythm choice.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3.
- Complete one familiar Saída.
- Return to Basic 3 or Marca.

### Success signal

Most couples can establish a lateral pathway and return to familiar flow without pulling or stopping.

### Transition

Separate students and ask them to listen for the cha-cha rhythm before moving.

## 6–13 minutes: Cha-cha rhythm and weight transfer individually

### Purpose

Teach the rhythmic logic before adding partner connection or a pattern.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Play a clear musical example and mark the rhythm with the body.
- Demonstrate the compact weight changes slowly, then with music.
- Contrast a complete transfer with tapping or rushing through the action.
- Use brief stops to confirm the weighted foot.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from a second angle.
- Observe students who move ahead of the music.
- Reinforce small steps and relaxed knees.

### Student task

- Mark the rhythm in place.
- Complete each transfer fully.
- Stop on request and identify the standing foot.
- Restart on the music without increasing step size.

### Success signal

Most students can keep the rhythm and identify their weighted foot without teacher counting.

### Common errors and corrections

- Rushing the rhythm: “Let the music give you the next action.”
- Partial transfer: “Arrive on one foot before changing.”
- Bouncing upward: “Travel across the floor, not above it.”

### Transition

Keep the rhythm and add a small lateral direction.

## 13–20 minutes: Compact lateral cha-cha individually

### Purpose

Build the visible movement while preserving the rhythm and balance.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the core lateral version with music and without music.
- Show the allowed range in a crowded social setting.
- Compare a clear compact version with an oversized travelling version.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the same pathway from the opposite visual angle.
- Observe crossing, incomplete transfer, and steps outside personal balance.

### Student task

- Dance repeated lateral cha-cha cycles.
- Keep the feet under the body.
- Reduce the range while keeping the rhythm clear.
- Return to Marca after a deliberate reset.

### Success signal

Most students maintain rhythm and balance with compact lateral movement.

### Common errors and corrections

- Movement too wide: “Make the rhythm visible, not the distance.”
- Feet cross unintentionally: “Place each step under its own pathway.”
- Students lose the pulse when shrinking the movement: “Keep the transfer, reduce only the range.”

### Transition

Form partners and test connection before adding an entry.

## 20–28 minutes: Partner cha-cha from Marca

### Purpose

Transfer the rhythm into partner connection without changing the core footwork.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Marca into cha-cha and a clear return to Marca.
- Show the torso-led lateral proposal.
- Demonstrate the error of moving the follower through the arms.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining personal balance while responding to the changing rhythm.
- Reinforce waiting for the body direction before beginning the next transfer.
- Observe anticipation and tension.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Begin in Marca.
- Prepare the lateral direction through the body.
- Lead one or two cha-cha cycles.
- Return to Marca.

Followers:

- Complete each transfer.
- Respond to the pathway without guessing.
- Keep the connection responsive rather than rigid.

### Success signal

Couples dance cha-cha together without pulling, leaning, or verbal coordination.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once at the end of the section.

### Transition

Use the new partner to enter cha-cha from Basic 3.

## 28–35 minutes: Cha-cha from Basic 3 and back

### Purpose

Make the rhythm change part of an existing social flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3 into cha-cha, then cha-cha back into Basic 3.
- Show the preparation before the rhythm changes.
- Keep the transition clear and unhurried.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing Basic 3 before receiving the cha-cha entry.
- Observe followers who begin before the direction changes.

### Student task

- Dance a full Basic 3.
- Enter one or two compact cha-cha cycles.
- Exit to Basic 3.
- Recover through Marca if either partner loses the rhythm.

### Success signal

Most couples change rhythm and return to Basic 3 without a correction step or arm pull.

### Common errors and corrections

- Entry interrupts Basic 3: “Finish the current movement before changing the rhythm.”
- Exit is rushed: “Complete the last cha-cha transfer, then walk.”
- Follower guesses the change: “Stay with the current rhythm until the pathway changes.”

### Transition

Connect the rhythm change to the familiar Saída pathway.

## 35–43 minutes: Saída entry and clean exit

### Purpose

Give cha-cha a clear social-dance purpose after both Saídas.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate one prepared Saída into cha-cha.
- Show the smallest usable version first.
- Establish the exit line before returning to Basic 3 or closed flow.
- Do not add a second pattern until the core entry is stable.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the Saída pathway before responding to cha-cha.
- Reinforce personal axis and compact range.
- Observe early exits and anticipation.

### Student task

- Enter a familiar Saída.
- Complete the prepared lateral pathway.
- Dance one or two cha-cha cycles.
- Exit to Basic 3 or closed flow.
- Return to Marca if the entry becomes unclear.

### Success signal

Most couples use the Saída as an entry without pulling or extending the lateral range.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader starts cha-cha before the Saída finishes: “Arrive in the pathway first.”
- Couple drifts sideways: “Use one compact lane.”
- Follower anticipates the exit: “Complete the current rhythm before walking out.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

With a new partner, decide when the floor gives enough room for cha-cha.

## 43–50 minutes: Floorcraft and rhythm-choice drill

### Purpose

Teach selection rather than automatic use of the new movement.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Mark areas with limited lateral space.
- Ask leaders to choose cha-cha only when the lane is clear.
- Demonstrate returning to Basic 3 or Marca when space is restricted.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe whether followers remain responsive to the choice.
- Reinforce that simpler movement is the correct response to limited space.

### Student task

- Start with Basic 3.
- Assess the available lateral space.
- Choose cha-cha or remain in Basic 3.
- Use one Saída entry only when it is clear.
- Recover through Marca after an unclear lead.

### Success signal

Students choose cha-cha selectively and preserve the shared pulse in a changing floor environment.

### Transition

Remove teacher calling and move into short social practice.

## 50–58 minutes: Guided social dance

### Purpose

Turn the new rhythm into a choice inside continuous dancing.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start a clear moderate track.
- Limit prompts to finish the transfer, hear the rhythm, check the lane, and recover through Marca.
- Intervene only for arm force, oversized travel, or repeated loss of balance.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe anticipation, balance, and response to the new rhythm.
- Support students who need to remain with Basic 3.

### Student task

Dance continuously using familiar Level 1 vocabulary.

Central rule:

> Use cha-cha as a compact rhythmic choice. Enter only after a clear pathway and return to Marca or Basic 3 whenever the rhythm, connection, or floor space is unclear.

### Success signal

Most couples use one clear cha-cha choice and continue dancing without losing connection or floor awareness.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 7 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap rhythm recognition, compact range, Saída entry, Basic 3 exit, and recovery.
- Explain that Week 7 uses the same complete transfer and pathway awareness for Vírgula and Retrocesso.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce waiting for the rhythm and exit information.
- Ask students which transition felt least clear.

### Student task

- Name the safe entry and exit.
- Identify the recovery option.
- Ask final questions.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After partner cha-cha from Marca
2. After Saída entry and exit
3. Before guided social dance

Use one consistent direction. Students may switch roles or decline a rotation.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Cha-cha in place or with compact lateral range
- Entry from Marca or Basic 3
- Exit to Marca or Basic 3

## Progressing version

- One prepared Saída entry
- Two cha-cha cycles
- Exit to closed flow
- Choice according to floor space

## Challenge version

- Delayed entry after the Saída is complete
- Modest travelling version in an open lane
- Change the cha-cha range while preserving rhythm and connection

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Rushing the rhythm | “Let the music give you the next action.” |
| Partial transfer | “Arrive on one foot before changing.” |
| Movement too wide | “Make the rhythm visible, not the distance.” |
| Leader uses the arms | “Move the pathway before the hands.” |
| Follower anticipates | “Finish the current rhythm first.” |
| Cha-cha begins before the Saída finishes | “Arrive in the pathway first.” |
| Lateral travel ignores floor space | “Choose Basic 3 when the lane is not clear.” |

# Safety and consent

- Keep lateral travel compact.
- Both partners remain responsible for personal balance.
- Open hold remains valid.
- Do not pull a partner into the rhythm or movement range.
- Students may decline a partner, role, movement, or physical correction.
- Use cha-cha only when the lane is clear.

# Music plan

| Section | Music quality | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Rhythm recognition | Clear cha-cha phrase and steady pulse | Low |
| Individual lateral action | Spacious, predictable phrasing | Low |
| Partner and Saída work | Clear social Kizomba phrasing | Low to medium |
| Guided social dance | Moderate track with audible rhythmic texture | Medium |

# Open questions and test points

- Do students hear the rhythm before counting is added?
- Is one Saída entry enough for the first cha-cha class?
- Does the lateral range remain social-dance appropriate?
- Do students exit to Basic 3 without rushing?
- Which transition causes the most anticipation?
