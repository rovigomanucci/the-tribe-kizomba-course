# Level 1, Week 6: Vírgula and Retrocesso

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 5

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to redirect the partnership with a simple Vírgula or Retrocesso and recover immediately into social flow without twisting, pulling, or stopping the dance.

## Class purpose

Week 5 introduced follower redirection through a prepared side pathway. Week 6 develops that preparation into two compact redirection choices.

The objective is not to create a dramatic pivot. Students should learn to rotate only as much as the floor, balance, and communication allow, then select a clear exit and continue dancing.

## Starting conditions

Students should have introductory control of:

- Follower's Saída
- Quarter-turns
- Basic 3
- Controlled stops and restarts
- Stable closed connection
- Free-leg recognition
- Compact floorcraft

Students who are not yet stable in redirection remain with the core Vírgula into Basic 3.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Prepare a compact redirection from the follower pathway.
- Turn through the feet and body rather than a planted knee.
- Dance a simple Vírgula.
- Exit Vírgula into Basic 3.
- Dance a controlled Retrocesso.
- Choose between the two according to available space.
- Avoid arm-led rotation and follower anticipation.
- Recover through Marca or Basic 3 after unclear communication.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Quarter-turn and pivot-safety recap
- Redirection preparation from Follower's Saída
- Simple Vírgula
- Vírgula into Basic 3
- Controlled Retrocesso
- Recovery through Marca
- Exit selection according to floor space

### Previewed only

- Entry from Estrela
- 90-degree exit
- Delayed redirection
- Open-position exit

### Excluded

- Large pivots
- Forced 180-degree rotation as core material
- Fast repeated Vírgulas
- Deep counterbalance
- Full open-position sequences
- Arm-led turning

## Technical foundations

- Complete the current transfer before redirecting.
- Release the foot before changing orientation.
- Keep pivots small and close to the floor.
- Use the body and pathway rather than the arms to rotate.
- The follower waits for the exit direction instead of guessing.
- The leader selects the exit before momentum becomes large.
- Both partners maintain independent balance.
- Recovery is part of the movement, not evidence of failure.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Welcome and Week 5 recap

### Purpose

Reconnect Follower's Saída, free-leg recognition, and quarter-turn exits before introducing redirection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Introduce the class promise.
- Lead a short recap of Basic 3 and Follower's Saída.
- Reinforce preparing the pathway before moving the follower.
- Add one controlled quarter-turn exit.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting for the prepared pathway.
- Observe balance, free-leg clarity, and anticipation.
- Reinforce compact lateral steps.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3.
- Complete one Follower's Saída.
- Return through a small quarter-turn.
- Recover into Marca if the exit is unclear.

### Success signal

Most couples complete the follower pathway and a compact exit without pulling or twisting.

### Transition

Separate students and review safe pivot mechanics.

## 5–10 minutes: Safe pivot preparation individually

### Purpose

Prepare the feet and knees for small controlled redirection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a small step-and-turn action.
- Show how the standing foot releases before orientation changes.
- Contrast safe foot release with twisting over a planted knee.
- Keep the rotation below a quarter or half turn.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from another angle.
- Observe knee alignment, foot release, and balance.
- Reinforce small range and soft knees.

### Student task

- Transfer fully onto one foot.
- Release the foot before turning.
- Complete a small redirection.
- Stop in balance.
- Return to the original orientation.

### Success signal

Most students redirect without twisting, hopping, or losing the standing axis.

### Common errors and corrections

- Planted-knee twist: “Release the foot before turning.”
- Rotation too large: “Use only the direction you need.”
- Weight between both feet: “Finish one foot before redirecting.”

### Safety cue

No student should force rotation through a fixed knee or ankle.

### Transition

Connect the pivot action to a short change of direction.

## 10–16 minutes: Redirection pathway individually

### Purpose

Teach the movement logic without partner connection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a short forward pathway, compact redirection, and exit.
- Show the difference between Vírgula's rotational pathway and Retrocesso's return pathway.
- Avoid long counts and focus on direction.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower pathway from front and side.
- Observe crossing, over-rotation, and incomplete transfer.
- Reinforce stable personal axis.

### Student task

- Walk for a few steps.
- Complete a compact redirection.
- Exit into a new line.
- Repeat with smaller movement.
- Practise a short return pathway for Retrocesso.

### Success signal

Students can distinguish a rotational redirection from a controlled return without losing balance.

### Common errors and corrections

- Students spin instead of redirecting: “Choose the new line; do not chase the turn.”
- Feet cross unintentionally: “Keep each step underneath the pathway.”
- Exit happens before balance: “Arrive, then travel.”

### Transition

Form partners and practise preparation, stop, and recovery before the full figure.

## 16–22 minutes: Partner preparation and recovery drill

### Purpose

Teach clear preparation and immediate recovery before adding complexity.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Follower's Saída preparation.
- Add a small redirection without completing Vírgula.
- Return immediately to Marca.
- Show the incorrect arm-led turn.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain the follower experience of waiting through preparation.
- Demonstrate maintaining axis and completing the standing foot.
- Show how to recover when the redirection is unclear.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Prepare the follower pathway.
- Communicate a small direction change.
- Return to Marca when clarity is lost.

Followers:

- Complete the current transfer.
- Wait for the redirection.
- Keep personal balance.
- Recover into Marca without guessing.

### Success signal

Couples attempt a small redirection and recover without pulling, freezing, or verbal negotiation.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader turns only with the arms: “Move the pathway before the hands.”
- Follower anticipates the exit: “Stay with the current direction until it changes.”
- Couple stops between both feet: “Choose one standing foot and recover through Marca.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once after the drill.

### Transition

Keep the new partner and teach the simple Vírgula.

## 22–30 minutes: Simple Vírgula

### Purpose

Teach the core redirection from Follower's Saída.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the full simple Vírgula with music.
- Repeat slowly without music.
- Show the leader pathway and preparation.
- Keep the rotation compact.
- Pause before the exit.
- Observe arm use, foot release, and movement size.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower pathway.
- Explain completing each transfer through the rotation.
- Reinforce waiting for the exit.
- Observe planted-foot twisting and anticipation.

### Student task

- Begin in Basic 3.
- Enter Follower's Saída.
- Complete the simple Vírgula.
- Stop in balance before the exit.
- Return to Marca when necessary.

### Success signal

Most couples complete a compact Vírgula without arm-led rotation or knee twisting.

### Common errors and corrections

- Rotation becomes large: “Redirect only as far as the pathway needs.”
- Leader turns follower with the arms: “Lead the direction through the centre.”
- Follower predicts the exit: “Complete the rotation before choosing the next line.”
- Feet remain planted: “Release and replace the feet through the turn.”

### Transition

Add the core exit into Basic 3.

## 30–36 minutes: Vírgula into Basic 3

### Purpose

Connect the redirection back into familiar social flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate preparing the exit before the final rotational step.
- Establish the new walking line.
- Continue into Basic 3.
- Show the rushed exit that pulls the follower off balance.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate finishing the rotation before walking out.
- Reinforce full transfer into the new line.
- Observe followers who continue rotating.

### Student task

- Dance the simple Vírgula.
- Complete the redirection.
- Exit into Basic 3.
- Continue for at least one full Basic 3.
- Recover through Marca if timing is lost.

### Success signal

Most couples exit into Basic 3 without a correction step or arm pull.

### Common errors and corrections

- Exit begins too early: “Finish the redirection first.”
- Follower continues turning: “Receive the new walking line.”
- Couple accelerates: “Keep the pulse and reduce the travel.”

### Transition

Introduce Retrocesso as a smaller return option.

## 36–42 minutes: Controlled Retrocesso

### Purpose

Teach a compact reversal when a rotational pathway is unnecessary or unavailable.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Retrocesso with music.
- Repeat slowly and compare it with Vírgula.
- Emphasise controlled reversal rather than pulling backward.
- Keep travel small.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the current step before reversing.
- Reinforce balance and compact backward movement.
- Observe momentum and anticipation.

### Student task

- Begin from a familiar walking or follower-pathway position.
- Complete the current transfer.
- Reverse direction through Retrocesso.
- Continue into Basic 3 or Marca.

### Success signal

Couples reverse direction without pulling, stumbling, or taking oversized backward steps.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader pulls backward: “Prepare the return through the body.”
- Follower continues forward: “Finish the step and wait for the new direction.”
- Backward step too large: “Keep the return underneath the body.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

With the new partner, choose Vírgula or Retrocesso according to floor space.

## 42–48 minutes: Redirection and exit selection

### Purpose

Move students from fixed repetition into social-dance decision-making.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate choosing Vírgula when compact rotational space is available.
- Demonstrate choosing Retrocesso when a direct return is clearer.
- Ask leaders to decide before the redirection begins.
- Reduce verbal prompting.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without knowing which option will be selected.
- Observe anticipation and balance.
- Reinforce completing the current transfer.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Select Vírgula or Retrocesso according to space.
- Prepare the movement clearly.
- Exit into Basic 3 or recover through Marca.

Followers:

- Follow the current pathway.
- Avoid predicting the selected option.
- Maintain personal balance.

### Success signal

Couples complete either redirection without verbal coordination or loss of timing.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader changes decision mid-transfer: “Choose before redirecting.”
- Follower guesses: “Respond to the pathway that exists now.”
- Couple loses orientation: “Recover into Marca and rebuild the line.”

### Transition

Practise missed-lead recovery and selective use.

## 48–53 minutes: Missed-lead recovery and floorcraft

### Purpose

Make recovery an explicit social skill.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Intentionally demonstrate an unclear redirection and safe recovery.
- Reinforce that forcing the original idea is never required.
- Ask leaders to scan the floor before choosing a rotational or direct return.
- Encourage Marca and Basic 3 as neutral recoveries.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate staying balanced when the lead is unclear.
- Avoid completing a memorised pattern.
- Reinforce small steps and available posture.

### Student task

- Attempt one redirection.
- When communication is unclear, stop adding complexity.
- Recover immediately through Marca or Basic 3.
- Choose another movement only after balance and pulse return.

### Success signal

Couples recover from an unclear lead without force, blame, or stopping the dance completely.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before independent social dance.

### Transition

Explain the limited vocabulary and decision rule.

## 53–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Purpose

Integrate Vírgula and Retrocesso into Level 1 social flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start a clear moderate track.
- Limit prompts to finish the transfer, release the foot, choose the exit, check the space, and recover immediately.
- Gradually stop speaking.
- Intervene only for repeated arm force, planted-knee twisting, or unsafe travel.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe anticipation, balance, and foot release.
- Reinforce waiting for the exit direction.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Student task

Dance continuously using Level 1 vocabulary.

Central rule:

> Choose Vírgula or Retrocesso according to the available floor space. Recover immediately through Marca or Basic 3 after any missed lead.

### Success signal

Most couples use one compact redirection and return to social flow without forcing the movement.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 7 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap preparation, foot release, compact rotation, exit selection, and recovery.
- Explain that Week 7 will use the same balance and weight clarity to add pauses, taps, and delayed timing.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce waiting for the exit and maintaining personal balance.
- Ask students whether Vírgula, Retrocesso, or recovery felt hardest.

### Student task

- Identify when each redirection is useful.
- Name the two recovery options.
- Ask final questions.

### Success signal

Students understand that redirection quality and recovery matter more than completing a large turn.

# Demonstration plan

For Vírgula:

1. Show the complete movement with music.
2. Repeat without music.
3. Show the leader pathway.
4. Show the follower pathway.
5. Demonstrate safe foot release from the side.
6. Show the arm-led and planted-knee errors.
7. Show the corrected compact version.
8. Exit into Basic 3 with music.

For Retrocesso:

1. Show the movement with music.
2. Compare its pathway with Vírgula.
3. Demonstrate the unsafe pulled reversal.
4. Show the prepared compact return.
5. Recover into Marca.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After preparation and recovery drill
2. After Retrocesso
3. Before independent social dance

Use one consistent direction. Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven. Students may switch roles or decline a rotation.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Follower's Saída preparation
- Simple Vírgula
- Exit into Basic 3
- Marca recovery

## Progressing version

- Choose Vírgula or Retrocesso.
- Select the exit according to floor space.
- Enter from Follower's Saída or Estrela.
- Recover immediately after an unclear lead.

## Challenge version

- Use a 90-degree exit.
- Use a compact 180-degree exit only when balance and space allow.
- Delay the redirection for one pulse.
- Exit into an open-position preview.

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Twisting a planted knee | “Release the foot before changing orientation.” |
| Leader rotates only with the arms | “Move the pathway before the hands.” |
| Follower anticipates the exit | “Complete the current direction first.” |
| Rotation becomes too large | “Use only the turn the exit requires.” |
| Retrocesso becomes a pull | “Prepare the return through the body.” |
| Exit starts before balance | “Finish the redirection, then travel.” |
| Couple forces a missed lead | “Recover through Marca or Basic 3.” |

# Safety and consent

- Keep pivots small and controlled.
- Release unnecessary arm tension.
- Never force rotation through a planted knee or ankle.
- No pulling the follower backward during Retrocesso.
- Both partners remain responsible for personal balance.
- Open hold remains valid.
- Students may decline a partner, role, or physical correction.
- Use rotational exits only when the surrounding floor space is clear.

# Teaching-role responsibilities

## Leader-role teacher

- Explain preparation, exit selection, and floor-space decisions.
- Demonstrate the leader pathway for Vírgula and Retrocesso.
- Observe arm use, foot release, movement size, and recovery.
- Manage class timing and rotations.

## Follower-role teacher

- Demonstrate the follower pathways.
- Explain balance, waiting, and complete transfer through redirection.
- Observe anticipation, planted-foot twisting, and momentum.
- Provide a second viewing angle.

The roles may be exchanged without changing the plan.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Most students release the foot before redirecting.
- Couples complete a simple Vírgula without arm force.
- Most couples exit into Basic 3 without correction steps.
- Retrocesso remains compact and controlled.
- Followers avoid predicting the exit.
- Students recover immediately after unclear communication.
- Leaders choose the movement according to floor space.

## After the class

Record:

- Percentage completing Vírgula without planted-knee twisting
- Most common leader arm-use error
- Most common follower anticipation error
- Whether Retrocesso was distinct from Vírgula
- Whether the exit into Basic 3 remained stable
- Whether students recovered rather than forcing missed leads
- Sections that exceeded their allocated time
- Content requiring review in Week 7
