# Level 1, Week 1: Your First Kizomba Flow

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous experience: Open Night is helpful but not required

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to dance a short Kizomba flow using Marca, Basic 2, and Basic 1 while staying grounded, balanced, and on the pulse.

## Class purpose

Week 1 establishes the foundations that every later class depends on:

- Pulse recognition
- Complete weight transfer
- Personal balance
- Small controlled steps
- Comfortable partner connection
- Clear transitions between simple movements
- Recovery through Marca

The objective is not to teach a long sequence. Students should understand that they are making simple social-dance choices rather than memorising choreography.

Basic 2 is taught before Basic 1 to preserve continuity with Open Night, give beginners an immediately visible lateral movement, and then transition into the more compact and grounded feeling of Basic 1.

## Starting conditions

No previous Kizomba experience is required.

Students only need to be able to stand and walk comfortably. Pulse recognition, posture, and complete weight transfer are taught during this class rather than treated as prerequisites.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Recognise and move with the pulse.
- Transfer their full weight from foot to foot.
- Maintain an upright and relaxed posture.
- Dance Marca, Basic 2, and Basic 1.
- Change between the three movements without losing balance.
- Lead or follow through body movement rather than arm force.
- Return to Marca when uncertain.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Pulse
- Complete weight transfer
- Marca
- Basic 2
- Basic 1
- Open partner connection
- Transition from Marca into Basic 2
- Transition from Marca into Basic 1
- Return to Marca as the principal recovery

### Previewed only

- Walking as the subject of Week 2
- Small changes of direction
- Pausing and restarting

### Excluded

- Basic 3 as a fully taught movement
- Saídas
- Vírgula
- Extended rotations
- Styling
- Complex musical timing
- Long combinations

## Technical foundations

- Neutral standing posture
- Soft knees without collapsing
- Complete weight transfer
- Stable personal axis
- Small steps
- Relaxed upper body
- Light partner connection
- Pulse before movement vocabulary

# Class sequence

## 0–4 minutes: Welcome and class framing

### Purpose

Set expectations, reduce anxiety, and explain how the class connects to Open Night and the rest of Level 1.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Welcome the students.
- Explain the student-facing promise.
- Clarify that students will learn three simple choices rather than one long routine.
- Explain that returning to Marca is always a valid recovery.
- State that partners will rotate.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Stand where all students can see the follower role.
- Reinforce that no previous experience is required.
- Demonstrate a comfortable open connection beside the leader-role teacher.

### Student task

- Listen to the class goal and expectations.
- Choose whether to begin as a leader or follower.
- Prepare to move individually.

### Success signal

Students understand that mistakes are expected and that the class is focused on foundations rather than performance.

### Safety and consent cue

- Open hold remains valid throughout.
- Closer connection is not required.
- Students may decline any partner or connection distance.

### Transition

The leader-role teacher asks everyone to spread out with enough room to step comfortably in every direction.

## 4–10 minutes: Pulse, posture, and complete weight transfer

### Purpose

Establish the physical foundation for Marca, Basic 2, and Basic 1.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate neutral standing posture.
- Guide students through shifting weight from one foot to the other.
- Progress from weight shifts into stepping on the spot.
- Use brief freezes to test whether students know which foot holds their weight.
- Emphasise finishing one transfer before beginning the next.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Mirror the movement from a second viewing angle.
- Watch for students keeping weight between both feet.
- Quietly support students who are looking at their feet or losing balance.

### Student task

- Shift fully from foot to foot.
- Lift the free foot briefly to confirm that the standing leg holds the weight.
- Keep the knees relaxed and the upper body upright.
- Follow the musical pulse without travelling.

### Success signal

Most students can stop and identify which foot holds their weight without correcting themselves.

### Common errors and corrections

- Partial weight transfer: “Finish one foot before moving the other.”
- Looking at the feet: “Keep your eyes at partner or horizon level.”
- Bouncing vertically: “Transfer across the floor rather than up and down.”
- Steps becoming too large: “Keep the movement underneath your body.”

### Transition

Keep the same pulse and reduce the action into Marca.

## 10–14 minutes: Marca individually

### Purpose

Introduce the movement students will use to begin, reset, and recover throughout the course.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Marca with music.
- Show it again slowly without music.
- Explain that Marca is not a waiting position; it is an active dance choice.
- Demonstrate how to return to Marca after briefly losing the pulse.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower role.
- Show the difference between complete and incomplete weight transfer.
- Observe posture and balance.

### Student task

- Dance Marca individually.
- Keep the steps small.
- Stop on request, identify the weighted foot, and restart.
- Recover through Marca after an intentional mistake.

### Success signal

Most students can continue Marca without teacher counting and can restart after stopping.

### Common errors and corrections

- Tapping without transferring weight: “Step onto the foot; do not only touch the floor.”
- Rushing: “Let the pulse arrive before moving.”
- Stiff upper body: “Relax the shoulders and allow the body to settle over the standing leg.”

### Transition

Explain that Basic 2 is the first visible movement choice built from the same complete transfer.

## 14–20 minutes: Basic 2 individually

### Purpose

Introduce a clear lateral movement that is easy for beginners to see, reproduce, and connect to the Open Night experience.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 2 with music.
- Teach the essential lateral version.
- Show how it grows from Marca while preserving the same pulse and weight transfer.
- Keep counting brief and return quickly to music.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower role beside the leader-role teacher.
- Observe students who widen the movement excessively.
- Reinforce that both feet must complete their weight transfer.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 2 individually.
- Keep the lateral range controlled.
- Complete every transfer.
- Return to Marca when unsure.

### Success signal

Most students can dance repeated Basic 2 cycles and return to Marca without stopping.

### Common errors and corrections

- Steps becoming too wide: “Keep the movement smaller than you think you need.”
- Feet closing without weight transfer: “Arrive fully before changing direction.”
- Upper body moving after the feet: “Let the body travel with the standing foot.”

### Transition

Alternate Marca and Basic 2 on the leader-role teacher’s verbal cue.

## 20–26 minutes: Basic 1 individually

### Purpose

Shift from the visible lateral quality of Basic 2 into a more compact and grounded Kizomba feeling.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the complete Basic 1 with music.
- Break down the essential version.
- Compare its compact feeling with Basic 2.
- Emphasise that posture, pulse, and full transfer remain unchanged.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower role.
- Show the movement from the students’ opposite viewing angle if necessary.
- Observe whether students remain balanced and close to their own position.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 1 individually.
- Maintain small controlled steps.
- Complete every transfer.
- Alternate between Basic 1 and Marca.

### Success signal

Most students complete repeated Basic 1 cycles without losing balance or leaving weight between both feet.

### Common errors and corrections

- Moving too far: “Make the movement fit inside your own space.”
- Skipping a transfer: “Do not move the free foot until the standing foot is complete.”
- Becoming mechanical: “Keep the pulse continuous even when the shape changes.”

### Transition

Ask students to find a partner and establish open hold.

## 26–32 minutes: Partner connection and Marca

### Purpose

Introduce leading and following without adding a new movement.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate open hold.
- Explain that the leader proposes direction through body movement.
- Explain that the follower completes their own step and maintains personal balance.
- Demonstrate incorrect pulling and the corrected version.
- Lead Marca slowly with the follower-role teacher.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain the follower experience.
- Demonstrate the difference between following and anticipating.
- Show that the follower should not lean or give body weight to the leader.
- Support students having difficulty recognising the lead.

### Student task

- Establish a comfortable open hold.
- Dance Marca together.
- Maintain personal balance.
- Keep the arms responsive but not forceful.
- Stop and restart together.

### Success signal

Couples can dance Marca together without pulling, pushing, or leaning.

### Common errors and corrections

- Pulling with the arms: “Move your centre before moving your hands.”
- Follower anticipating: “Complete the current step before responding to the next one.”
- Both students leaning: “Stand independently and let the connection communicate rather than support.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once at the end of this section using one clearly indicated direction.

### Transition

Keep the new partner and add Basic 2.

## 32–39 minutes: Basic 2 with a partner

### Purpose

Apply Basic 2 through connection while preserving individual balance and controlled lateral space.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the entry from Marca into Basic 2.
- Demonstrate the return from Basic 2 to Marca.
- Show how oversized lateral movement affects the partner.
- Observe leaders’ posture, movement size, and use of the arms.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate how the follower completes each lateral transfer without guessing.
- Observe follower timing and balance.
- Support couples who lose the pulse during the transition.

### Student task

- Begin in Marca.
- Enter Basic 2.
- Repeat it at least once.
- Return to Marca.
- Adjust movement size to the available space and partner.

### Success signal

Most couples can move from Marca into Basic 2 and back without stopping, pulling, or travelling excessively.

### Common errors and corrections

- Movement too large: “Reduce the distance, not the clarity.”
- Leader uses only the hands: “Let your body establish the direction.”
- Follower steps before receiving direction: “Remain available rather than predicting.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

Repeat the same entry-and-recovery logic with Basic 1.

## 39–46 minutes: Basic 1 with a partner

### Purpose

Add the compact grounded movement after students have already established connection through Basic 2.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Marca into Basic 1.
- Demonstrate the exit back to Marca.
- Explain that the leader should complete a movement before changing.
- Observe leaders’ posture, movement size, and arm use.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower role without anticipation.
- Observe whether followers complete every transfer.
- Correct students who collapse their posture or lean into the connection.

### Student task

- Begin in Marca.
- Enter Basic 1.
- Repeat it.
- Return to Marca.
- Avoid changing movement halfway through a transfer.

### Success signal

Couples complete Basic 1 together without arm force, rushing, or losing balance.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader changes too quickly: “Repeat before changing.”
- Follower begins before the lead is clear: “Finish the current weight transfer first.”
- Connection becomes rigid: “Keep the hands available, not fixed.”

### Transition

The leader-role teacher explains that Marca, Basic 2, and Basic 1 are now choices rather than a fixed sequence.

## 46–52 minutes: Choosing between Marca, Basic 2, and Basic 1

### Purpose

Move students from copying the teachers into making simple social-dance decisions.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- First call the movement choices aloud.
- Gradually reduce the prompts.
- Ask leaders to choose one movement at a time.
- Reinforce returning to Marca whenever uncertain.
- Stop adding new information.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without knowing the next movement.
- Observe followers who begin guessing.
- Support students who lose confidence during transitions.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Choose Marca, Basic 2, or Basic 1.
- Complete the current movement before changing.
- Return to Marca when uncertain.

Followers:

- Complete every weight transfer.
- Avoid guessing.
- Maintain personal balance and comfortable connection.

### Success signal

Most couples can make several movement changes without stopping, pulling, or verbally coordinating.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader creates too many changes: “Use fewer choices and repeat them longer.”
- Couple loses the pulse: “Return to Marca before rebuilding the flow.”
- Follower guesses the next movement: “Respond to what is happening now.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before the final social-dance task.

### Transition

Explain that the teachers will now reduce their instructions and allow the students to dance.

## 52–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Purpose

Give students an authentic first Level 1 social-dance experience using a limited vocabulary.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start one clear, beginner-friendly track.
- Limit prompts to Marca, Basic 2, Basic 1, complete the transfer, and return to Marca.
- Gradually stop speaking.
- Circulate and offer short corrections.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Circulate on the opposite side of the room.
- Support followers with balance and anticipation.
- Join temporarily if role numbers are uneven.
- Observe which transition causes the most difficulty.

### Student task

Dance continuously using only:

- Marca
- Basic 2
- Basic 1

Change movement only after completing the current weight transfer. Return to Marca when uncertain.

### Success signal

Most couples dance continuously for several minutes without constant teacher counting.

### Teacher intervention rule

Do not stop the whole class for an individual error unless the same error is visible across much of the room.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and connection to Week 2

### Purpose

Consolidate the foundations and create a clear bridge into walking and Basic 3.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap pulse, full weight transfer, Marca, Basic 2, Basic 1, and recovery.
- Explain that Week 2 develops travelling, stopping, and restarting.
- Avoid teaching Basic 3 during the closing.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce personal balance and comfortable connection.
- Invite students to identify which movement felt most natural.

### Student task

- Reflect briefly on their strongest and weakest movement.
- Ask final questions.
- Understand what will carry into Week 2.

### Success signal

Students can name the three movement choices and understand that Marca is their recovery.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After partner connection and Marca
2. After partnered Basic 2
3. Before independent social dance

Use one consistent rotation direction throughout the class.

When role numbers are uneven:

- The leader- and follower-role teachers join temporarily.
- Use a short waiting position in the rotation.
- Avoid leaving the same person without a partner repeatedly.
- Allow role switching when appropriate.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Marca
- Basic 2
- Basic 1
- Return to Marca after each movement
- Teacher-led movement changes

## Progressing version

- Enter Basic 2 from Marca.
- Return to Marca.
- Enter Basic 1 from Marca.
- Change between the two basics after completing the transfer.
- Adjust movement size to a new partner.

## Challenge version

- Pause briefly and restart without losing balance.
- Change step size while preserving timing.
- Change movement at a musical phrase.
- Use fewer verbal cues.
- Dance with a new partner and adapt immediately.

Faster students should receive tighter timing, control, and communication constraints rather than additional figures.

# Music plan

| Section | Music quality | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Pulse and weight transfer | Very clear and steady pulse | Low |
| Marca and Basic 2 | Clear, accessible, and slightly energetic | Low to medium |
| Basic 1 | Slow, grounded, and uncluttered | Low to medium |
| Partner practice | Predictable pulse and phrasing | Medium |
| Independent social dance | Accessible social Kizomba track | Medium |

Specific songs can be added after the teaching structure is tested.

# Teaching-role responsibilities

## Leader-role teacher

- Lead the main instruction.
- Demonstrate the leader role.
- Control class timing and transitions.
- Observe leading, movement size, and arm use.
- Manage rotations and whole-room corrections.

## Follower-role teacher

- Demonstrate the follower role.
- Observe follower balance, anticipation, and weight transfer.
- Support students during partner work.
- Provide a second demonstration angle.
- Help identify students needing a simplified task.

The teaching roles may be exchanged without changing the structure of the plan.

# Safety and consent

- Open hold remains valid throughout.
- Connection distance is chosen by the students.
- No pulling, pushing, or leaning.
- Both partners remain responsible for their own balance.
- Students may decline any partner.
- Teachers do not force students into body positions.
- Movement remains small when floor space is limited.
- Corrections should be explained verbally before physical contact.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Most students recognise and maintain the pulse.
- Most students complete full weight transfers.
- Most students perform Marca, Basic 2, and Basic 1.
- Couples enter and exit the basics through Marca.
- Connection remains light and controlled.
- Students recover after mistakes without stopping the dance.
- Most couples dance independently for several minutes.

## After the class

Record:

- Number of attendees
- Number who attended Open Night
- Movement students found easiest
- Movement students found hardest
- Most common weight-transfer error
- Most common connection error
- Percentage of the room dancing without teacher counting
- Content that must be repeated in Week 2
- Whether the final social-dance section needs more time

# Open questions and test points

- Does Basic 2 first create faster engagement and clearer continuity from Open Night?
- Is six minutes enough for the final independent dance?
- Do students need more time in partner connection before Basic 2?
- Is Basic 1 understood more clearly after Basic 2?
- Are three partner rotations appropriate?
- Do students use Marca naturally as recovery?
- Does the class remain accessible to students who missed Open Night?
- Which demonstration angle works best for Basic 1?
- Which section regularly exceeds its allocated time?
