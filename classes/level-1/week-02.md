# Level 1, Week 2: The Kizomba Walk

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 1

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to travel with a partner, stop safely, change direction, and restart without pulling, rushing, or losing the pulse.

## Class purpose

Week 1 taught students to dance mostly within their own space. Week 2 introduces controlled travelling.

The objective is not to cover a large distance. Students should learn that Kizomba walking depends on:

- Clear weight transfer
- The torso communicating before the feet travel
- Small adaptable steps
- Awareness of the partner
- Awareness of surrounding couples
- Stopping before changing direction
- Returning to Marca when recovery is needed

Basic 3 acts as the bridge between dancing in place and travelling naturally.

## Starting conditions

Students should already have introductory experience with:

- Pulse
- Complete weight transfer
- Personal balance
- Marca
- Basic 1
- Basic 2
- Light partner connection
- Returning to Marca when uncertain

Students who missed Week 1 may participate, but they should receive the core version and extra support during the recap.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Maintain the pulse while travelling.
- Dance Basic 3 individually and with a partner.
- Walk forward and backward using small controlled steps.
- Communicate direction through the body rather than the arms.
- Stop with both partners balanced.
- Restart only after the new direction is clear.
- Avoid travelling into another couple’s space.
- Return to Marca when space or communication becomes unclear.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Week 1 recap through Marca, Basic 1, and Basic 2
- Basic 3
- Forward walking
- Backward walking
- Torso-led direction
- Controlled stop
- Restart in the same direction
- Stop before reversing direction
- Return to Marca as recovery
- Basic floorcraft awareness

### Previewed only

- Small quarter-turn after a stop
- Change of lane
- Estrela preparation

### Excluded

- Full Estrela
- Continuous circular travelling
- Saídas
- Large backward steps
- Fast direction changes
- Contratempo
- Complex musical pauses
- Extended choreography

## Technical foundations

- Complete the current weight transfer before travelling.
- The body communicates direction before the free foot moves.
- Steps remain underneath the body.
- Backward steps remain short.
- Both partners maintain personal balance.
- The follower responds rather than predicts.
- The leader checks the available space before travelling.
- A stop is a complete balanced action, not a hesitation.
- Restart only when both partners are stable.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Welcome and Week 1 recap

### Purpose

Reconnect students with the Week 1 foundations and identify anyone needing extra support before travelling.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Introduce the class promise.
- Explain that the class adds travelling, not speed.
- Lead a short recap of Marca, Basic 1, and Basic 2.
- Reinforce complete weight transfer and small steps.
- Explain that Marca remains the recovery option throughout the class.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower role during the recap.
- Observe whether students are transferring fully.
- Support students who missed Week 1.
- Reinforce personal balance and relaxed connection.

### Student task

- Dance Marca, Basic 1, and Basic 2 individually.
- Complete every weight transfer.
- Return to Marca after each movement.
- Identify which foot is weighted when the teachers call “stop.”

### Success signal

Most students can complete the three Week 1 movements and stop with clear weight placement.

### Common errors and corrections

- Incomplete transfer: “Finish the step before travelling.”
- Oversized movement: “Keep the step underneath your body.”
- Students rushing the recap: “Quality before distance.”

### Transition

Keep students dancing individually and introduce deliberate stopping.

## 5–10 minutes: Stop, balance, and restart individually

### Purpose

Teach stopping as a controlled movement before adding partner travel.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate walking on the spot and stopping fully.
- Show the difference between stopping on one balanced foot and freezing between both feet.
- Demonstrate restarting on the pulse.
- Introduce the sequence: move, complete, stop, breathe, restart.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from a second viewing angle.
- Observe students who continue shifting after the stop.
- Reinforce soft knees and upright posture.

### Student task

- Step on the pulse.
- Stop when cued.
- Identify the weighted foot.
- Hold balance for one or two pulses.
- Restart without hopping or transferring twice.

### Success signal

Most students can stop and restart without an additional adjustment step.

### Common errors and corrections

- Weight between both feet: “Choose one standing foot.”
- Restarting immediately: “Balance first, then move.”
- Free foot touching for support: “Let the standing leg finish the stop.”

### Transition

Use the same controlled weight transfer to introduce Basic 3.

## 10–16 minutes: Basic 3 individually

### Purpose

Introduce Basic 3 as the bridge between stationary basics and natural travelling.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3 with music.
- Show the essential version slowly.
- Connect it to the weight-transfer principles from Week 1.
- Demonstrate entry from Marca.
- Demonstrate exit back to Marca.
- Avoid presenting it as a long memorised count.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower role beside the leader-role teacher.
- Show the movement from the opposite viewing angle.
- Observe students skipping or tapping a step.
- Reinforce that every step receives the body weight.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3 individually.
- Enter from Marca.
- Repeat Basic 3.
- Stop in balance.
- Return to Marca.
- Keep the movement compact.

### Success signal

Most students complete Basic 3 repeatedly without tapping, rushing, or losing the pulse.

### Common errors and corrections

- Treating a step as a tap: “Every step receives your weight.”
- Travelling too far: “Basic 3 can stay almost underneath you.”
- Counting without hearing the pulse: “Let the music organise the steps.”

### Transition

Explain that Basic 3 can gradually become natural walking.

## 16–22 minutes: Forward and backward walking individually

### Purpose

Separate the technique of walking from the complexity of partner connection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate small forward steps.
- Demonstrate small backward steps.
- Show how the torso remains organised over the standing foot.
- Demonstrate stopping before changing direction.
- Set short travel lanes across the room.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the same pathway from another angle.
- Highlight safe backward step size.
- Observe students leaning ahead of or behind their feet.
- Reinforce looking forward rather than down.

### Student task

- Walk forward for several steps.
- Stop in balance.
- Walk backward with smaller steps.
- Stop before reversing direction.
- Keep the pulse and maintain awareness of surrounding students.

### Success signal

Most students travel and reverse direction without crossing their feet, leaning, or taking a correction step.

### Common errors and corrections

- Backward steps too large: “Place the foot close enough to remain balanced.”
- Upper body leaning: “Move the body with the step, not ahead of it.”
- Reversing without stopping: “Complete, stop, then choose the new direction.”
- Looking down: “Look through the room, not at your feet.”

### Safety cue

Students must check the path behind them before travelling backward.

### Transition

Form partners and teach direction through connection without significant travel.

## 22–28 minutes: Partner connection and directional communication

### Purpose

Teach how travel begins through the body rather than the arms.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a comfortable open connection.
- Show the leader preparing forward movement through posture and centre.
- Contrast torso-led direction with pushing through the hands.
- Demonstrate a clear stop.
- Keep the first movements extremely small.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain the follower experience of receiving forward, backward, and stopping information.
- Demonstrate waiting for direction instead of predicting.
- Show how the follower maintains personal balance.
- Observe followers moving before information is clear.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Communicate one small forward or backward step.
- Stop before changing direction.
- Keep the arms responsive rather than forceful.

Followers:

- Complete the current transfer.
- Wait for the direction to become clear.
- Keep personal balance.
- Avoid stepping automatically.

### Success signal

Couples communicate single steps and stops without pushing, pulling, or verbally coordinating.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader moves the arms first: “Move your centre before your hands.”
- Follower anticipates: “Wait until the direction is available.”
- Couple leans into connection: “Stand independently and communicate through contact.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once at the end of the section.

### Transition

Keep the new partner and introduce partnered Basic 3.

## 28–35 minutes: Basic 3 with a partner

### Purpose

Apply Basic 3 through connection before extending it into longer walks.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Marca into Basic 3.
- Repeat Basic 3 before changing.
- Demonstrate a controlled stop.
- Exit back to Marca.
- Observe whether leaders use the torso or arms.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing each transfer without anticipating.
- Observe follower balance and step size.
- Reinforce that the follower does not need to know the pattern in advance.

### Student task

- Begin in Marca.
- Enter Basic 3.
- Repeat it several times.
- Stop in balance.
- Return to Marca.
- Maintain light connection.

### Success signal

Most couples complete repeated Basic 3 cycles and stop without pulling or losing the pulse.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader changes direction halfway through a transfer: “Finish before redirecting.”
- Follower rushes the final step: “Give every step the same time.”
- Couple travels unintentionally: “Reduce the step until the pattern is controlled.”

### Transition

Extend the same communication into several forward walking steps.

## 35–42 minutes: Forward travel with a partner

### Purpose

Develop controlled travel while maintaining connection, pulse, and floorcraft.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the leader travelling forward while the follower travels backward.
- Keep the pathway short.
- Show how to scan the available floor before moving.
- Demonstrate stopping before reaching another couple.
- Observe leaders who walk through the follower’s space.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate safe backward walking.
- Show how the follower places each foot underneath their own body.
- Reinforce that the follower does not need to increase step size to match force.
- Observe balance and anticipation.

### Student task

- Travel for three to five small steps.
- Stop while space remains available.
- Return to Marca.
- Repeat only when the pathway is clear.

### Success signal

Couples travel a short distance and stop before entering another couple’s space.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader walks through follower space: “Create direction, then allow space for the follower’s step.”
- Follower reaches backward: “Place the foot close, then transfer.”
- Couple accelerates while travelling: “Keep the same pulse and reduce the distance.”
- Leader watches only the partner: “Include the room in your awareness.”

### Safety cue

Backward travel must remain small, controlled, and appropriate to the available floor.

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

With the new partner, practise stopping and reversing the travelling direction.

## 42–48 minutes: Stop and reverse direction

### Purpose

Teach direction change as a stop followed by a new decision rather than an abrupt reversal.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate forward travel.
- Stop completely.
- Confirm partner balance.
- Restart in the opposite direction.
- Show the unsafe version where direction changes before the follower finishes.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the stop before responding to the new direction.
- Explain that the free leg may change only after weight is clear.
- Observe followers carrying momentum through the stop.

### Student task

- Travel for a few steps.
- Stop.
- Hold the stop for one pulse.
- Restart in the opposite direction.
- Return to Marca if the direction becomes unclear.

### Success signal

Most couples reverse direction without stumbling, pulling, or adding corrective steps.

### Common errors and corrections

- Reversal happens too early: “Complete the stop before creating the next direction.”
- Leader pulls the follower back: “Invite the new direction through the centre.”
- Follower continues travelling: “Finish the standing foot and wait.”

### Transition

Add floorcraft decisions and allow leaders to choose whether to travel or remain in place.

## 48–53 minutes: Floorcraft and travel choices

### Purpose

Shift from practising a fixed exercise into making safe social-dance decisions.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Explain that travelling is optional.
- Ask leaders to scan the floor before moving.
- Encourage Marca, Basic 1, or Basic 2 when space is limited.
- Introduce the rule: stop before the space disappears.
- Reduce verbal instructions gradually.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate responding to both travelling and stationary choices.
- Observe followers who continue moving after the leader stops.
- Reinforce compact backward steps.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Choose between remaining in place and travelling.
- Travel only when a clear pathway exists.
- Stop early.
- Use Marca when space becomes limited.

Followers:

- Complete each transfer.
- Avoid predicting whether the couple will travel.
- Maintain personal balance during stops and restarts.

### Success signal

Couples change between stationary movements and walking without collisions or verbal negotiation.

### Common errors and corrections

- Travelling because it was practised: “Travel is a choice, not a requirement.”
- Stopping too late: “Protect the space before it disappears.”
- Couple loses the pulse while scanning: “Return to Marca and reorganise.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before the final social-dance task.

### Transition

Explain the limited vocabulary and floorcraft rule for independent practice.

## 53–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Purpose

Integrate Week 1 vocabulary with Basic 3, walking, stopping, and restarting.

### Allowed vocabulary

- Marca
- Basic 1
- Basic 2
- Basic 3
- Short forward walk
- Short backward walk
- Stop and restart

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start a clear beginner-friendly track.
- Remind leaders to travel only when space is available.
- Limit prompts to complete the transfer, check the space, stop early, and return to Marca.
- Gradually stop speaking.
- Correct only high-impact or repeated issues.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Circulate on the opposite side of the room.
- Observe balance during backward walking.
- Reinforce waiting through stops.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Student task

Dance continuously using the allowed vocabulary.

The central rule is:

> Travel only when space is available. Stop before reaching another couple, then restart when the direction and pulse are clear.

### Success signal

Most couples travel, stop, and restart independently without pulling, rushing, or colliding.

### Teacher intervention rule

Stop the whole room only when an unsafe travelling pattern or a widespread technical error is present.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and connection to Week 3

### Purpose

Consolidate walking and introduce the idea of circular navigation without teaching it fully.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap Basic 3, walking, stopping, restarting, and floorcraft.
- Reinforce that distance is less important than clarity.
- Explain that Week 3 introduces Estrela and controlled circular direction.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce small backward steps and personal balance.
- Invite students to identify whether stopping or restarting felt more difficult.

### Student task

- Reflect on the most difficult travelling direction.
- Identify Marca as the recovery option.
- Ask final questions.

### Success signal

Students understand that safe travel requires direction, space, balance, and a clear stop.

# Demonstration plan

For Basic 3 and walking:

1. Show the complete movement with music.
2. Repeat without music.
3. Show the leader-role pathway.
4. Show the follower-role pathway.
5. Demonstrate from the side so torso preparation is visible.
6. Show the common arm-led error.
7. Show the corrected torso-led version.
8. Return to music.

For stopping and reversing:

1. Demonstrate the unsafe immediate reversal.
2. Demonstrate travel, complete stop, and balanced restart.
3. Highlight the follower’s completed weight transfer.
4. Repeat with reduced movement size.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After directional communication
2. After partnered forward travel
3. Before independent social dance

Use one consistent rotation direction.

When roles are uneven:

- The leader- and follower-role teachers join temporarily.
- Students may switch roles.
- Use a brief waiting position rather than leaving one student repeatedly unpartnered.
- Students may decline any partner or rotation.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Marca
- Basic 3
- Three small travelling steps
- Controlled stop
- Return to Marca
- Restart in the same direction

## Progressing version

- Enter Basic 3 from Marca.
- Extend Basic 3 into natural walking.
- Stop and reverse direction.
- Choose between travelling and stationary movement.
- Adjust step size to the partner and floor space.

## Challenge version

- Vary travelling distance without changing the pulse.
- Delay the restart for one or two pulses.
- Use fewer verbal cues.
- Change direction only after a clear balanced stop.
- Add one small quarter-turn after the stop.
- Enter from Basic 2 and exit through Marca.

The quarter-turn remains a challenge variation, not core material.

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Stepping before the body communicates | “Let the centre establish the direction first.” |
| Leader walks through follower space | “Create space for the follower’s step.” |
| Follower anticipates | “Complete what is happening before preparing the next step.” |
| Backward steps are too large | “Place the foot closer and keep your balance.” |
| Couple accelerates while travelling | “Keep the pulse; reduce the distance.” |
| Direction changes before the stop | “Complete, balance, then redirect.” |
| Leader pulls through the arms | “Communicate through the body, not the hands.” |
| Couple travels into another couple | “Stop while the space is still available.” |
| Students lose the flow after stopping | “Return to Marca and restart from clarity.” |

# Safety and consent

- Open hold remains valid throughout.
- Backward steps must remain small.
- No pulling, pushing, or forced acceleration.
- Both partners remain responsible for personal balance.
- Leaders must scan the available floor before travelling.
- Students should stop before entering another couple’s space.
- Students may decline a partner, role, or connection position.
- Teachers explain physical corrections before making contact.
- Students with balance concerns may practise the core version without travelling.

# Teaching-role responsibilities

## Leader-role teacher

- Lead the explanation of direction and floorcraft.
- Demonstrate the leader pathway.
- Observe arm use, movement size, and available-space decisions.
- Manage travel lanes and rotation timing.
- Stop the room when an unsafe travel pattern appears.

## Follower-role teacher

- Demonstrate safe forward and backward follower movement.
- Explain waiting, balance, and weight completion.
- Observe anticipation, backward step size, and posture.
- Support students who missed Week 1.
- Provide a second viewing angle during walking demonstrations.

The roles may be exchanged without changing the teaching plan.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Most students maintain the pulse while travelling.
- Most students complete Basic 3 without tapping.
- Couples communicate direction without arm force.
- Followers use compact backward steps.
- Couples stop with both partners balanced.
- Couples reverse direction only after completing the stop.
- Leaders check the floor before travelling.
- Most couples complete the final task without collisions or constant counting.

## After the class

Record:

- Number of students requiring Week 1 support
- Whether Basic 3 or natural walking caused more difficulty
- Most common backward-walking error
- Most common direction-communication error
- Percentage stopping without a correction step
- Percentage checking the floor before travelling
- Whether three partner rotations were appropriate
- Whether five minutes of independent practice was sufficient
- Content requiring review in Week 3

# Open questions and test points

- Is six minutes enough to teach Basic 3 individually?
- Do students need longer partner-direction practice before travelling?
- Does Basic 3 help natural walking, or do students become too focused on the pattern?
- Should the reverse-direction section remain core material?
- Is the quarter-turn appropriate as a challenge, or should it be removed entirely until Week 3?
- Do students stop early enough when space becomes limited?
- Which demonstration angle makes torso-led direction easiest to understand?
- Is five minutes sufficient for independent social practice?
