# Level 1, Week 3: The Clockwise Flow

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 2

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to rotate smoothly with a partner using a compact clockwise Estrela and exit in a chosen direction without pulling, losing balance, or twisting the knees.

## Class purpose

Week 2 introduced linear travel. Week 3 redirects that travel around a shared centre.

Students should understand that circular movement is created through small walking steps, complete weight transfer, and both partners moving around a shared centre. It is not created by pulling with the arms, taking large sideways steps, or twisting over a planted foot.

## Starting conditions

Students should have introductory control of:

- Marca
- Basic 1 and Basic 2
- Basic 3
- Forward and backward walking
- Controlled stopping
- Torso-led direction
- Personal balance
- Basic floorcraft

Students who struggle with walking remain with the compact core version.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Turn safely through the feet rather than twisting a planted leg.
- Walk around a shared centre.
- Dance a compact clockwise Estrela.
- Maintain personal axis during rotation.
- Avoid pulling through the arms.
- Complete a quarter-turn exit.
- Exit into either a short walk or Marca.
- Use Estrela only when floor space allows.
- Return to Marca when orientation becomes unclear.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Week 2 walking recap
- Quarter-turn preparation
- Circular walking
- Shared-centre awareness
- Clockwise Estrela
- Entry from Basic 3
- Quarter-turn exit into walking
- Quarter-turn exit into Marca
- Floorcraft during rotation

### Previewed only

- Entry from Marca
- Pause before the exit
- Open corridor for Leader's Saída
- Variation in the number of Estrela steps

### Excluded

- Counter-clockwise Estrela
- Full pivots
- Continuous spinning
- Leader's Saída as a complete movement
- Large circular travel
- Fast rotational changes
- Fixed choreography combining all previous movements

## Technical foundations

- Complete each weight transfer before changing direction.
- Walk around the shared centre rather than spinning in place.
- Maintain personal balance and a light connection.
- Keep the rotational pathway compact.
- Turn through the feet and body together.
- Prepare the exit before redirecting the partnership.
- Scan the floor before entering the Estrela.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Welcome and Week 2 recap

### Purpose

Reconnect walking, stopping, and floorcraft before adding rotation.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Introduce the class promise.
- Explain that rotation is walking around a shared centre.
- Lead Marca, Basic 3, and short forward travel.
- Add controlled stops.
- Reinforce scanning the floor before moving.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate small backward walking.
- Observe weight transfer and backward step size.
- Support students who remain unstable after stopping.
- Reinforce personal balance.

### Student task

- Dance Marca and Basic 3 individually.
- Walk forward and backward.
- Stop in balance.
- Restart without an adjustment step.

### Success signal

Most students walk and stop with stable weight before rotation is introduced.

### Transition

Keep students individual and introduce safe quarter-turns.

## 5–11 minutes: Quarter-turn preparation individually

### Purpose

Teach the physical turning action before partner connection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a quarter-turn using small steps.
- Show how both feet and the body reorganise together.
- Contrast safe turning with twisting over a planted foot.
- Use four directions in the room as visual references.
- Ask students to finish each turn before continuing.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from a second angle.
- Observe knees, foot placement, and balance.
- Support students who rotate only the shoulders.
- Reinforce small movement size.

### Student task

- Step and complete a quarter-turn.
- Reorganise both feet beneath the body.
- Pause in balance.
- Repeat in the same direction.
- Avoid twisting on a fixed leg.

### Success signal

Most students complete quarter-turns without crossing, hopping, or twisting the standing knee.

### Common errors and corrections

- Upper body turns before the feet: “Let the feet and body arrive together.”
- Planted-foot twisting: “Release the foot before changing orientation.”
- Turn too large: “One quarter is enough.”
- Loss of balance: “Reduce the step and finish the standing foot.”

### Safety cue

Students should never force a turn through a planted knee.

### Transition

Connect consecutive quarter-turns into a compact circular walk.

## 11–16 minutes: Circular walking individually

### Purpose

Help students understand the geometry of Estrela without partner complexity.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Mark a small imaginary centre.
- Demonstrate walking around it with compact steps.
- Show that the body remains oriented toward the centre.
- Demonstrate a controlled exit into a straight walk.
- Avoid prescribing an exact number of steps.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the same circle from the opposite side.
- Observe students drifting away from the centre.
- Reinforce smooth continuous transfer.
- Correct students who cross their feet unintentionally.

### Student task

- Walk around a small imaginary centre.
- Keep the circle compact.
- Maintain the pulse.
- Exit after several steps into a chosen direction.
- Repeat without rushing.

### Success signal

Students maintain a recognisable compact circle and exit without losing balance.

### Common errors and corrections

- Circle expands: “Keep the centre close.”
- Feet cross: “Walk around the circle; do not wrap the legs.”
- Students spin instead of walk: “One complete step at a time.”
- Exit is abrupt: “Finish the circle before choosing the line.”

### Transition

Show how two people share the centre.

## 16–22 minutes: Shared-centre partner drill

### Purpose

Establish circular communication before teaching the full Estrela.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a light open connection.
- Establish a shared centre between the partners.
- Walk one or two small clockwise steps.
- Demonstrate the incorrect arm-led version.
- Emphasise that both partners move around the centre.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain the follower experience.
- Demonstrate maintaining personal axis.
- Show the difference between travelling around the centre and being pulled around it.
- Observe anticipation and crossed feet.

### Student task

- Establish comfortable connection.
- Walk one or two clockwise steps together.
- Stop in balance.
- Return to Marca.
- Keep the centre compact and stable.

### Success signal

Couples rotate slightly without pulling, leaning, or widening the circle.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader turns with the arms: “Move around the centre before moving the hands.”
- Follower is dragged sideways: “Keep your axis and walk your own path.”
- Couple collapses inward: “Share the centre; do not lean into it.”
- Circle becomes large: “Reduce the step, not the clarity.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once after the drill.

### Transition

Keep the new partner and build the clockwise Estrela.

## 22–30 minutes: Clockwise Estrela

### Purpose

Teach the core rotational movement through connected walking.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the complete clockwise Estrela with music.
- Repeat slowly without music.
- Show the leader pathway.
- Enter from Basic 3.
- Repeat the Estrela before teaching the exit.
- Observe arm use, step size, and floorcraft.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower pathway.
- Explain how the follower walks around the shared centre.
- Reinforce full weight transfer and personal axis.
- Observe unintended crossing or prediction.

### Student task

- Begin in Basic 3.
- Enter the clockwise Estrela.
- Continue for several compact steps.
- Stop together.
- Return to Marca if orientation becomes unclear.

### Success signal

Most couples complete a compact clockwise Estrela without pulling or losing the pulse.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader rotates the arms: “Lead the pathway, not the partner’s shoulders.”
- Follower crosses unintentionally: “Walk each step around the centre.”
- Couple travels across the room: “Keep the centre almost in one place.”
- Students count mechanically: “Follow the walking pulse, not a fixed number.”

### Transition

Teach the first quarter-turn exit into walking.

## 30–36 minutes: Quarter-turn exit into walking

### Purpose

Give students a controlled way to leave the circle and continue travelling.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the Estrela.
- Prepare the exit before changing direction.
- Complete a quarter-turn.
- Continue into a short closed walk.
- Show the unsafe abrupt exit.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the rotational step before following the new line.
- Explain how the follower recognises the exit.
- Observe momentum carrying followers past the intended direction.

### Student task

- Dance the Estrela.
- Complete the current circular step.
- Follow the quarter-turn exit.
- Continue for two or three walking steps.
- Stop safely.

### Success signal

Couples leave the circle on a clear line without stumbling or pulling.

### Common errors and corrections

- Exit begins halfway through a transfer: “Finish the circle step first.”
- Leader pulls the follower into the line: “Prepare the new direction through the centre.”
- Exit travels too far: “Choose the line; keep the steps small.”
- Follower continues rotating: “Complete the current step and receive the new direction.”

### Transition

Introduce Marca as the second exit choice.

## 36–42 minutes: Quarter-turn exit into Marca

### Purpose

Provide a compact recovery exit when travelling space is unavailable.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the Estrela.
- Complete the quarter-turn.
- Settle into Marca instead of travelling.
- Explain when this exit is useful.
- Compare both exits clearly.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate settling weight before Marca.
- Reinforce that stopping rotation does not mean freezing.
- Observe followers who continue travelling.

### Student task

- Enter Estrela from Basic 3.
- Choose the quarter-turn exit.
- Settle into Marca.
- Rebuild the pulse.
- Repeat with the other exit.

### Success signal

Couples can distinguish between exiting into walking and exiting into Marca.

### Common errors and corrections

- Students stop between both feet: “Arrive on one foot, then continue Marca.”
- Rotation continues after the exit: “Complete the new orientation.”
- Leaders choose too late: “Prepare the exit before the final step.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of this section.

### Transition

Ask students to practise choosing between both exits with the new partner.

## 42–48 minutes: Entry, repetition, and exit choices

### Purpose

Move from a fixed exercise into decision-making.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask leaders to enter from Basic 3.
- Encourage several Estrela steps before exiting.
- Ask leaders to choose walking or Marca according to space.
- Reduce verbal prompts.
- Observe whether exits remain clear with a new partner.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without knowing the chosen exit.
- Observe followers who guess.
- Reinforce complete transfer through the change of direction.
- Support couples who lose orientation.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Enter from Basic 3.
- Maintain a compact Estrela.
- Choose one of the two exits.
- Return to Marca when uncertain.

Followers:

- Walk around the centre.
- Avoid predicting the exit.
- Complete each weight transfer.
- Maintain personal balance.

### Success signal

Most couples enter, rotate, and choose either exit without verbal coordination.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader changes too frequently: “Give the movement time to become clear.”
- Follower predicts the exit: “Continue the current pathway until it changes.”
- Couple loses orientation: “Return to Marca and rebuild the direction.”

### Transition

Add explicit floorcraft decisions.

## 48–53 minutes: Circular floorcraft

### Purpose

Teach students to decide when Estrela is appropriate on a social floor.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Explain that Estrela requires a clear compact area.
- Ask leaders to scan before entering.
- Demonstrate abandoning the entry when space disappears.
- Encourage stationary basics when rotation is unsafe.
- Introduce the rule: rotate only when the space already exists.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate remaining available when the leader cancels the entry.
- Observe followers who begin rotating automatically.
- Reinforce compact movement.

### Student task

- Dance using Week 1 and Week 2 vocabulary.
- Enter Estrela only when space allows.
- Choose the exit according to the available direction.
- Return to Marca when the pathway closes.

### Success signal

Couples use Estrela selectively rather than repeating it continuously.

### Common errors and corrections

- Entering because it was practised: “The floor decides whether the movement is available.”
- Circle expands into another couple: “Protect the centre and reduce the step.”
- Leader scans too late: “Check before entering.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before the final social-dance task.

### Transition

Explain the vocabulary and decision rule for independent practice.

## 53–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Purpose

Integrate clockwise Estrela into the existing social vocabulary.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start a clear, moderate Kizomba track.
- Limit prompts to check the space, keep the centre compact, finish the step, choose the exit, and return to Marca.
- Gradually stop speaking.
- Intervene only for repeated or unsafe patterns.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe balance, crossing, and anticipation.
- Reinforce walking rather than spinning.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.
- Identify which exit causes more confusion.

### Student task

Dance continuously using:

- Marca
- Basic 1
- Basic 2
- Basic 3
- Short walks
- Stop and restart
- Clockwise Estrela
- Exit into walking
- Exit into Marca

Central rule:

> Use Estrela only when floor space allows. Choose the exit according to the available direction rather than repeating a fixed pattern.

### Success signal

Most couples use Estrela selectively, remain compact, and complete a clear exit.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 4 connection

### Purpose

Consolidate shared centre, compact rotation, and exits.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap shared centre, compact rotation, and quarter-turn exits.
- Reinforce safe turning through the feet.
- Explain that Week 4 uses the open corridor created by the exit to introduce Leader's Saída.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce personal axis and avoiding anticipation.
- Ask students which exit felt clearer.

### Student task

- Identify the two exit options.
- Reflect on whether entry, rotation, or exit was hardest.
- Ask final questions.

### Success signal

Students understand that Estrela is a circular walking choice governed by available space.

# Demonstration plan

For Estrela:

1. Show the complete movement with music.
2. Repeat without music.
3. Show the leader-role pathway.
4. Show the follower-role pathway.
5. Demonstrate from the side so the shared centre is visible.
6. Show the common arm-led error.
7. Show the corrected walking version.
8. Return to music.

For the exits:

1. Demonstrate the unsafe abrupt exit.
2. Demonstrate completing the circular step first.
3. Show the walking exit.
4. Show the Marca exit.
5. Repeat both without verbal counting.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After the shared-centre drill
2. After both exits have been taught
3. Before independent social dance

Use one consistent rotation direction. The teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven. Students may switch roles or decline a rotation.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Basic 3
- Compact clockwise Estrela
- Quarter-turn exit into Marca

## Progressing version

- Entry from Basic 3
- Several Estrela steps
- Choice between walking and Marca exits
- Adapt movement size to partner and space

## Challenge version

- Change the number of Estrela steps.
- Pause for one pulse before exiting.
- Exit on a different orientation.
- Enter from Marca.
- Create the open corridor that previews Leader's Saída.

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Turning with the arms | “Move around the centre before moving the hands.” |
| Follower crosses unintentionally | “Walk each step around the centre.” |
| Circle becomes too large | “Reduce the step and protect the centre.” |
| Planted-knee twisting | “Release the foot before changing orientation.” |
| Exit starts too early | “Finish the circular step first.” |
| Follower predicts the exit | “Continue the current pathway until it changes.” |
| Couple loses orientation | “Return to Marca and rebuild the direction.” |

# Safety and consent

- Keep the rotation compact.
- Turn through the feet and body rather than twisting a planted leg.
- Open hold remains valid.
- No pulling, pushing, or forced close connection.
- Both partners remain responsible for personal balance.
- Students may decline a partner, role, or physical correction.
- Rotate only when sufficient floor space is already available.

# Teaching-role responsibilities

## Leader-role teacher

- Explain shared-centre navigation and exit preparation.
- Demonstrate the leader pathway.
- Observe arm use, circle size, exit timing, and floorcraft.
- Manage class timing and rotations.

## Follower-role teacher

- Demonstrate the follower pathway.
- Explain personal axis, walking around the centre, and avoiding anticipation.
- Observe crossing, balance, and momentum through the exit.
- Provide a second viewing angle.

The roles may be exchanged without changing the plan.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Most students complete safe quarter-turns.
- Couples walk around a shared centre rather than spinning.
- Estrela remains compact.
- Leaders avoid arm-led rotation.
- Followers maintain personal axis and avoid anticipation.
- Couples complete both exits.
- Leaders use Estrela only when space allows.

## After the class

Record:

- Whether quarter-turn preparation was sufficient
- Percentage completing Estrela without pulling
- Most common follower foot-placement error
- Most common leader floorcraft error
- Which exit was clearer
- Whether students used Marca as recovery
- Whether five minutes of independent practice was sufficient
- Content requiring review in Week 4

- Does teaching two exits improve adaptability or create overload?
- Are students walking around the centre or spinning?
- Does the Estrela remain compact with a new partner?
- Which exit causes more anticipation?
- Is five minutes enough for independent practice?
