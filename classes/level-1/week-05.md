# Level 1, Week 5: Follower's Saída

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 4

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to guide the follower into a clear side pathway, maintain balance through a short lateral walk, and return to social flow without pulling, pushing, or memorising a fixed sequence.

## Class purpose

Week 4 taught the leader to create and use a corridor beside the follower. Week 5 keeps the same lane awareness but changes who travels through the side pathway.

The Follower's Saída should be taught through clear preparation, free-leg recognition, and available space. The follower should not be pushed into the pathway or expected to remember the sequence in advance.

## Starting conditions

Students should have introductory control of:

- Basic 3
- Leader's Saída
- Corridor awareness
- Complete weight transfer
- Follower free-leg recognition
- Small lateral steps
- Return to closed orientation

Students who struggle to identify the free leg remain with the one-step core pathway and direct return.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Identify the follower's weighted and free leg.
- Prepare a lateral pathway without arm force.
- Dance a compact Follower's Saída.
- Keep the leader out of the follower's corridor.
- Complete a short lateral walk.
- Return into Basic 3 without losing the pulse.
- Recover into Marca when the pathway becomes unclear.
- Avoid anticipating the entry or exit.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Weight and free-leg recognition
- Lateral pathway drill
- Follower's Saída from Basic 3
- One- to three-step lateral walk
- Simple return into Basic 3
- Recovery into Marca

### Previewed only

- Entry from a closed walk
- Different numbers of lateral steps
- Rotational exit
- Delayed return
- Cha-cha entry preparation

### Excluded

- Forced torso rotation
- Large lateral travel
- Fast follower redirections
- Full cha-cha pattern
- Full Vírgula
- Fixed choreography combining both Saídas
- Styling that interferes with balance

## Technical foundations

- The follower's free leg must be available before redirection.
- Preparation occurs through body direction and weight information, not the arms.
- The leader protects the follower's pathway.
- The follower remains responsible for personal balance.
- Lateral steps stay compact and close to the floor.
- Both partners complete the current transfer before returning.
- The leader scans lateral space before creating the pathway.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Welcome and Week 4 recap

### Purpose

Reconnect lane awareness and corridor walking before redirecting the follower.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Introduce the class promise.
- Lead a short recap of Basic 3 and Leader's Saída.
- Reinforce protecting the follower's lane.
- Highlight the difference between the leader changing lane and the follower changing pathway.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate maintaining the original lane during Leader's Saída.
- Observe complete weight transfer and anticipation.
- Reinforce independent balance.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3.
- Complete one Leader's Saída.
- Return to Basic 3.
- Identify which partner changed lane.

### Success signal

Most couples complete the Week 4 pathway without collision or arm force.

### Transition

Separate students and focus on weighted-foot recognition.

## 5–10 minutes: Weight and free-leg recognition

### Purpose

Establish the information required before the follower can be redirected safely.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Guide slow weight transfers.
- Call stops and ask students to identify the weighted and free foot.
- Demonstrate why a pathway cannot be created through a weighted leg.
- Connect the drill to the follower's lateral movement.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate lifting the free foot slightly without losing posture.
- Observe students who keep weight between both feet.
- Reinforce soft knees and complete arrival.

### Student task

- Transfer fully from foot to foot.
- Stop on request.
- Identify the weighted foot.
- Move only the free foot laterally.
- Return to a stable standing position.

### Success signal

Most students can identify and move the free leg without an adjustment transfer.

### Common errors and corrections

- Weight remains centred: “Finish one foot before moving the other.”
- Student moves the weighted leg: “Free the foot before creating the pathway.”
- Lateral step becomes large: “Place the foot close enough to remain balanced.”

### Transition

Use the free leg to create a short lateral walk.

## 10–16 minutes: Lateral walking individually

### Purpose

Teach the follower pathway without partner connection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate one compact lateral step followed by one or two walks.
- Show the difference between lateral travel and crossing the feet.
- Demonstrate a controlled stop and return to forward orientation.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower pathway from front and side views.
- Observe foot crossing, hip forcing, and loss of axis.
- Reinforce complete weight transfer.

### Student task

- Begin with clear weight placement.
- Move the free foot laterally.
- Continue for one or two compact steps.
- Stop in balance.
- Return to forward walking.

### Success signal

Students complete a short lateral pathway without crossing, twisting, or leaning.

### Common errors and corrections

- Feet cross unintentionally: “Walk the side pathway; do not wrap the legs.”
- Torso rotates excessively: “Let the pathway change without forcing the body.”
- Students rush the return: “Finish the lateral transfer before redirecting.”

### Transition

Form partners and practise communicating the free leg without travel.

## 16–22 minutes: Partner preparation drill

### Purpose

Teach clear preparation before the full Follower's Saída.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3 and a controlled stop.
- Show how to recognise the follower's weighted leg.
- Prepare the side pathway through body direction.
- Contrast this with pulling the follower's arm.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain the follower experience of waiting for a clear pathway.
- Demonstrate keeping personal balance.
- Show the error of stepping sideways automatically.
- Observe followers who anticipate.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Complete Basic 3.
- Identify the follower's free leg.
- Prepare one small lateral step.
- Stop before continuing.

Followers:

- Complete the current transfer.
- Keep personal balance.
- Move only when the pathway becomes clear.
- Avoid memorising the expected side.

### Success signal

Couples create one clear lateral follower step without pulling or prediction.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader redirects through the arms: “Prepare the pathway through the centre.”
- Follower steps before weight is clear: “Finish the standing foot and wait.”
- Leader blocks the corridor: “Create the space before inviting the step.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once at the end of the drill.

### Transition

Keep the new partner and add the complete core Follower's Saída.

## 22–30 minutes: Core Follower's Saída

### Purpose

Teach the essential movement from Basic 3 into a compact side pathway.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the complete movement with music.
- Repeat slowly without music.
- Show the leader pathway and corridor position.
- Enter from Basic 3.
- Pause after the follower enters the side pathway.
- Observe preparation, lane protection, and arm use.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower pathway.
- Explain receiving the redirection without anticipating.
- Reinforce personal axis and complete transfer.
- Observe forced torso rotation or oversized steps.

### Student task

- Begin in Basic 3.
- Complete the preparation.
- Guide or follow the follower's lateral entry.
- Take one controlled side step.
- Stop in balance before returning.

### Success signal

Most couples enter the Follower's Saída with a visible corridor and no arm-led redirection.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader blocks the follower path: “Move out of the corridor before inviting the step.”
- Follower predicts the entry: “Stay with Basic 3 until the pathway changes.”
- Connection becomes rigid: “Let weight and direction communicate the movement.”
- Follower loses axis: “Keep the step underneath your own body.”

### Transition

Extend the follower pathway into a short lateral walk.

## 30–36 minutes: Lateral walk

### Purpose

Develop controlled follower travel while the leader preserves the corridor.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate one to three compact lateral steps.
- Keep the leader outside the follower pathway.
- Show how excessive arm guidance disrupts balance.
- Observe leaders who follow into the corridor.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate independent lateral walking.
- Reinforce equal timing and complete transfer.
- Observe crossing, reaching, or forced hip movement.

### Student task

- Enter the Follower's Saída.
- Continue for one to three lateral steps.
- Maintain the pulse.
- Keep the follower pathway open.
- Stop before the return.

### Success signal

Couples complete a short lateral walk with the follower balanced and the corridor clear.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader follows into follower line: “Protect the corridor from outside it.”
- Follower reaches sideways: “Place the foot closer and transfer fully.”
- Timing separates: “Keep one shared pulse across different pathways.”

### Transition

Teach the simple return into Basic 3.

## 36–42 minutes: Simple return into Basic 3

### Purpose

Recover from the side pathway without forcing rotation or losing timing.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate preparing the return before the final lateral step.
- Re-establish the shared forward line.
- Continue into Basic 3.
- Show the collision caused by returning too late.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the current lateral transfer before accepting the return.
- Reinforce that the follower does not rotate until the new pathway is clear.
- Observe followers who turn early.

### Student task

- Dance the Follower's Saída.
- Complete the lateral walk.
- Prepare the return.
- Re-establish closed orientation.
- Continue into Basic 3.

### Success signal

Most couples return into Basic 3 without pulling, twisting, or adding correction steps.

### Common errors and corrections

- Return begins halfway through a transfer: “Finish the side step first.”
- Leader pulls the follower back: “Open the return pathway before requesting it.”
- Follower rotates early: “Stay with the current direction until it changes.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

With the new partner, compare Basic 3 and Marca recoveries.

## 42–48 minutes: Return choices and adaptability

### Purpose

Prevent sequence dependence and test the movement with a new partner.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate return into Basic 3.
- Demonstrate settling into Marca when the return is unclear or space closes.
- Ask leaders to vary the number of lateral steps within a small range.
- Reduce verbal prompting.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without knowing the number of steps or exit.
- Observe anticipation and weight clarity.
- Reinforce personal balance.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Prepare the Follower's Saída.
- Choose one, two, or three lateral steps.
- Return into Basic 3 or recover through Marca.

Followers:

- Complete each lateral transfer.
- Avoid counting or predicting the return.
- Maintain personal balance.

### Success signal

Couples adapt the pathway length and exit without verbal coordination.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader changes the number too late: “Decide before redirecting.”
- Follower counts the pattern: “Respond to the current step, not an expected sequence.”
- Couple loses timing: “Use Marca to recover before continuing.”

### Transition

Add selective use and floorcraft.

## 48–53 minutes: Floorcraft and pathway decisions

### Purpose

Teach when the Follower's Saída is appropriate on a social floor.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Explain that the side pathway needs clear lateral space.
- Ask leaders to scan before preparation.
- Demonstrate cancelling the entry when the corridor closes.
- Encourage simpler movement choices when space is limited.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate remaining in Basic 3 when the entry is cancelled.
- Observe followers who step laterally automatically.
- Reinforce compact movement.

### Student task

- Dance using previous Level 1 vocabulary.
- Use the Follower's Saída only after clear preparation and available space.
- Return safely after one short pathway.
- Recover through Marca when necessary.

### Success signal

Couples use the movement selectively and avoid entering occupied space.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once before independent social dance.

### Transition

Explain the vocabulary and preparation rule.

## 53–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Purpose

Integrate the Follower's Saída into social flow without memorised sequencing.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start a clear moderate track.
- Limit prompts to identify the free leg, prepare the pathway, protect the corridor, finish the transfer, and recover through Marca.
- Gradually stop speaking.
- Intervene only for repeated pulling, blocked corridors, or unsafe range.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe anticipation, lateral step size, and balance.
- Reinforce waiting for the pathway.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Student task

Dance continuously using Level 1 vocabulary.

Central rule:

> Use the Follower's Saída only after a clear preparation. Followers wait for the pathway rather than memorising the sequence.

### Success signal

Most couples complete one compact Follower's Saída and return without pulling, prediction, or loss of balance.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 6 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap free-leg recognition, pathway preparation, corridor protection, and simple return.
- Explain that Week 6 will use the Follower's Saída and lateral pathway to introduce cha-cha rhythm, entry, and exit choices.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce personal balance and waiting for clear direction.
- Ask students whether preparation, lateral walking, or return felt hardest.

### Student task

- Identify the follower's weighted and free leg.
- Name the recovery options.
- Ask final questions.

### Success signal

Students understand that the movement begins with weight clarity and preparation, not arm force.

# Demonstration plan

For the Follower's Saída:

1. Show the complete movement with music.
2. Repeat without music.
3. Show the leader pathway alone.
4. Show the follower pathway alone.
5. Demonstrate from the side so the corridor is visible.
6. Show the arm-led and blocked-corridor errors.
7. Show the corrected preparation and pathway.
8. Return to music.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After the partner preparation drill
2. After the simple return into Basic 3
3. Before independent social dance

Use one consistent direction. Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven. Students may switch roles or decline a rotation.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Entry from Basic 3
- One follower lateral step
- Simple return into Basic 3

## Progressing version

- One to three lateral steps
- Choice between Basic 3 and Marca recovery
- Adjust pathway size to the partner and floor
- Entry from a short closed walk

## Challenge version

- Change the number of lateral walks.
- Delay the return for one pulse.
- Add a compact rotational exit.
- Prepare the Saída entry used for cha-cha in Week 6.

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Arm-led redirection | “Prepare the pathway through the centre.” |
| Follower steps before weight is available | “Finish the standing foot and wait.” |
| Leader blocks the corridor | “Create the space before inviting the step.” |
| Follower crosses unintentionally | “Walk the side pathway; do not wrap the legs.” |
| Forced torso rotation | “Let the pathway change without forcing the body.” |
| Return begins too early | “Finish the lateral transfer first.” |
| Couple loses timing | “Recover through Marca.” |

# Safety and consent

- No forced torso rotation.
- Do not push or pull the follower into the pathway.
- Keep lateral steps compact and close to the floor.
- The follower remains responsible for personal balance.
- Open hold remains valid.
- Students may decline a partner, role, or physical correction.
- Use the movement only when the lateral corridor is clear.

# Teaching-role responsibilities

## Leader-role teacher

- Explain weight recognition, pathway preparation, and corridor protection.
- Demonstrate the leader pathway.
- Observe arm use, free-leg timing, return preparation, and floorcraft.
- Manage class timing and rotations.

## Follower-role teacher

- Demonstrate the follower pathway.
- Explain waiting, personal balance, and lateral weight transfer.
- Observe anticipation, crossing, torso rotation, and step size.
- Provide a second viewing angle.

The roles may be exchanged without changing the plan.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Most students identify the follower's free leg.
- Leaders prepare the pathway without arm force.
- Leaders keep the corridor clear.
- Followers wait rather than anticipate.
- Lateral walking remains compact.
- Most couples return into Basic 3 without correction steps.
- Students use Marca to recover after unclear communication.

## After the class

Record:

- Percentage identifying the free leg correctly
- Most common preparation error
- Most common follower anticipation error
- Whether the corridor remained clear
- Whether one to three lateral steps created overload
- Whether the Basic 3 return remained stable
- Sections that exceeded their allocated time
- Content requiring review in Week 6
