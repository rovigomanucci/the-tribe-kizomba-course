# Level 1, Week 7: Vírgula and Retrocesso

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 6, Cha-cha

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to redirect the partnership with a simple Vírgula or Retrocesso and recover into social flow without twisting, pulling, or stopping the dance.

## Class purpose

Week 6 added a rhythm change and reinforced complete transfer, pathway clarity, and return to Basic 3. Week 7 uses those same skills for two compact redirection choices.

The objective is not a dramatic pivot. Students rotate only as much as the floor, balance, and communication allow, then choose a clear exit. A missed lead ends in Marca or Basic 3, not force.

## Starting conditions

Students should have introductory control of:

- Follower's Saída and quarter-turn exit
- Basic 3 and controlled walking
- Cha-cha entry and exit vocabulary
- Complete transfer and free-leg recognition
- Compact floorcraft

Students who are not yet stable in redirection remain with the core Vírgula into Basic 3.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Prepare a compact redirection from the follower pathway.
- Release the foot before changing orientation.
- Dance a simple Vírgula and exit to Basic 3.
- Dance a controlled Retrocesso.
- Choose between the two according to available space.
- Avoid arm-led rotation and anticipation.
- Recover through Marca or Basic 3 after unclear communication.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Pivot-safety recap
- Redirection preparation from Follower's Saída
- Simple Vírgula
- Vírgula into Basic 3
- Controlled Retrocesso
- Recovery through Marca
- Exit selection according to floor space

### Previewed only

- Entry from Estrela or cha-cha
- 90-degree exit
- Delayed redirection

### Excluded

- Large pivots
- Forced 180-degree rotation as core material
- Fast repeated Vírgulas
- Deep counterbalance
- Arm-led turning

## Technical foundations

- Complete the current transfer before redirecting.
- Release the foot before changing orientation.
- Keep pivots small and close to the floor.
- Use the body and pathway rather than the arms to rotate.
- The follower waits for the exit direction.
- Recovery is part of the movement.

# Class sequence

## 0–6 minutes: Welcome and Week 6 recap

### Purpose

Reconnect complete transfer, Basic 3 exits, and compact range before redirection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap Basic 3 into one compact cha-cha choice and back.
- Reinforce completing the last transfer before changing direction.
- Introduce redirection as a pathway decision, not a spin.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting for the exit information.
- Observe balance, anticipation, and foot release.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3.
- Add one compact cha-cha choice.
- Return to Basic 3 or Marca.

### Success signal

Most couples complete the rhythm change and return without pulling or rushing.

### Transition

Separate students and review safe pivot mechanics.

## 6–13 minutes: Safe pivot preparation individually

### Purpose

Prepare the feet and knees for small controlled redirection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a small step-and-turn action.
- Show foot release before orientation changes.
- Contrast safe release with twisting over a planted knee.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from a second angle.
- Observe knee alignment, balance, and movement size.

### Student task

- Transfer fully onto one foot.
- Release the foot before turning.
- Complete a small redirection.
- Stop in balance and return to the original line.

### Success signal

Most students redirect without twisting, hopping, or losing axis.

### Common errors and corrections

- Planted-knee twist: “Release the foot before turning.”
- Rotation too large: “Use only the direction you need.”
- Weight between both feet: “Finish one foot before redirecting.”

### Transition

Connect the safe pivot action to a short change of direction.

## 13–20 minutes: Redirection pathway individually

### Purpose

Teach the difference between a rotational redirection and a controlled return without partner connection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a short forward pathway, compact redirection, and exit.
- Compare Vírgula with Retrocesso.
- Keep the range below a quarter or half turn.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower pathway from front and side.
- Observe crossing, over-rotation, and incomplete transfer.

### Student task

- Walk for a few steps.
- Redirect compactly and choose a new line.
- Practise a short return pathway for Retrocesso.
- Repeat at a smaller range.

### Success signal

Students distinguish the two pathways without losing balance.

### Transition

Form partners and practise preparation, stop, and recovery.

## 20–27 minutes: Partner preparation and recovery drill

### Purpose

Teach clear preparation and immediate recovery before the full figures.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Follower's Saída preparation.
- Add a small direction change without completing Vírgula.
- Return immediately to Marca.
- Show the incorrect arm-led turn.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain completing the standing foot before the new direction.
- Demonstrate personal axis and available posture.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Prepare the pathway.
- Communicate a small direction change.
- Return to Marca when clarity is lost.

Followers:

- Complete the current transfer.
- Wait for the new direction.
- Recover without guessing.

### Success signal

Couples attempt a small redirection and recover without pulling, freezing, or verbal negotiation.

### Partner rotation

Rotate once after the drill.

### Transition

Keep the new partner and teach the simple Vírgula.

## 27–35 minutes: Simple Vírgula and Basic 3 exit

### Purpose

Teach the core redirection from Follower's Saída and return it to familiar social flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the complete simple Vírgula with music, then slowly.
- Show the leader pathway and compact rotation.
- Establish the new walking line before Basic 3.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing each transfer through the rotation.
- Reinforce waiting for the exit.
- Observe planted-foot twisting and anticipation.

### Student task

- Begin in Basic 3.
- Enter Follower's Saída.
- Complete simple Vírgula.
- Exit to Basic 3.
- Recover through Marca when needed.

### Success signal

Most couples exit into Basic 3 without correction steps, arm force, or knee twisting.

### Common errors and corrections

- Rotation becomes large: “Redirect only as far as the pathway needs.”
- Leader turns with the arms: “Move the pathway before the hands.”
- Exit starts early: “Finish the redirection first.”

### Transition

Introduce Retrocesso as the smaller return option.

## 35–42 minutes: Controlled Retrocesso

### Purpose

Teach a compact reversal when a rotational pathway is unnecessary or unavailable.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Retrocesso with music, then compare it with Vírgula.
- Emphasise a prepared reversal rather than a pull backward.
- Keep travel small.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate completing the current step before reversing.
- Reinforce compact backward movement and personal balance.

### Student task

- Begin from familiar walking or follower pathway.
- Complete the current transfer.
- Reverse direction through Retrocesso.
- Continue into Basic 3 or Marca.

### Success signal

Couples reverse direction without pulling, stumbling, or oversized backward steps.

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

With the new partner, choose Vírgula or Retrocesso according to floor space.

## 42–50 minutes: Redirection and exit selection

### Purpose

Move students from fixed repetition into social-dance decision-making.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate choosing Vírgula when compact rotational space is available.
- Demonstrate choosing Retrocesso when a direct return is clearer.
- Ask leaders to decide before the redirection begins.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without knowing the selected option.
- Observe anticipation and balance.

### Student task

- Choose one option according to space.
- Prepare it clearly.
- Exit to Basic 3 or recover through Marca.
- Reduce the movement when the floor is busy.

### Success signal

Couples complete either redirection without verbal coordination or loss of timing.

### Transition

Practise one missed-lead recovery before independent social dance.

## 50–58 minutes: Guided social dance and recovery

### Purpose

Integrate redirection as a selective choice inside Level 1 social flow.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start a clear moderate track.
- Prompt only to finish the transfer, release the foot, check the space, and recover.
- Intervene only for arm force, planted-knee twisting, or unsafe travel.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe anticipation, foot release, and balance.
- Reinforce returning to Marca or Basic 3 after unclear communication.

### Student task

Dance continuously using Level 1 vocabulary.

Central rule:

> Choose Vírgula or Retrocesso according to the available floor space. Recover immediately through Marca or Basic 3 after any missed lead.

### Success signal

Most couples use one compact redirection and return to social flow without force.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 8 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap preparation, foot release, compact rotation, exit selection, and recovery.
- Explain that Week 8 adds pauses and taps, then integrates the complete Level 1 vocabulary in social flow.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce waiting for the exit and maintaining personal balance.

### Student task

- Identify when each redirection is useful.
- Name the two recovery options.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Follower's Saída preparation
- Simple Vírgula
- Exit to Basic 3
- Marca recovery

## Progressing version

- Choose Vírgula or Retrocesso.
- Select the exit according to floor space.
- Enter from Follower's Saída or cha-cha.

## Challenge version

- Use a compact 90-degree exit.
- Delay the redirection for one pulse.
- Use a cha-cha entry only when it remains clear and compact.

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Twisting a planted knee | “Release the foot before changing orientation.” |
| Leader rotates only with the arms | “Move the pathway before the hands.” |
| Follower anticipates the exit | “Complete the current direction first.” |
| Rotation becomes too large | “Use only the turn the exit requires.” |
| Retrocesso becomes a pull | “Prepare the return through the body.” |
| Exit starts before balance | “Finish the redirection, then travel.” |

# Safety and consent

- Keep pivots small and controlled.
- Never force rotation through a planted knee or ankle.
- No pulling a partner backward during Retrocesso.
- Both partners remain responsible for personal balance.
- Open hold remains valid.
- Students may decline a partner, role, movement, or physical correction.
- Use rotational exits only when surrounding floor space is clear.
