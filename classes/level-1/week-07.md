# Level 1, Week 7: Pause, Tap, and Play

Status: Draft — Test in class

## Class details

- Duration: 60 minutes
- Teaching roles: Leader and Follower
- Audience: Level 1 beginners
- Previous class: Level 1, Week 6

## Student-facing promise

By the end of the class, students will be able to use simple pauses, taps, and delayed exits without losing balance, connection, or the shared pulse.

## Class purpose

Week 6 taught students to redirect and recover. Week 7 reduces movement and increases listening.

Students should understand that a pause is an active shared choice, a tap does not receive body weight, and a delayed exit must be prepared clearly. Musical play should come from balance and communication rather than added complexity.

## Starting conditions

Students should have introductory control of:

- Basic 3
- Stable stop and restart
- Clear weight identification
- Marca
- Compact redirection
- Personal balance
- Recovery through Marca or Basic 3

Students who cannot reliably distinguish a weighted step from a tap remain with the pause-and-restart core version.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the class, students should be able to:

- Pause while remaining balanced and connected.
- Identify the weighted and free foot during a pause.
- Tap without transferring weight.
- Restart only after clear preparation.
- Dance Basic 3 with a pause.
- Use a tap-and-go action.
- Delay an exit without pulling or guessing.
- Use two timing choices within one movement family.
- Avoid adding body texture before balance is established.

## Movement scope

### Fully taught

- Balanced pause
- Stop and restart on the pulse
- Tap without weight transfer
- Basic 3 with pause
- Tap and go
- Delayed exit
- Continue walking after the delay
- Recovery through Marca

### Previewed only

- Slow timing
- Quick timing
- Small optional Tarraxinha texture
- Vírgula after a delayed exit

### Excluded

- Forced hip movement
- Extended Tarraxinha sequences
- Complex syncopation
- Multiple quick timing changes as core material
- Body isolations without stable balance
- Long musical choreography

## Technical foundations

- A pause finishes on one clearly weighted foot.
- A tap touches the floor without transferring body weight.
- Both partners remain independently balanced during a delay.
- The restart is prepared before movement resumes.
- The follower waits for direction rather than counting the delay.
- The leader avoids holding the partner in place through the arms.
- Texture remains optional, small, and subordinate to balance.

# Class sequence

## 0–5 minutes: Welcome and Week 6 recap

### Purpose

Reconnect stopping, redirection, and recovery before introducing timing play.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Introduce the class promise.
- Lead a short recap of Basic 3, one compact redirection, and Marca recovery.
- Reinforce finishing each transfer before changing direction.
- Explain that Week 7 adds less movement, not more figures.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting through redirection and recovery.
- Observe balance and weight clarity.
- Reinforce personal axis.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3.
- Complete one compact redirection.
- Recover into Marca.
- Stop with clear weight placement.

### Success signal

Most couples stop and recover without an adjustment step or arm force.

### Transition

Separate students and turn the stop into a balanced pause.

## 5–10 minutes: Balanced pause individually

### Purpose

Teach the physical quality of a pause before partner communication.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate walking and pausing on one weighted foot.
- Show the difference between a balanced pause and freezing between both feet.
- Hold the pause for one or two pulses.
- Restart without hopping.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from a second angle.
- Observe students who touch the free foot for support.
- Reinforce soft knees and upright posture.

### Student task

- Walk or mark the pulse.
- Pause on one weighted foot.
- Identify the free foot.
- Hold for one or two pulses.
- Restart without an extra transfer.

### Success signal

Most students pause and restart without losing balance or changing the weighted foot.

### Common errors and corrections

- Weight between both feet: “Choose one standing foot.”
- Free foot becomes support: “Let the free foot remain available.”
- Restart happens immediately: “Finish the pause before moving.”

### Transition

Use the available free foot to introduce a tap.

## 10–16 minutes: Tap without weight transfer

### Purpose

Make the difference between a step and a tap unmistakable.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a weighted step and a non-weighted tap side by side.
- Show that the same foot remains free after the tap.
- Add a simple tap-and-return action.
- Use brief balance checks.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower foot action.
- Observe students who transfer onto the tapping foot.
- Reinforce small range and relaxed legs.

### Student task

- Pause on one foot.
- Tap the free foot lightly.
- Lift it again to confirm no weight transferred.
- Return the free foot without changing the standing leg.
- Repeat on both sides.

### Success signal

Most students tap and lift the same foot again without an adjustment transfer.

### Common errors and corrections

- Tap receives weight: “Touch the floor; keep the body on the standing foot.”
- Tap becomes large: “Make the action smaller than a step.”
- Upper body collapses: “Keep the standing side organised.”

### Transition

Combine pause, tap, and restart individually.

## 16–22 minutes: Pause, tap, and restart individually

### Purpose

Build the full timing action before partner connection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate walk, pause, tap, and restart.
- Vary the pause between one and two pulses.
- Show that the restart begins from the standing foot, not the tap.
- Keep the music clear and slow.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate from front and side.
- Observe students who restart from the wrong foot.
- Reinforce complete balance through the delay.

### Student task

- Walk or dance Basic 3.
- Pause on one foot.
- Add one tap.
- Restart on the prepared direction.
- Repeat without counting a fixed sequence.

### Success signal

Students complete the action and restart on the correct foot without rushing.

### Common errors and corrections

- Restart from the tapping foot: “The tap stays free; restart from the standing leg.”
- Students count instead of listening: “Let the pause length change.”
- Balance shifts during the tap: “Keep the body over the standing foot.”

### Transition

Form partners and communicate a simple pause without a tap.

## 22–28 minutes: Partner pause and restart drill

### Purpose

Teach shared stillness and clear restart preparation.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3 into a balanced pause.
- Show how the leader stops through body organisation rather than arm tension.
- Prepare the restart before moving.
- Contrast this with holding the follower in place.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Explain the follower experience of completing the current step and waiting.
- Demonstrate maintaining personal balance.
- Show the error of restarting automatically after one count.

### Student task

Leaders:

- Lead Basic 3.
- Complete a clear pause.
- Hold for one or two pulses.
- Prepare the restart.

Followers:

- Complete the current transfer.
- Remain balanced.
- Wait for the restart direction.
- Avoid counting a fixed pause length.

### Success signal

Couples pause and restart together without gripping, leaning, or verbal cues.

### Common errors and corrections

- Leader holds through the arms: “Organise the body; keep the hands available.”
- Follower restarts automatically: “Wait for the new movement information.”
- Couple loses the pulse: “Keep listening even while still.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate once after the drill.

### Transition

Keep the new partner and add the pause inside Basic 3.

## 28–35 minutes: Basic 3 with pause

### Purpose

Integrate the timing choice into familiar movement.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3 with one planned pause.
- Show different safe moments for the pause without creating a fixed routine.
- Repeat with music and reduce counting.
- Observe balance and clarity.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following without knowing the pause location.
- Reinforce completing the current transfer.
- Observe anticipation and unstable stops.

### Student task

- Dance Basic 3.
- Add one pause after a completed transfer.
- Hold for one or two pulses.
- Restart clearly.
- Return to Marca when timing is lost.

### Success signal

Most couples include one pause in Basic 3 without losing balance or the shared pulse.

### Common errors and corrections

- Pause interrupts a transfer: “Finish the step before stopping.”
- Restart is abrupt: “Prepare before moving.”
- Follower guesses the pause: “Complete what is happening now.”

### Transition

Add the non-weighted tap during the pause.

## 35–41 minutes: Tap and go

### Purpose

Teach a simple decorative timing choice without changing the weighted foot.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate Basic 3, pause, tap, and restart.
- Keep the tap small and close to the floor.
- Show the error of transferring weight onto the tap.
- Observe leaders who force the follower's leg action.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate the follower tap action.
- Explain that the tap follows available balance and direction.
- Observe weight transfer and forced range.

### Student task

- Enter a balanced pause.
- Add one small tap.
- Keep the standing foot weighted.
- Restart only after preparation.
- Use Marca recovery when unclear.

### Success signal

Couples complete a tap-and-go action without changing the standing foot or forcing the partner.

### Common errors and corrections

- Tap carries weight: “Touch and remain on the standing foot.”
- Leader moves the follower's leg through the arms: “Create the pause and direction; do not place the foot.”
- Tap becomes styling competition: “Keep the action functional and small.”

### Transition

Use the pause to delay an exit from a familiar movement.

## 41–47 minutes: Delayed exit

### Purpose

Teach students to hold an exit decision without losing connection.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate a familiar movement ending in a balanced pause.
- Delay the exit for one or two pulses.
- Prepare the new direction clearly.
- Continue walking or return to Marca.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate waiting without predicting the exit.
- Reinforce personal balance and free-leg availability.
- Observe followers who add extra body movement.

### Student task

- Complete Basic 3, Estrela, or a compact redirection.
- Pause before the exit.
- Wait for one or two pulses.
- Continue walking or return to Marca.

### Success signal

Couples delay the exit and resume without pulling, guessing, or losing timing.

### Common errors and corrections

- Exit is not prepared: “Show the new direction before restarting.”
- Follower predicts: “Stay balanced and available.”
- Added movement disrupts balance: “Keep the pause quiet before adding texture.”

### Partner rotation

Rotate at the end of the section.

### Transition

With the new partner, practise two timing choices within one movement family.

## 47–53 minutes: Two timings and optional texture

### Purpose

Move from a fixed pause exercise into controlled musical choice.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Ask leaders to dance one movement family normally and with a pause.
- Introduce slower timing as a progression.
- Offer quick timing only to students with clear weight control.
- Present a small Tarraxinha texture as optional and not required.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Demonstrate following timing changes without guessing.
- Reinforce balance before any body texture.
- Show that hip movement is not forced or mechanically led.
- Observe consent and comfort.

### Student task

- Choose one movement family.
- Dance it with normal timing.
- Repeat with one pause or delayed transfer.
- Add a tap only when balance remains clear.
- Keep any texture optional and small.

### Success signal

Couples use two timing choices while preserving connection, balance, and comfort.

### Safety and consent cue

Tarraxinha-inspired texture is optional. No partner should force hip movement or body isolation.

### Transition

Rotate and explain the independent social-dance task.

## 53–58 minutes: Guided independent social dance

### Purpose

Integrate timing play into Level 1 vocabulary.

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Start a clear track with audible phrasing and space.
- Limit prompts to finish the transfer, balance first, tap without weight, prepare the restart, and recover through Marca.
- Gradually stop speaking.
- Intervene only for forced movement, unstable pauses, or repeated prediction.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Observe weight clarity, anticipation, and comfort.
- Reinforce remaining balanced during delays.
- Join temporarily if roles are uneven.

### Student task

Dance one movement family using two timings.

Central rule:

> Use pauses only when both partners remain balanced. A tap does not take weight, and the restart must be prepared.

### Success signal

Most couples use one pause or tap-and-go action and continue dancing without losing the pulse.

## 58–60 minutes: Recap and Week 8 connection

### Leader-role teacher actions

- Recap balanced pauses, taps, delayed exits, and clear restarts.
- Explain that Week 8 integrates the full Level 1 vocabulary into complete social-dance choices.

### Follower-role teacher actions

- Reinforce waiting, balance, and comfort.
- Ask students which timing choice felt clearest.

### Student task

- Explain the difference between a tap and a weighted step.
- Identify Marca as the recovery option.
- Ask final questions.

### Success signal

Students understand that musical play depends on balance and communication, not added complexity.

# Demonstration plan

For pause and tap:

1. Show the complete action with music.
2. Repeat without music.
3. Show the weighted foot and free foot clearly.
4. Demonstrate a tap that incorrectly receives weight.
5. Show the corrected non-weighted tap.
6. Demonstrate restart preparation.
7. Return to music.

For delayed exits:

1. Show the normal exit.
2. Show the delayed exit.
3. Demonstrate the follower waiting without prediction.
4. Show recovery into Marca.

# Partner-rotation plan

Recommended rotations:

1. After partner pause-and-restart drill
2. After delayed exits
3. Before independent social dance

Use one consistent direction. Teaching-role partners join temporarily when roles are uneven. Students may switch roles or decline a rotation.

# Differentiation

## Core version

- Basic 3 with one balanced pause
- Clear restart
- Marca recovery

## Progressing version

- Tap and go
- One- or two-pulse delay
- Delayed exit into walking
- Use the timing choice with a new partner

## Challenge version

- Slow timing
- Quick timing when weight remains clear
- Delayed exit into Vírgula
- Small optional Tarraxinha texture without forced hip movement

# Common errors and correction cues

| Error | Correction cue |
|---|---|
| Tapping with weight | “Touch the floor; keep the body on the standing foot.” |
| Pausing between both feet | “Choose one standing foot.” |
| Restarting without preparation | “Show the direction before moving.” |
| Follower predicts pause length | “Wait for the restart information.” |
| Leader holds through the arms | “Organise the body; keep the hands available.” |
| Body movement begins before balance | “Balance first, texture second.” |
| Timing play becomes rushed | “Use fewer choices and keep the pulse.” |

# Safety and consent

- Keep Tarraxinha content optional and small.
- Never force hip movement or body isolation.
- Both partners maintain personal balance during pauses.
- Do not hold the partner still through arm tension.
- Open hold remains valid.
- Students may decline a partner, role, texture, or physical correction.
- Use quick timing only when weight transfer remains controlled.

# Music plan

| Section | Music quality | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Week 6 recap | Clear steady pulse | Low |
| Pause and tap drills | Spacious phrasing | Low |
| Partner timing practice | Clear beat with audible breaks | Low to medium |
| Delayed exits | Predictable musical structure | Medium |
| Independent practice | Social Kizomba with clear phrasing and texture | Medium |

# Teaching-role responsibilities

## Leader-role teacher

- Explain pause preparation, tap weight, and restart clarity.
- Demonstrate timing choices from the leader role.
- Observe arm tension, balance, delay length, and recovery.
- Manage class timing and rotations.

## Follower-role teacher

- Demonstrate weighted and free-foot responsibilities.
- Explain waiting, balance, and optional texture.
- Observe anticipation, unstable taps, and comfort.
- Provide a second viewing angle.

The roles may be exchanged without changing the plan.

# Success measures

## During the class

- Most students pause on one clearly weighted foot.
- Students distinguish taps from weighted steps.
- Couples restart after clear preparation.
- Followers avoid predicting pause length.
- Leaders avoid holding through the arms.
- Optional texture remains small and consensual.
- Most couples use two timing choices without losing the pulse.
